Sales & Service Cloud — 100 Scenarios

Forecasting, pricing and revenue structures, territories, Omni-Channel routing, entitlements and the SLA clock, Knowledge, and the metrics the business will judge you on.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Sales & Service Cloud and its Advanced section.

Pipeline & forecasting (1–20)

1 The forecast does not match the pipeline report.Hard

Forecast categories. Stages map to categories (Pipeline, Best Case, Commit, Closed), and the forecast sums categories while your report probably sums stages or all open opportunities.

The stage-to-category mapping is the forecast design — stage names are just labels on top of it.

2 A manager's forecast roll-up is wrong.Medium

Forecasts roll up the forecast hierarchy, which is derived from roles — so a broken or out-of-date role hierarchy produces a broken forecast.

Check the role hierarchy before investigating the opportunities.

3 A manager wants to override their team's number.Easy

Adjustments — the manager overrides the roll-up without touching the underlying opportunities, and both numbers are retained.

That is the auditable "my rep is optimistic" mechanism, and it is what stops managers editing their team's records.

4 You need forecasts by product family and by revenue.Medium

Multiple forecast types can run simultaneously — revenue, quantity, by product family, by split, by territory.

That is how sales and finance get different views of the same pipeline without arguing about which one is correct.

5 Quotas need loading for 400 users.Easy

Via the API or a data load — the UI is not viable at that scale, and quotas are per user per period.

Plan the load as a recurring process, since quotas change every period.

6 Amount became read-only on opportunities.Easy

You added Opportunity Products, so Amount is now a roll-up of line items.

That is expected and usually desirable — but it changes how reps work, so it needs communicating before you enable products.

7 Revenue must be recognised monthly over a year.Medium

Product Schedules — revenue or quantity spread across periods, per line item.

They get large quickly (a line item times twelve months times thousands of opportunities), so treat them as a volume consideration.

8 Two reps share credit for a deal.Medium

Opportunity Splits — revenue splits must total 100%; overlay splits can total anything.

Enabling splits on a large org is a long-running background process, so plan it as a change rather than a checkbox.

9 A product is not selectable on an opportunity.Easy

No Price Book Entry for that product in the selected price book and currency — the usual cause by a wide margin.

Check the price book on the opportunity, then the entry, then whether the product is active.

10 Different channels need different prices.Medium

Multiple price books, with the right one selected per opportunity.

Every product needs an entry per book and per currency, which is the maintenance cost people underestimate.

11 When do you outgrow standard products and schedules?Medium

When you need configurable bundles with options and rules, discount schedules, subscription pricing, amendments mid-term and renewals.

That is CPQ. Standard pricing covers simple catalogue selling and then stops abruptly.

12 Opportunity stages do not match the real sales process.Medium

Fix the sales process, not the reporting — stages that nobody follows produce a pipeline nobody trusts.

And remember each stage maps to a forecast category, so changing stages changes the forecast.

13 Reps leave close dates in the past.Easy

A validation rule on save, plus a data-quality report grouped by owner so it is visible and actionable.

Past close dates silently corrupt the forecast, which is why this small rule earns its place.

14 Territory management or role hierarchy?Hard

Role hierarchy when access follows management lines and ownership is stable. Enterprise Territory Management when access follows territory assignment that changes independently — and when you need territory-based forecasting.

Territories are a second hierarchy to maintain, with their own assignment rules and recalculation. Powerful and genuinely heavy.

15 Pipeline velocity is requested.Medium

(Opportunities × win rate × average deal size) ÷ sales cycle length — more actionable than any single stage conversion number.

Define each component explicitly, because "average deal size" and "sales cycle" both have several plausible definitions.

16 Win rate differs between two reports.Medium

Denominator: all opportunities, closed opportunities, or opportunities created in a period? Each gives a different number.

And beware averaging per-rep win rates, which weights a rep with three deals equally with one who had three hundred.

17 Forecast accuracy is poor.Hard

Usually the stage-to-category mapping does not reflect reality — "Commit" includes deals reps are not actually committing to.

Measure committed versus actual by period and by manager; that number tells you whether the mapping is real or aspirational.

18 Leads are not converting cleanly.Medium

Custom lead fields need explicit field mapping to Account, Contact and Opportunity, with compatible types.

Unmapped fields are silently lost at conversion and discovered months later when someone asks where the data went.

19 Duplicate leads block conversion.Medium

Prefer alert over block, with the matched Account and Contact offered so the rep converts into the existing records.

Blocking produces workarounds — slightly altered names — which is worse than a visible duplicate with a merge path.

20 Campaign influence shows nothing.Medium

It requires campaign members, an influence time window, and an agreed attribution model — all three.

Without them every marketing-ROI report becomes a debate about the numbers. Set the window and model in writing first.

Routing & case management (21–42)

21 Agents are overwhelmed while others are idle.Hard

Omni-Channel capacity weights. If every work item costs 1, an agent with three voice calls looks the same as one with three chats.

Weight a call at 5 and a chat at 1 against a capacity of 5, and the engine balances correctly. Getting weights right fixes most of these complaints.

22 Queue-based or skills-based routing?Medium

Queue-based is simple and predictable: items go to a queue, assigned agents receive them. Skills-based matches required skills on the item to agents who hold them.

Skills-based also supports timed skill dropping, which relaxes requirements if nothing matches — essential to stop items starving.

23 High-priority items are overtaking everything.Medium

Routing configuration priority, with secondary routing priority as the tie-break — usually oldest-first.

Without a tie-break, old low-priority items are never worked, which surfaces as complaints about ignored cases.

24 Least Active or Most Available?Medium

Least Active balances workload across agents; Most Available fills the quietest agent first.

Least Active is the usual default for fairness; Most Available suits specialist pools where concentration is acceptable.

25 An item sat unrouted for an hour.Medium

No agent matched its skill requirements, or nobody with capacity was available in the right presence status.

Timed skill dropping and a fallback queue both prevent starvation. Monitor the oldest unrouted item as a metric.

26 Cases are assigned to the wrong team.Medium

Assignment rule entries are evaluated in order and the first match wins — a broad entry near the top swallows records intended for a specific entry below it.

Reorder most-specific first, and keep a catch-all last so nothing silently stays with the default owner.

27 Escalation fires at the wrong time.Medium

Business Hours — the wrong set applied, holidays missing, or a timezone mismatch between the business hours and the case.

Missing holidays is the standard cause of "it escalated while we were closed".

28 SLA metrics measure the customer's response time, not yours.Hard

You are not stopping the clock. The case's IsStopped flag pauses the SLA while you wait on the customer.

Without it, every slow customer reply counts against you — and the metric becomes meaningless.

29 How do milestones work?Medium

An Entitlement Process defines them; the Business Hours on the entitlement or case define what "four hours" means; time triggers fire warning actions before and violation actions after.

Configure both warning and violation — a violation you only learn about afterwards is a report, not a process.

30 Changing the entitlement process broke open cases.Medium

Milestones are per case per process, and changing the process does not retrofit history — open cases can end up in an inconsistent state.

Plan it as a data exercise: complete or migrate in-flight cases deliberately.

31 Email-to-Case creates new cases instead of threading.Medium

Thread identification failing — the token missing from outbound templates, or a mechanism the customer's client strips.

Test with the mail clients your customers actually use. Forked reply chains wreck both metrics and customer experience.

32 Attachments are lost on inbound email.Medium

Size limits, and differences between standard and On-Demand Email-to-Case handling.

Test with a large attachment before go-live — discovering the limit from a customer is the expensive way to learn it.

33 You hit the daily Email-to-Case limit.Medium

Find what is consuming it: auto-reply loops, spam, or a mailbox forwarding notifications as well as customer email.

Filter before forwarding, suppress auto-reply loops, and alert on approaching the limit.

34 Agents cannot find Knowledge articles.Medium

Search is the whole product — articles nobody can find have no value.

Invest in titles, keywords and synonyms, and review the search terms that returned nothing. That report is your content backlog.

35 Customers see internal-only articles.Hard

Data category group visibility — that is the mechanism controlling which articles which audiences see, including guest users.

Test as an authenticated customer and a guest; this is a realistic exposure path.

36 Knowledge is never updated.Medium

Build attach-and-close into the console workflow: agents attaching the article that solved the case is what makes the knowledge base improve itself.

If it is a nice-to-have rather than part of the flow, it will not happen.

37 Deflection is claimed but not measured.Medium

Then it is not deflection. Track self-service sessions resolved without creating a case, against cases created on the same topic.

And define "resolved" before reporting it, or the number is unfalsifiable.

38 A case needs multiple people working it.Medium

Case teams for ongoing collaboration, or swarming patterns where specialists join temporarily.

Decide how ownership and SLA responsibility work when several people are involved — that is the part usually left undefined.

39 Agents work in six browser tabs.Medium

A console app with sub-tabs, a utility bar and the Workspace API for programmatic tab control.

But watch performance: console tabs stay alive, so leaked listeners and timers accumulate across a shift.

40 CTI integration is required.Medium

Open CTI for a browser-based softphone in the utility bar, or Service Cloud Voice for a deeper telephony integration.

Either way, routing capacity must account for voice alongside digital channels, or agents get overloaded.

41 Macros and quick text are underused.Easy

Usually a discoverability and curation problem — too many, poorly named, or not available where agents work.

Curate a small, well-named set and surface them in the console; they are among the cheapest handle-time reductions.

42 Omni-Channel routing needs external logic.Hard

External Routing via API lets a third-party engine make the assignment decision while Salesforce handles presence and capacity.

Justified when routing logic genuinely exceeds what the platform expresses — and it adds an availability dependency.

Entitlements, pricing & contracts (43–62)

43 Two clocks disagree about an SLA.Hard

The SLA clock runs on the entitlement's Business Hours; other processes may run on different hours or timezones.

Reconcile them deliberately. A contract promising four working hours against business hours nobody checked produces breaches that every rule "passed".

44 Entitlement is not found on a case.Medium

No active entitlement on the contact, account or asset — or it has expired, or the case was created before the entitlement started.

Automate entitlement assignment on case creation rather than relying on agents to pick one.

45 Service contracts versus entitlements.Medium

Entitlements define the support level and milestones; service contracts (with contract line items) represent the commercial agreement.

You can use entitlements alone for simple support tiers; contracts matter when you are tracking what was sold and for how long.

46 A customer disputes an invoice total.Medium

Work the pricing chain: list price from the price book entry, then discounts, then tax, then the roll-up to the parent record.

And confirm which currency and which rate applied, since multi-currency conversion is a frequent cause.

47 How do order and opportunity relate?Medium

Opportunity is the pursuit; Order (with order products) is what was actually committed, often generated from the quote or opportunity.

Many orgs skip Orders and integrate to an ERP instead — which is legitimate, but then the ERP owns fulfilment truth.

48 Quotes and opportunities are out of sync.Medium

Standard quotes sync to the opportunity only when you designate a synced quote — otherwise they drift deliberately.

Decide whether the opportunity or the quote is authoritative, and make the sync behaviour match that decision.

49 Pricing needs approval above a discount threshold.Medium

An approval process on the quote or opportunity, with a dynamic approver rather than named individuals.

Remember submitted records lock, so sequence any automation around that — see approvals.

50 Multi-currency totals look wrong.Hard

Static conversion rates mis-state history; Advanced Currency Management applies dated rates to opportunities but changes roll-up and report behaviour.

Both are effectively irreversible decisions, so settle them with finance before enabling anything.

51 Products need different prices per customer.Medium

Price books per segment for broad differences; negotiated per-customer pricing is a CPQ contracted-price concern.

Hand-maintaining per-customer prices in standard price books does not scale past a handful of accounts.

52 A renewal needs generating from a contract.Medium

Standard objects do not do this well — renewals and amendments are CPQ territory, driven from subscriptions.

Building renewal logic by hand on standard objects is a common and regretted decision.

53 Assets are not tracking what customers own.Medium

Assets need creating from orders or fulfilment, with a hierarchy if equipment contains components.

They are the foundation for warranty, maintenance and field service — and they are frequently left empty, which blocks all three.

54 Warranty coverage must drive billing.Medium

Warranty terms define what is covered and the customer's share of parts, labour and travel — which then flows into work order pricing.

See warranties for how this connects to maintenance.

55 Time tracking is needed for billing.Medium

Time Sheets and Time Sheet Entries capture work, travel and breaks per resource per period — the feed to payroll and to billable time.

Entry types matter: billing "travel" as "work" is a conversation with a customer you do not want to have.

56 Work order totals are read-only.Easy

They roll up from work order line items — subtotal, discount, tax, grand total.

So pricing happens at line level, and attempting to set the header total directly is the wrong model.

57 Billing integration is required.Medium

Usually an integration: completed work with consumed products and labour becomes an invoice in ERP or Revenue Cloud.

Define which system owns the invoice and the tax calculation — that decision shapes the whole interface.

58 Person Accounts are proposed.Hard

Appropriate for B2C where the customer is an individual — but enabling it is irreversible and it changes duplicate management, sharing and integration behaviour.

Decide it deliberately, and test the implications in a throwaway org first.

59 B2B and B2C in one org.Hard

Possible with Person Accounts plus record types, and genuinely more complex: different processes, different duplicate rules, different reporting.

Model it explicitly rather than letting both grow on the same objects by accident.

60 Contacts relate to multiple accounts.Medium

Contacts to Multiple Accounts handles it, with a primary account plus related ones and roles.

Report carefully: the relationship object means a contact can appear under several accounts, which changes counts.

61 Account hierarchy reporting is needed.Medium

Parent/child accounts give you the structure, but roll-up reporting across a hierarchy is weak in standard reports.

Maintain hierarchy roll-up fields via automation, or use CRM Analytics where hierarchy flattening is straightforward.

62 Which object owns the customer's "status"?Medium

Decide and document it — Account, a related object, or an external system. Multiple competing status fields is a common and corrosive problem.

Then make every other surface read it rather than maintaining its own.

Metrics (63–82)

63 How do you define First Contact Resolution honestly?Hard

Cases closed with one customer interaction and no reopen within n days.

Without the reopen window, closing cases prematurely improves the metric — which is exactly the behaviour you did not want to reward.

64 AHT is falling and reopens are rising.Hard

A problem being hidden, not solved — agents are closing faster and not resolving.

Always pair a throughput metric with a quality metric. Measuring AHT alone reliably produces this outcome.

65 How should AHT be calculated?Medium

Agent time on the case, excluding stopped-clock periods when you are waiting on the customer.

Including customer-wait time measures your customers' responsiveness, which nobody can manage.

66 SLA attainment is reported as 100% and customers complain.Hard

The milestone definition does not reflect the customer's experience — perhaps it measures first response while customers care about resolution.

Measure what the contract promises and what customers feel, and be explicit about the difference.

67 CSAT is high but churn is rising.Medium

Survey response bias — the satisfied respond, and the dissatisfied leave without answering.

Look at response rates, and pair CSAT with behavioural signals (reopens, escalations, contact frequency).

68 Agents are gaming a metric.Medium

Every metric gets gamed in the direction you point it — case volume closed produces split cases, AHT produces premature closes, CSAT produces avoidance of hard cases.

The fix is paired metrics reviewed together, not better enforcement.

69 Which service metrics actually matter?Medium

Resolution quality (FCR with a reopen window, reopen rate), timeliness against the promise (SLA attainment on business hours), effort (AHT, transfers), and outcome (CSAT).

Four numbers reviewed together beats twenty on a dashboard.

70 Which sales metrics actually matter?Medium

Pipeline coverage, win rate (correctly computed), average deal size, sales cycle length, and forecast accuracy.

Forecast accuracy is the one that tells you whether the rest of your pipeline data is real or theatre.

71 A metric has no agreed definition.Medium

Then reports will disagree and meetings will be about the number. Write the definition down: numerator, denominator, date basis, exclusions.

Put it in the report description so it travels with the metric.

72 The business wants a single health score.Medium

Composite scores hide the inputs and are hard to act on. Be clear about the weighting and keep the components visible.

A score that drops without telling anyone why generates investigation rather than action.

73 How do you baseline before a change?Medium

Capture the metrics for a comparable period before you change anything, and keep the definitions fixed.

Without a baseline you cannot distinguish improvement from seasonality, and every claim becomes contestable.

74 Reports show different case volumes.Medium

Date basis (created versus closed), record type scope, or queue-owned cases excluded by an ownership filter.

Compare the filters side by side; those three account for most case-volume discrepancies.

75 Averages are hiding the problem.Medium

Show the distribution or the median — an average handle time containing one 40-day case tells nobody anything.

Percentiles are usually the honest statistic for durations.

76 A manager wants per-agent rankings.Medium

Be careful: case mix varies, so ranking on throughput penalises whoever takes the hard cases.

Normalise for complexity where you can, and always show a quality metric alongside.

77 How do you measure deflection credibly?Medium

Self-service sessions that did not result in a case, on the same topic, over the same period — with "resolved" defined in advance.

Comparing total case volume before and after conflates deflection with demand changes.

78 Nobody acts on the dashboard.Medium

Each number needs an owner and a decision it informs. Numbers without either are decoration.

Ask which three decisions the dashboard supports and rebuild around those.

79 Metrics look good and customers are unhappy.Hard

You are measuring process compliance rather than outcomes — SLA met, case closed, survey not returned.

Add outcome measures (reopens, repeat contacts, churn) and listen to the qualitative signal. Good metrics with unhappy customers means the metrics are wrong.

80 How often should metrics be reviewed?Easy

Operationally weekly, definitions quarterly — because processes change and definitions quietly stop matching them.

A metric nobody has revisited in two years is probably measuring a process that no longer exists.

81 Leadership wants a new metric every month.Medium

Push back gently: each metric is reporting, definition and maintenance cost, and attention is finite.

Ask what decision the new one informs, and whether an existing one already answers it.

82 What single metric would you add to most service orgs?Hard

Reopen rate, paired with whatever throughput metric they already watch.

It is cheap to compute, hard to game, and it exposes the premature-closure behaviour that most other service metrics actively encourage.

Design judgement (83–100)

83 The business wants a custom object for cases.Medium

Almost always wrong: you lose entitlements, milestones, Omni-Channel routing, Email-to-Case, Knowledge integration and the whole service feature set.

Establish what Case cannot do. Usually it is a field or a process question rather than an object one.

84 Opportunity does not fit the sales motion.Medium

Try record types, sales processes and page layouts first — the standard object flexes further than people expect.

A custom object means losing forecasting, products, quotes and every report type built on Opportunity.

85 Which licence do service agents need?Medium

Service Cloud, plus permission set licences for console, Omni-Channel and any add-ons.

Check object availability before designing: discovering a licence restriction after the build is an expensive week.

86 Sales and service want conflicting Account layouts.Medium

Dynamic Forms with visibility by profile, or separate page assignments per app — one page definition where possible.

Two maintained layouts drift apart, and the drift is invisible until someone complains.

87 Where should the customer's single view live?Hard

If sales and service share customers, one org — a fragmented customer view is the hardest thing to retrofit.

If they genuinely do not, separate orgs plus integration is defensible. That question decides the architecture more than any other.

88 A process spans sales and service.Medium

Model the handoff explicitly — which object carries it, who owns it at each stage, and what the SLA is across the boundary.

Unowned handoffs are where customer experience actually fails.

89 Einstein features are proposed for service.Medium

Case classification and article recommendation are genuinely useful if the data supports them — enough closed cases, clean categorisation, maintained Knowledge.

On poor data they produce confident wrong suggestions that agents learn to ignore.

90 Agents complain the console is slow.Medium

Measure EPT and component cost — usually too many components, several Apex calls on load, or an unselective related list.

Console tabs also persist, so leaks accumulate. See page performance.

91 How do you reduce handle time legitimately?Medium

Fewer clicks (macros, quick text, guided flows), better information on screen, and better Knowledge so agents are not searching.

Not by pressuring the number, which produces premature closes and reopens.

92 A customer wants a portal.Medium

Experience Cloud with case submission, case tracking and Knowledge — and the licence choice is driven by volume and whether they need CRM objects.

Guest and external sharing design is the risky part. See Experience Cloud.

93 Field service is being considered.Medium

Justified when you send people to places and travel is a real cost. If nobody drives anywhere and there are no parts, it is expensive machinery you will not use.

See Scheduler versus Field Service.

94 The sales team will not update the CRM.Medium

Usually because it costs them more than it returns — too many required fields, no benefit visible to them.

Reduce the ask, surface something useful back (next best action, account context), and measure whether adoption follows.

95 How do you validate a service design before building?Medium

Walk three real cases through it with the agents who will work them, end to end, including the awkward ones.

Designs fail on the exceptions — transfers, reopens, multi-party cases — and those only surface when you walk real examples.

96 The business asks for every field to be required.Medium

Required fields slow every save and push users to enter anything to get past them — which produces worse data, not better.

Require what is genuinely needed at that stage, and use data-quality reporting for the rest.

97 Two teams define "customer" differently.Hard

That is the real problem, and it is organisational. Resolve the definition before modelling it.

Building two parallel representations to avoid the conversation gives you permanent reconciliation work and the conversation anyway.

98 How do you prioritise a long service backlog?Medium

By frequency × population × pain: a small improvement on something 200 agents do fifty times a day beats a large one on a monthly task.

And prefer items that remove work over items that add features.

99 What is the most common Sales/Service design mistake?Hard

Replacing standard objects with custom ones and losing the feature set — followed by stage-to-forecast-category mappings nobody agreed, and SLA clocks that never stop.

All three are hard to unwind once data exists.

100 One habit that most improves these implementations?Hard

Agreeing the metric definitions before building the process — because the metrics reveal what the business actually means.

Defining FCR with a reopen window, or forecast categories against real commitment, forces the process conversation that configuration otherwise papers over.
