Experience Cloud — 100 Scenarios

External sharing mechanics, guest-user hardening, licence maths, LWR versus Aura, performance and SEO, self-registration, and the exposure paths every security review looks for.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Experience Cloud and its Advanced section.

External sharing (1–22)

1 100,000 customers need to see their own cases.Medium

High-volume Customer Community licences — which have no role hierarchy, so access comes from a Sharing Set matching the user's Contact or Account to a field on the Case.

That single fact drives most community sharing design.

2 Internal staff cannot see records a community user owns.Hard

Records owned by high-volume community users are invisible to everyone else by default — you need a Share Group.

It is the mechanism people most often do not know exists, and it presents as a mysterious reporting gap.

3 Should External OWD be enabled?Easy

Yes. It gives external users a separate OWD so you can lock their access down without constraining internal users.

A single OWD for both populations is how community data leaks — an internal convenience becomes an external grant.

4 How does a Sharing Set actually work?Medium

It grants access based on a match between a field on the user's Contact or Account and a field on the record — "see Cases where AccountId equals my Account".

Which means the match field must be reliably populated, or access silently fails for some users.

5 A customer can see another customer's record.Hard

Contain first — tighten the object permission or disable the page while you diagnose.

Then find the grant: an over-broad Sharing Set match field, a sharing rule including external users, elevated Apex, a system-context flow, or External OWD left open.

6 Partners need to see their resellers' opportunities.Hard

Partner Community licences with partner roles under each account, Super User access for partner managers, and External Account Hierarchy for the distributor-to-reseller tier.

Verify the licence includes Opportunity — Customer Community does not.

7 What is Super User access for?Medium

A partner or customer user who can see their whole account's records — delegated, per-account visibility.

Available on Partner and Customer Community Plus, and it is how you avoid building custom sharing for "the manager sees everything at their company".

8 How many partner roles per account?Easy

Up to three, which is a hard constraint on how much hierarchy you can express inside a partner organisation.

Deeper structures need External Account Hierarchy or a different approach.

9 You designed on the assumption roles exist.Hard

If the licence is high-volume, there are no roles — so hierarchy-based access and sharing rules to groups of them do not apply.

Discovering that after design is a redesign, which is why the licence decision precedes the sharing design.

10 Community users cannot see Knowledge articles.Medium

Data category group visibility — that is the mechanism controlling which articles which audiences see.

Check it for authenticated external users and guests separately; they are configured independently.

11 A contractor should see only their own appointments.Medium

Sharing Sets matching their Contact to the Service Resource and Assigned Resource records.

Test with a real login including the mobile app — under-sharing shows up as blank screens on the device, which looks like a mobile bug.

12 Sharing rules do not seem to apply to community users.Medium

High-volume licences do not participate in ordinary sharing rules. Customer Community Plus and Partner licences do.

So the mechanism available to you depends entirely on the licence — check that before debugging the rule.

13 An external user needs access to one specific record.Medium

Apex managed sharing with a custom RowCause, or a field the Sharing Set matches on.

Manual shares are removed on ownership change, so they are a poor fit for anything intended to persist.

14 Reporting is needed for external users.Medium

Customer Community Plus and Partner licences include reports; high-volume Customer Community does not.

Which is frequently the deciding factor between the two, and it costs real money per user.

15 An external user's record ownership is causing skew.Hard

If community users create many records, their ownership distribution matters — and a default owner receiving everything produces ownership skew.

Spread ownership, and keep any skewed owner out of the role hierarchy.

16 How do you test external sharing?Medium

Log in as the actual external persona — authenticated customer, partner, partner manager, guest — and attempt to reach what you did not intend.

Reasoning from configuration misses the combination effects; logging in finds them.

17 An auditor asks what external users can see.Hard

Produce the External OWD, the Sharing Sets and their match fields, the Share Groups, the profile permissions per licence type, and the persona test evidence.

That evidence only exists if you created the test matrix during build, which is an argument for doing so.

18 Community users see too many fields.Medium

Field-level security on the community profile — which is the control that actually prevents exposure, not page layouts.

Review it field by field for every object they can reach.

19 A case comment is visible when it should not be.Medium

Case comments and feed items have their own visibility semantics for external users — internal-only flags matter.

Test with a real community login; this is a common and embarrassing leak.

20 Files shared to a case are visible externally.Medium

File sharing follows its own rules via ContentDocumentLink, and sharing a file to a record can expose it to anyone with record access.

Audit file visibility separately from record visibility — they are not the same model.

21 How do you handle a customer with multiple accounts?Hard

Contacts to Multiple Accounts, plus a Sharing Set or sharing logic that accounts for the relationship object rather than a single AccountId.

Which the default Sharing Set match does not do, so this needs explicit design.

22 What is the commonest external sharing mistake?Hard

Designing on the assumption that roles and sharing rules are available, when the licence is high-volume.

Followed by leaving External OWD at the internal setting, and by forgetting the Share Group so internal staff cannot see community-owned records.

Guest users (23–44)

23 What can an unauthenticated visitor reach?Hard

Five surfaces: the guest profile's object and field permissions, guest sharing rules, every @AuraEnabled method reachable from a public page, every flow running in system context on that page, and the self-registration handler.

Those five are where every serious Experience Cloud incident has originated.

24 Guest users cannot be given access via sharing rules.Medium

Correct — they need explicitly scoped guest sharing rules, and "Secure guest user record access" should stay enabled.

Ordinary sharing rules do not target them, which is deliberate and protective.

25 Who owns records a guest creates?Medium

Guest users cannot own records, so creations are reassigned — usually to a default owner you must configure deliberately.

At volume that owner develops ownership skew, which is a second reason to configure it thoughtfully.

26 An @AuraEnabled method is on a public page.Hard

It is a public endpoint callable by anyone. Audit it for CRUD and FLS enforcement, parameter validation, row bounds, and whether anything in the path runs without sharing.

Elevated code reachable from a guest page is the classic Salesforce exposure path.

27 A flow on a public page runs in system context.Hard

Same risk class as without sharing Apex — and reviewed far less often, because flows do not look like code.

Run it in user context where possible, validate every input, and audit what it reads and writes.

28 Guests are creating spam records.Medium

A guest-writable object with no validation is a target. Add CAPTCHA and honeypots, rate-limit at the web layer, validate server side, and route submissions to a review queue.

Blocking the submission entirely loses legitimate enquiries, so route rather than reject.

29 Self-registration is granting too much.Hard

The handler is a public endpoint — never trust supplied profile, role or permission values. Assign them server side from fixed, least-privilege configuration.

Add CAPTCHA, rate limiting and email verification. This handler is among the most commonly exploited surfaces.

30 Each site has its own guest user.Medium

Yes — and its own profile. Hardening one site does not harden the others.

Audit every site separately, including ones created for a campaign and forgotten.

31 How do you verify guest hardening?Medium

Open the site in an incognito window and try to read what you did not intend — including by calling the Apex methods directly.

That step finds what the configuration review missed, which is the point of doing it.

32 A public page needs to show record data.Hard

Grant the minimum through guest sharing rules and FLS, and prefer CMS or Knowledge content over records where possible.

Exposing records to guests for SEO is how customer data ends up indexed.

33 Guest access to files is needed.Medium

Possible, and it needs care: file sharing has its own model, and a publicly linked file is accessible to anyone with the link.

Use asset files or CMS for genuinely public content rather than sharing record-attached files.

34 A guest-accessible endpoint needs rate limiting.Medium

At the web or CDN layer primarily — Apex is a poor place to implement it, since the request has already consumed resources.

Inside the org, bound payload sizes and reject oversized requests clearly.

35 A security researcher reports an exposure.Hard

Acknowledge, contain (disable the page or tighten the permission, accepting the functionality loss), verify the claim yourself as a guest, then fix forward with a test.

And check whether the same class of hole exists on your other sites.

36 How do you review guest Apex systematically?Hard

Enumerate every component on public pages, then every @AuraEnabled method they call, then whether each enforces CRUD/FLS, validates parameters, bounds rows and avoids elevation.

Grep for without sharing and cross-reference against that list.

37 A guest page exposes an object name parameter.Hard

That is a generic read endpoint for anonymous visitors. Allow-list the permitted objects and fields.

Accepting an object name or filter from a client is a data-exfiltration surface regardless of intent.

38 What should the guest profile look like?Medium

Minimal: read on exactly the objects the public pages need, with FLS reviewed field by field, no create or edit unless deliberately required, and no API-enabled permission unless necessary.

Default guest profiles are more permissive than people assume.

39 A form must create a case with attachments.Hard

Web-to-Case handles files poorly, so an Experience Cloud page or a custom endpoint is the robust option.

Treat it as a public endpoint: guest permissions reviewed, size limits enforced, spam controls, and nothing running without sharing.

40 Guest users are consuming API limits.Medium

Public pages making Apex calls consume org resources, and a scraped or attacked page can consume a great deal.

Rate-limit, cache aggressively (public content is identical for everyone), and monitor site traffic.

41 A campaign site was created and forgotten.Medium

Its guest user still exists with whatever permissions it was given, and nobody is reviewing it.

Maintain an inventory of sites with owners, and decommission deliberately — orphaned sites are a standing exposure risk.

42 Should guests be able to run reports?Easy

Essentially never — and the permission should not be on the guest profile.

If public aggregate data is genuinely needed, publish it as content rather than exposing a reporting capability.

43 How do you prevent SEO indexing of private content?Medium

Keep anything record-backed behind authentication, and configure the site's SEO settings deliberately for what should be public.

A search engine indexing customer data is the worst possible way to discover an access misconfiguration.

44 What is the single highest-value guest control?Hard

Field-level security on the guest profile, reviewed field by field — it is what actually prevents data exposure when a page or method is more permissive than intended.

Followed by auditing elevated code reachable from public pages.

Building the site (45–70)

45 LWR or Aura template for a new site?Medium

LWR: substantially faster, static content from CDN, much better SEO — the right choice for new builds, public sites and performance-sensitive portals.

Aura templates have a richer legacy component set, which is occasionally the deciding factor.

46 A component you need is unavailable in LWR.Medium

Check the current feature matrix — the gap narrows every release, and basing the decision on year-old knowledge is how a project discovers a missing component in week six.

Otherwise build it as an LWC, or reconsider the template.

47 Aura components will not work in LWR.Easy

Correct — LWR supports LWC only.

Which is frequently the real reason an existing site cannot simply be migrated, and it needs scoping.

48 A public site is slow for anonymous visitors.Medium

Enable the CDN — the largest single win for anonymous traffic, since public pages are identical for everyone.

Then component count, image weight and the number of Apex calls on load.

49 Pages are megabytes of images.Easy

Compress, resize and use modern formats. Community pages are frequently far heavier than anyone has measured.

Test on a throttled connection from the geography your users are in, not your office laptop.

50 Five components each call Apex on load.Medium

Five round trips. Consolidate into one call returning a composite payload.

On a community page, that matters more than internally because external users are often on worse connections.

51 Authenticated pages cannot be cached.Medium

Correct — caching applies to public content where the response is identical for every visitor.

So performance work on authenticated pages is about reducing work, not about caching.

52 You need different content per segment.Medium

Audience targeting — vary components, pages and branding by profile, location, record field or custom criteria.

It is the declarative way to serve several segments from one site rather than building several.

53 Content must be reused across sites and channels.Medium

Salesforce CMS, with workspaces and translations — so the content is managed once and consumed in several places.

Also the right home for genuinely public content, rather than exposing records.

54 The site needs to support three languages.Medium

Site translations, locale-aware formatting and right-to-left support where needed — planned at the start.

Retrofitting localisation usually means rebuilding pages, which is why it is a design decision rather than a phase.

55 User-generated content needs controlling.Medium

Moderation: content rules, rate limits on posting, member criteria and a flagged-content queue.

Essential for any site with discussions — and someone must actually work the queue.

56 Case deflection is a goal.Medium

Surface Knowledge search before the case form, and measure the deflection — sessions resolved without creating a case.

Deflection claimed without measurement is not deflection.

57 A branded mobile app is wanted for the community.Medium

Mobile Publisher wraps the site with your branding and app store presence.

Which also means the site's mobile responsiveness matters more — test at phone width with a real device.

58 SEO is a requirement.Medium

LWR plus CDN, with the site's SEO settings configured and public content as CMS or Knowledge rather than records.

Aura templates are poor for indexable public content, which is frequently the deciding factor.

59 Login must use the customer's existing identity.Medium

Social sign-on or an external identity provider, with Salesforce as the service provider — or Salesforce as the IdP if it owns the customer identity.

Decide which system owns identity before building the login experience.

60 Passwordless login is requested.Medium

Supported via email or SMS verification, which is genuinely better for consumer experience.

It needs reliable contact data and a thought-through recovery path for people who change their number.

61 A fully custom front end is proposed.Hard

Headless identity APIs plus the platform APIs make it possible — and you then own authentication surfaces, authorisation, accessibility and every security finding.

Justified by genuinely bespoke UX; expensive when chosen for appearance.

62 How do you test the site properly?Medium

As each persona — guest, authenticated customer, partner, partner manager — on a throttled connection and a real mobile device.

And with Lighthouse for performance and accessibility, which catches things configuration review does not.

63 The site looks different from the internal app.Easy

Different theming context, which is expected. Use SLDS styling hooks in custom components so they inherit whichever theme applies.

Hard-coded colours break in exactly this situation.

64 A custom LWC works internally and breaks in the community.Medium

Different runtime, guest context, unavailable APIs, or a navigation page reference that behaves differently in Experience Cloud.

Declare the right targets and test as the actual external persona.

65 Navigation works in the app and not the site.Medium

Community URLs are structured differently and some page reference types are unsupported there.

Use community-appropriate page references, and never hard-code a URL.

66 Toasts do not appear on the site.Easy

Toast behaviour differs by runtime and is not available everywhere.

Provide an inline message fallback so user feedback is never silently lost.

67 How do you deploy a community?Hard

Site metadata deploys, with real caveats — some settings, branding and content are environment-specific or need manual steps.

Document the manual steps as part of the release runbook rather than rediscovering them each time.

68 Site changes were made directly in production.Medium

Common, because Experience Builder invites it — and it is config drift with no review trail.

Decide whether the site is pipeline-managed or production-managed, and be consistent rather than both.

69 Performance budgets are needed.Medium

Set a target (page weight, Lighthouse score, time to interactive) and measure it on a throttled connection as part of release.

Without a budget, pages accumulate weight one reasonable addition at a time.

70 What makes a community succeed operationally?Medium

Maintained Knowledge, measured deflection, moderation someone actually performs, and performance budgets enforced.

The build is the easy part; the ongoing content and moderation work is what determines whether anyone uses it.

Licences & judgement (71–100)

71 Member-based or login-based licences?Medium

Member licences suit frequent users; login licences are a monthly pool suiting occasional use. The crossover is usually around two or three logins per member per month.

Model it with expected behaviour, then measure in a pilot — estimates here are consistently wrong.

72 Customer Community versus Customer Community Plus.Medium

Customer Community is high volume and cheap, with no roles, no reports and no sharing-rule participation. Plus adds roles, reports and sharing rules at a higher price.

The decision is driven by whether you need roles and reporting, not by user count alone.

73 Partners need Leads and Opportunities.Easy

Partner Community — the external licence family that includes CRM objects, partner roles and Super User.

Customer Community cannot do this, and discovering that mid-build is an expensive correction.

74 The licence does not include an object you need.Hard

Check object availability per licence before design — it is the single most expensive licence mistake in Experience Cloud.

Redesigning around custom objects or upgrading the population are the two options, both costly.

75 Login counts are higher than forecast.Medium

Run a pilot before committing at scale, and monitor consumption against the pool.

Login-based licensing can become more expensive than member-based very quickly if usage is higher than modelled.

76 Do community users consume platform limits?Medium

Yes — API calls, storage and transaction limits are org-wide, so community traffic competes with internal usage.

Which is worth modelling before a high-traffic public site goes live.

77 How do you reduce community licence cost?Medium

Choose the right licence type for actual behaviour, deactivate dormant users, and consider whether some populations need authenticated access at all.

Public content for anonymous visitors costs no licences.

78 An employee needs community access.Easy

Internal users can access communities with their existing licence — you do not buy a community licence for staff.

Check the member configuration includes their profile, which is the usual reason they cannot get in.

79 A user cannot log in to the community.Medium

Their profile is not a member of the site, the site is not active, their contact is not enabled as a customer user, or login settings block them.

Check membership first — it accounts for most of these cases.

80 How do you provision community users at scale?Medium

Self-registration with a hardened handler, or programmatic creation from the Contact — and a deactivation process for when the relationship ends.

Provisioning without deprovisioning leaves active external accounts indefinitely.

81 A customer relationship ended. What happens to their login?Medium

It must be deactivated as part of the business process, not discovered at audit.

External user offboarding is routinely missing, and it is a straightforward control to add.

82 How do you decide Experience Cloud versus a custom portal?Medium

Experience Cloud for speed, built-in identity, sharing and CMS where the UX fits its templates. Custom when the UX is genuinely bespoke or must live inside an existing application.

Be honest about what custom means: you own auth surfaces, accessibility and every security finding.

83 A partner wants their own branding.Medium

Audience-based branding within one site, or separate sites — weighed against the maintenance of several sites.

Several sites also means several guest users to harden, which is a real ongoing cost.

84 How many sites is too many?Medium

When nobody can enumerate them or say who owns each — because each is a guest user, a permission surface and a performance budget.

Maintain an inventory, and decommission deliberately rather than leaving them dormant.

85 A community is being used as an intranet.Medium

Legitimate — internal users with their existing licences, and no external exposure to worry about.

The guest user still exists though, so if any page is public the usual hardening applies.

86 Adoption is poor.Medium

Usually content, findability or performance rather than features. Check search terms returning nothing, page load times, and whether the task users came for takes fewer steps than phoning.

A portal slower than calling will not be used.

87 How do you measure a community's value?Medium

Deflection (sessions resolved without a case), self-service completion rates, search success, and the reduction in contact volume on the topics it covers.

Logins alone measure curiosity, not value.

88 Knowledge is out of date and the community suffers.Medium

The community exposes the content problem rather than causing it.

Review search terms that returned nothing — that report is the content backlog, and it is the highest-value investment.

89 A community needs to scale to a million users.Hard

High-volume licences, Sharing Sets rather than roles, aggressive caching and CDN for public content, and careful attention to org-wide limits and storage.

Also sharing recalculation behaviour at that volume, which is a genuine performance consideration.

90 How do you handle an incident on a public site?Hard

Contain first — deactivate the page or the site if necessary, accepting the outage. Then assess exposure with Event Monitoring, fix forward, and review the other sites.

A public exposure justifies taking the site down; hesitating because of availability makes it worse.

91 Legal asks whether customer data could be public.Hard

Answer with the five-surface review and the evidence: guest profile permissions, guest sharing rules, reachable Apex, system-context flows, and the registration handler.

And say honestly if any of those have not been reviewed recently.

92 Who should own an Experience Cloud site?Medium

A named owner for content, moderation, performance and the guest security review — spanning marketing, service and platform.

Unowned sites accumulate content rot and permission drift.

93 A site needs accessibility compliance.Medium

Public-facing sites frequently have a legal obligation, and standard components help but custom ones are yours to get right.

Keyboard operability, accessible names, contrast in both themes, and real heading structure. See accessibility.

94 Marketing wants to launch a site next week.Medium

The non-negotiables are the guest profile review, the five-surface audit and a performance check. Everything else can be simplified.

Shortcuts on guest security produce incidents that outlast the campaign.

95 How do you keep guest security from drifting?Medium

Re-run the five-surface review on a schedule and whenever a component or flow is added to a public page.

New components are the usual way a hardened site becomes exposed again.

96 A component was added to a public page without review.Hard

Treat it as a potential exposure: audit its Apex, its context and what it reads.

Add "is this page guest-accessible?" to the review checklist, because the risk is invisible in the component's own code.

97 How do you hand over a community?Medium

The licence model and why, the sharing design (External OWD, Sharing Sets, Share Groups) with the persona test matrix, the guest hardening evidence, the deployment manual steps, and the content and moderation owners.

The persona matrix is the part most often missing.

98 What is the commonest Experience Cloud design mistake?Hard

Choosing a licence before understanding the sharing implications — particularly assuming high-volume licences have roles.

Followed by under-hardened guest access, and by exposing records for SEO instead of using CMS.

99 What question do you expect in a security review?Hard

"What can an unauthenticated visitor reach?" — answered with evidence across the five surfaces rather than an assurance.

Being able to answer it with artefacts is the difference between a review that passes and one that becomes a project.

100 One habit that most improves Experience Cloud work?Hard

Testing as the actual persona — guest in an incognito window, authenticated customer, partner — and trying to reach what you did not intend.

Configuration review misses combination effects. Thirty minutes of adversarial testing per release catches what every other control misses.
