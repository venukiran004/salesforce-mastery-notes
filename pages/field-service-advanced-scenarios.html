Field Service Advanced — 100 Scenarios

Inside the engine and the gap matrix, availability resolution, optimisation and bundling internals, complex work, the FSL Apex API, scale, sharing and the deployment trap.

📚
How to use this Harder than the Field Service bank — this one assumes the model and goes at the engine. Concepts on Field Service — Advanced.

Inside the engine (1–22)

1 Describe the candidate pipeline.Hard

Candidate set (resources in territory) → work rules as a hard filter → a gap matrix of every legal slot built from availability and travel → objectives scoring each slot → graded slots 0–100, best first.

Nobody appears → rules. Wrong person appears → objectives. That diagnostic split is the point.

2 What exactly is the gap matrix?Hard

The set of genuinely available slots per candidate, derived from effective availability minus existing appointments and the travel between them.

It is what objectives score — so an empty matrix is an availability problem, not an objectives one.

3 Availability is computed from how many sources?Hard

Five, every time: operating hours and time slots, shifts, territory membership with effective dates, resource absences, and capacity for capacity-based resources.

It is never a field — it is derived per resource per day, which is why "he is definitely working Thursday" and the Gantt can disagree.

4 Shifts and operating hours both exist for a resource.Hard

Where shifts exist they generally take precedence over the hours pattern.

Mixing both models in one population is a reliable source of confusion — pick one model per population.

5 A grade of 78% — what does it mean?Hard

A weighted sum of that policy's objectives normalised to 0–100. Not a probability, not a quality measure, and not comparable across policies.

So never write business logic against an absolute grade threshold without documenting which policy it assumes.

6 All candidate grades are within a point of each other.Hard

The objectives are not discriminating between these slots — weights too close, or an objective that does not vary across the options.

Which means the "best" slot is effectively arbitrary, and dispatchers will notice before you do.

7 Name the work rule types you would expect in a policy.Hard

Match Skills, Match Territory, Working Territories, Resource Availability, Appointment Visiting Hours, Match Fields/Extended Match, Count Rule, Required/Excluded Resources, Resource Capacity, Multiday, and time/dependency rules.

Names vary slightly by package version — the mechanics do not.

8 What is a Count Rule for?Medium

Limiting matching appointments per resource per period — "no more than two boiler installs per engineer per day".

It is the rule people reach for when they are trying to express a capacity constraint with skills.

9 When do you use Match Fields instead of a skill?Medium

For attributes rather than competencies: van type, security clearance, language, brand accreditation.

Cheaper and clearer than inventing fake skills, and it does not pollute the skills model.

10 Required versus Preferred resource.Medium

Required is enforced by a hard rule — only those resources are candidates, so one holiday makes the job unschedulable. Preferred is a soft objective that ranks them higher.

Preferred is almost always what the business actually means.

11 Six reasonable rules produce zero candidates.Hard

Rules compose with AND, so individually sensible constraints can intersect to nothing on a particular day.

Keep a diagnostic policy with only availability and territory, and compare against it the moment scheduling fails.

12 How do you tune objectives methodically?Hard

Write the business rule in one sentence, start with two objectives, change one weight at a time, re-run the same day's data, and measure — travel minutes, SLA hit rate, jobs per engineer, overtime, unscheduled count.

Multi-variable tuning is unfalsifiable.

13 Over-weighting ASAP — what happens?Medium

Travel explodes and the day fragments, because the engine takes the earliest slot regardless of location.

Every objective has a characteristic failure mode when over-weighted, which is worth knowing before you adjust one.

14 Why prefer the lowest qualified resource?Medium

To preserve specialist capacity for work that needs it — the skill-level objective can prefer lower skill.

Over-weight it the other way and experts spend the day on trivial jobs.

15 Which policy does an optimisation run use?Medium

The one on the optimisation request — frequently a different policy from the console's.

That mismatch explains a lot of "optimisation undid what the dispatcher did".

16 Booking slots take twelve seconds.Hard

Slot calculation cost grows with candidate resources × horizon length × rule count — and the user is waiting.

Offer the next ten working days rather than ninety, and keep the booking policy leaner than the dispatch one.

17 Returning slots does not reserve them.Medium

Correct — two agents can offer the same slot seconds apart.

Handle the collision explicitly on booking rather than assuming the slot is still available.

18 Exact appointments versus arrival windows.Medium

Exact times are more precise and far more brittle in the real world; arrival windows absorb travel variance.

Choose from how reliable your travel model is — exact times with poor travel estimates guarantee broken promises.

19 Should customers see low-grade slots?Medium

No — suppress them. Never show a slot you would regret them choosing.

Also suppress overtime slots unless you are willing to pay for them.

20 How does the engine use travel?Hard

Travel between consecutive appointments is part of building the gap matrix, and travel minimisation is an objective scoring the slots.

So travel accuracy affects both feasibility and ranking — which is why calibration comes first.

21 A resource's first and last legs look wrong.Medium

Home base or start/end location not set correctly on the resource, so the engine invents those journeys.

It distorts the whole day's plan, and it is a quick thing to verify.

22 What is the five-question engine diagnosis?Hard

Is the candidate set non-empty? Which hard rule excluded them? Does the gap matrix contain any slot? Are the objectives discriminating? Is the policy the one I think?

Asked in order, that resolves most scheduling investigations.

Optimisation & bundling (23–46)

23 Scheduling versus optimisation.Medium

Scheduling places one appointment well; optimisation rearranges many to make the whole day cheaper.

Different engines, different failure modes — and they can disagree, which is where pinning originates.

24 Where do you diagnose a run that "did nothing"?Medium

The optimisation request record — its status and result — not the Gantt.

It may be failing, or completing with nothing in scope, and the Gantt cannot tell you which.

25 What does the enhanced engine change?Medium

Faster runs over larger territories and horizons, better travel modelling, multi-territory handling, less org CPU — and it is a prerequisite for bundling.

Some legacy behaviours differ, so re-test every policy after migrating.

26 Optimisation is still asynchronous. Why does that matter?Medium

You cannot request it and read the result in the same transaction. Any code assuming otherwise is wrong.

You get a request record; the plan changes later.

27 How long should the optimisation horizon be?Medium

Short — days, not quarters. Runtime grows fast with the date range.

A short rolling horizon run often beats a long one run rarely, and it keeps the plan responsive.

28 Nightly runs collide with each other.Medium

Stagger territories, and leave headroom before the working day starts.

Runs that overlap compete for capacity and can finish after dispatchers have already started working.

29 In-day optimisation versus reshuffle.Medium

In-day re-plans today's remaining undispatched work; reshuffle moves appointments aside to fit one urgent job.

Use reshuffle for emergencies and in-day for recovering a morning that went wrong.

30 A cancellation left a gap at 11:00.Easy

Fill-in schedule for that resource, or group-nearby to find unscheduled work close to the existing route.

A recipe can do this automatically on cancellation, which is one of the higher-value automations.

31 What limits what optimisation can move?Hard

Pinned appointments, dispatched and in-progress ones, dependencies, multiday blocks, and the running user's record access.

Each is legitimate individually; together they can leave the engine almost nothing to rearrange.

32 Optimisation worked and now does not. Nothing deployed.Hard

Accumulated state: pinning creep, dispatch happening earlier, shifts not generated for the new period, seasonal absence volume, appointment volume outgrowing the horizon, or the job failing silently.

Also a package upgrade or an edit to a shared policy — which is data, so there is no deployment history.

33 What does bundling actually produce?Medium

A bundle appointment with the original appointments as members, scheduled and dispatched as one visit.

Meter reads, inspections and multi-unit sites are the classic cases.

34 Which bundling pieces do you configure?Hard

A bundle policy (the strategy), a config (max duration, max members, duration aggregation), a restriction policy (what may bundle with what), a sort policy (order of members), and a propagation policy (what flows from bundle to members).

And it requires the enhanced engine.

35 Bundles are not forming.Medium

Enhanced optimisation off, restriction policy too narrow (same location, work type, day), config limits exceeded, or the bundling job not running.

Check in that order — the restriction policy is the usual answer.

36 When should you not bundle?Medium

When members need different skills or resources, when each has its own SLA or customer promise, or when per-job reporting and billing must stay strict and you have not tested it.

And when you have not decided what happens if one member fails.

37 A bundle is broken after three of five members are done.Hard

Design that path deliberately: what happens to member statuses, actual times, products consumed and service reports.

It is the most commonly forgotten part of bundling, and it happens in week one.

38 Why does the propagation policy matter?Medium

It determines which changes flow from the bundle to its members, including status — so completing a bundle may or may not complete its members.

Get it wrong and reporting disagrees with reality.

39 Travel is still high after optimisation.Hard

Weights, pinning and dispatch, territory overlap, narrow arrival windows, wrong home base, insufficient horizon or resource pool, and travel settings (street routing versus straight line).

In that order — and calibrate travel before concluding anything about weights.

40 How do you calibrate travel?Hard

Compare a week of engine-estimated travel against actual start times from the mobile app, and adjust until they broadly agree.

Do it before tuning objectives. Otherwise every plan is fiction and engineers quietly stop following the schedule.

41 What is the cost of an uncalibrated model?Hard

Silent loss of the schedule as a source of truth — technicians fall behind by mid-morning and go their own way, and nobody raises a ticket.

That is worse than a visibly bad plan, because it is undetected.

42 Which metrics tell you optimisation is working?Medium

Total travel minutes, jobs per engineer per day, SLA hit rate, overtime hours and unscheduled count — against a baseline captured before you enabled it.

Without the baseline, improvement is an assertion.

43 How do you pilot optimisation?Medium

One territory, nightly, comparing travel and SLA against the manual baseline before rolling wider.

A pilot also surfaces the pinning and dispatch-timing behaviours you will need to manage at scale.

44 Dispatchers override optimisation constantly.Medium

They know something the model does not — frequently travel reality, customer preference or a skill nuance.

Capture what they are correcting for and encode it, rather than treating the overrides as noncompliance.

45 Can you request optimisation from Apex?Medium

Yes — build a request with the horizon, territories, policy and a filter field, and submit it. You get a request record back.

It is asynchronous, so do not design a flow that waits for the result.

46 What is the single biggest optimisation mistake?Hard

Tuning objective weights before calibrating travel — followed by letting pinning accumulate until the engine has nothing to move.

Both produce "optimisation does not work" conclusions about a correctly functioning engine.

Complex work, recipes & status (47–66)

47 Name the dependency types and a use for each.Hard

Start After Finish (survey then install), Immediately Follow (scaffolding up, work, down), Same Start (two specialists at once), Same Resource (return visit where context matters).

Chains of them become unschedulable in ways that are painful to diagnose.

48 Dependencies exist and the engine ignores them.Hard

The dependency rule is not in the policy — the records are data, enforcement is policy.

And it must be in both the scheduling and optimisation policies, which are frequently different records.

49 Crew or dependencies?Medium

Crew when the same people always work together — one schedulable unit, one arrival, far easier to optimise.

Dependencies when the pairing varies, different skills are needed simultaneously, time must be tracked separately, or phases fall on different days.

50 A dependency chain cannot be scheduled.Hard

Each dependency is another constraint, so a chain plus a narrow window can be genuinely infeasible.

Relax the window, break the chain, or reduce to the dependencies that genuinely matter.

51 How does the engine handle a multiday appointment?Medium

It spreads the duration across consecutive working days, respecting hours, shifts and absences on each one — with the multiday rule enabled.

Optimisation treats them as heavy blocks, which frequently explains a poor-looking plan.

52 Multiday progress tracking is too coarse.Medium

One appointment means one set of actual times. If the business wants day-by-day sign-off, you need separate dependent appointments.

That is a modelling decision to make before go-live, not after.

53 Can a multiday job be interrupted for an emergency?Medium

Decide the policy up front, and who is allowed to do it.

Undefined interruption rules mean dispatchers make it up under pressure, inconsistently.

54 What can scheduling recipes do?Medium

Event-driven automation: auto-schedule new appointments, reshuffle for emergencies, fill a gap on cancellation, group nearby work, bundle.

Each names its own policy and conditions — configuration rather than code.

55 Several recipes are producing churn.Hard

They are reacting to each other's changes. Enable one at a time and measure.

Because the engine is asynchronous, the resulting loop is invisible in a single debug log and can run for hours.

56 How do you guard an automation against the engine?Hard

Explicit conditions ("status category None and no assigned resource"), and never react to the fields the engine writes — scheduled start/end, assigned resource — unless you mean to.

Plus a circuit-breaker counter you can alert on.

57 What horizon should auto-dispatch use?Medium

Late enough that the day has settled, early enough that technicians are not starting blind — which is a business decision per territory.

Dispatch also protects appointments from optimisation, so the horizon directly affects how much the engine can improve.

58 Drip feed is proposed. What is the trade?Medium

It controls cherry-picking and suits volatile days; technicians who like to plan their own day dislike it.

That is a change-management decision rather than a technical one, and it needs push notifications to work.

59 Why do status categories matter so much?Hard

The engine, Gantt, mobile app and reporting all key off the category, not your label.

A custom status in the wrong category produces appointments optimisation will not move, work that never reaches a device, or jobs the engine still thinks are open.

60 List the categories and what each implies.Medium

None (fair game for auto-scheduling), Scheduled (movable), Dispatched (generally protected), In Progress (do not reschedule), Completed (frees capacity, triggers downstream), Cannot Complete (usually a follow-up), Cancelled (release capacity and refill).

Every custom status must map to one of these.

61 Status transitions need restricting.Medium

Status transition settings, by profile or permission — so a technician cannot mark Completed without capturing parts, and a dispatcher cannot reverse a completion.

Combine with required fields that are offline-safe.

62 A status change is not triggering the engine.Medium

Check the category rather than the label, and whether the recipe's conditions actually match.

Most "the engine ignored it" cases are a category or condition mismatch rather than an engine fault.

63 Capacity is not released on cancellation.Medium

Capacity is consumed by scheduled work, so a cancelled appointment must release it — if the status category is wrong, it does not.

Contractors then silently appear full, which looks like a capacity configuration problem.

64 How do you model "cannot complete, need to return"?Medium

The Cannot Complete category on the original appointment, plus a new appointment on the same Work Order for the return visit.

That is exactly why one Work Order can have several appointments, and it keeps the job's history intact.

65 Reporting on repeat visits is needed.Medium

Count appointments per Work Order, and categorise the reasons for the additional ones — parts, skills, access, diagnosis.

That report is the first-time-fix improvement backlog, and it is more useful than the headline rate.

66 What is the commonest complex-work mistake?Hard

Creating dependency records and not adding the rule to both policies — so the engine schedules out of order and optimisation undoes manual fixes.

Followed by using dependencies where a crew would have been far simpler.

Apex, scale & deployment (67–88)

67 How should Apex get candidate slots?Hard

Through the FSL API — resolving the policy by name, requesting graded slots, and suppressing poor grades before showing them.

Signatures vary by package version, so verify against the developer guide for your installed version.

68 Why never hard-code a policy Id?Easy

Policies are records, so Ids differ in every org and every refreshed sandbox.

Resolve by name at runtime with the name in Custom Metadata — this single habit prevents a whole category of post-deployment incidents.

69 Why not call the scheduling API from a trigger?Medium

It is slow, effectively a round trip to the engine, and cannot be bulkified per record — so a 200-record load blows CPU and a retry corrupts the plan.

Publish an event or enqueue a Queueable, or better, use a recipe.

70 A scheduling call returned nothing. Is that an error?Medium

No — it means no candidate passed every hard rule. Make that visible rather than silently losing the job.

Flag the appointment with the policy name and a note so a dispatcher can act on it.

71 How do you make scheduling automation safe at volume?Hard

Queueable, one appointment at a time, with failures recorded per record and a retry cap — plus a finalizer so an uncatchable limit breach is logged.

And guard the entry condition tightly so the automation does not react to the engine's own writes.

72 Which automation mechanism for which job?Medium

Recipe for standard scheduling behaviour; record-triggered flow for field updates and notifications; platform event plus Queueable for anything calling the engine or doing heavy post-sync work; batch for nightly reconciliation.

Keep triggers thin — the package has its own competing for the same CPU.

73 Territory size is hurting performance.Hard

Gantt rendering, candidate calculation and optimisation runtime are all superlinear in resources × appointments.

Split into territories a dispatcher can manage and use a hierarchy for reporting — the hierarchy gives you the roll-up without the performance cost.

74 Appointment volume is growing fast.Medium

Service Appointment becomes a large object with the usual LDV symptoms — reports, list views and sharing recalculation.

Archive completed work on a schedule and keep filters selective. See LDV.

75 Mobile sync is slow for everyone.Medium

Priming volume. Narrow the objects, related lists and date window — most technicians need days, not months.

It also reduces device storage and battery consumption.

76 Who needs broad record access, and why?Hard

Dispatchers (across territories), and the optimisation running user — whose access limits what the engine can see and move.

A narrow running user silently optimises a smaller world, with no error anywhere.

77 Technicians see blank screens on the device.Medium

Under-sharing. Log in as them in a browser first — if a related list is empty there too, it is record access, not the app.

That check saves days of debugging the wrong layer.

78 Crew members cannot see their work.Medium

Work is assigned to the crew resource, which is a different record from the person — so sharing must cover crew-assigned appointments.

Easy to forget, and it presents as a mobile problem.

79 Contractors need access from outside the org.Hard

Experience Cloud with Sharing Sets matching their Contact to the Service Resource and Assigned Resource records, or dedicated contractor licences.

Test with a real contractor login including the mobile app — external sharing mistakes here are genuinely serious.

80 Your deployment is missing everything.Hard

Because scheduling policies, work rules, objectives, territories, operating hours, shift patterns, skills and package settings are records.

You need a scripted, version-controlled data migration as part of the release process, with references re-pointed by name.

81 A sandbox refresh wiped the configuration.Medium

Expected — it is data. Re-run the seeding migration as part of the post-refresh checklist.

Which is why that migration must be repeatable rather than a one-off done at go-live.

82 How do you test scheduling changes?Hard

A sandbox with realistic geography, resources, skills and a week of appointments — run the real policies and inspect the plan.

Keep it as a fixed regression set with expected outcomes, re-run after every policy change and package upgrade.

83 Can you unit-test the optimiser?Medium

Not meaningfully. Unit-test your own handlers, guards and idempotency; scenario-test the engine.

Pretending otherwise produces tests that pass against a plan that is wrong.

84 A package upgrade is due.Medium

Install it in a Full sandbox, re-run the scheduling regression set, and check the enhanced-engine behaviours specifically.

Treat it as a project with regression time — FSL upgrades change behaviour more often than release notes suggest.

85 How do you monitor Field Service health?Medium

Unscheduled count, travel minutes, SLA attainment, first-time fix, overtime, pinned appointment count, optimisation request outcomes and mobile sync errors.

Review weekly. Pinned counts and sync errors are the two that degrade silently.

86 An FSL trigger competes with yours.Medium

The package's triggers run on the same save and share your CPU budget.

Keep your appointment and work order triggers thin, push heavy work async, and read CUMULATIVE_LIMIT_USAGE per namespace to attribute the cost.

87 AI in field service — what is genuinely useful?Medium

Pre-work briefing from asset history, post-work summaries from notes, knowledge surfacing for the fault code, triage before a visit is booked, and duration prediction from history.

Not writing to the schedule unsupervised — the optimiser already owns that decision.

88 Field Service or Salesforce Scheduler?Easy

Scheduler for appointments with people where travel is irrelevant. Field Service for sending people to places, where drive time is the cost driver and you need assets, parts and an offline app.

If nobody drives anywhere, Field Service is machinery you will not use.

Programme judgement (89–100)

89 What is the implementation sequence?Hard

Operating model on paper → work taxonomy with honest durations → availability foundation → resources and skills → calibrate travel → policy library → prove manually → automate one recipe at a time → pilot optimisation → ship mobile carefully → close the parts loop → maintenance and customer layer → instrument everything.

Skipping ahead is how territories get redone in month six.

90 Why does the work taxonomy come so early?Medium

Work Types set duration and required skills, and every downstream plan inherits those numbers.

Bad durations poison scheduling, travel, capacity and the business case simultaneously.

91 The business wants automation at go-live.Medium

Prove it manually first. If dispatchers do not trust the candidate grades, automated scheduling will be fought rather than used.

Then one recipe at a time against a measured baseline.

92 What are the anti-patterns you watch for?Hard

One giant territory; ten hard rules "to be safe"; custom statuses with no category mapping; skills invented for field matches; required-resource preferences where preferred was meant; tuning before calibrating travel; pinning everything; priming three months to every device; treating package config as metadata.

Each is individually reasonable and collectively fatal.

93 How do you diagnose "the system is wrong" complaints?Hard

Separate the symptom: no candidates (rules), wrong resource (objectives), optimisation inert (pinning and policy), plan unfollowable (travel calibration), work not on device (status category or sharing).

Each has a different investigation, and conflating them wastes days.

94 A programme is stalling on scheduling quality.Hard

Check the foundations before the engine: durations, travel accuracy, territory shape, skills data and absence feeds.

Engine tuning cannot compensate for inputs that do not describe reality.

95 How do you justify the investment?Medium

In the order the money actually sits: avoid the visit, fix it first time, cut travel, then shave minutes on site.

Teams start at the last because it feels technical, and then the business case does not land.

96 What do you hand over at the end?Medium

The policy library with each policy's intent, the configuration migration script, the regression set, the calibration evidence, the status category mapping, and the monitoring dashboard.

Without the migration script and the regression set, nobody can safely change scheduling.

97 How do you keep the model honest over time?Hard

Compare estimated against actual — travel, durations, arrival times — weekly, and correct the model.

Also re-tune seasonally: winter demand, holiday cover and new territories all invalidate last year's weights.

98 A dispatcher asks why they cannot just do it manually.Medium

At small scale they can, and well. The argument for the engine is volume, consistency and travel cost at scale.

And it only wins that argument if the travel model is accurate — otherwise the dispatcher genuinely is better.

99 What is the commonest Field Service programme failure?Hard

Optimising the schedule while ignoring parts and triage — so first-time fix never improves and the travel saving is swamped by repeat visits.

Followed by uncalibrated travel, which invalidates the scheduling work itself.

100 One habit that most improves advanced Field Service work?Hard

Maintaining a scheduling regression set — a fixed week of realistic data with expected outcomes — and re-running it after every policy change, configuration migration and package upgrade.

Because the configuration is data with no deployment gate, that set is the only thing standing between a policy tweak and a broken plan nobody notices for a week.
