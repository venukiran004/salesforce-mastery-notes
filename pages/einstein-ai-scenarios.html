Einstein & AI — 100 Scenarios

Predictive versus generative, data readiness, the Einstein Trust Layer, grounding and prompt templates, scoring and drift, bias and governance, and knowing when AI is the wrong answer.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Einstein & AI, its Advanced section, and Agentforce.

Choosing the right tool (1–22)

1 Predictive or generative for this problem?Medium

Predictive for a number or a class from structured history — likelihood to close, churn risk, case field values. Generative for language — summarising, drafting, rewriting, answering from documents.

Using the wrong family is the commonest AI design error, and it is visible from the problem statement alone.

2 The business wants "AI on the sales pipeline".Medium

Convert it into a decision someone will take differently. "Which deals should this rep call today?" is actionable; "AI on the pipeline" is not.

If no behaviour changes, the model has no value regardless of its accuracy.

3 Would a formula solve this?Medium

Ask it every time. If the rule is known and stable, a formula or flow is cheaper, explainable, testable and instant.

AI earns its place where the pattern is genuinely unknown or the input is unstructured language.

4 Einstein Prediction Builder or Opportunity Scoring?Medium

Out-of-the-box scoring when the standard prediction matches your question — far less work. Prediction Builder for a custom yes/no or numeric outcome on your own objects.

Check the standard feature first; teams build custom models for predictions Salesforce already ships.

5 Do you have enough data to train?Hard

You need a meaningful volume of historical records with known outcomes and reasonable balance between classes.

A few hundred records, or 2% positives, will not produce a usable model — and this is the point at which most AI projects should stop.

6 The data is poor. Should you proceed?Hard

No. Fix data quality first — AI amplifies what is in the data, and a model trained on inconsistent history produces confident nonsense.

Saying this early is more valuable than delivering a model nobody can trust.

7 Where does the outcome field come from?Hard

It must be reliably populated for historical records and reflect the real outcome — not a field people fill in inconsistently.

An unreliable label is the most common hidden cause of a model that performs badly for no apparent reason.

8 A field leaks the answer.Hard

Target leakage — a field only populated after the outcome is known, such as a closed-reason on a won/lost prediction.

It produces a model that scores brilliantly in training and fails in production, which is the signature of the problem.

9 Scores look suspiciously accurate.Hard

Suspect leakage before celebrating. Review every input field for whether it could be known at prediction time.

Near-perfect accuracy on a hard business problem is a bug report, not a result.

10 Einstein Bots or Agentforce?Medium

Bots are dialogue-tree based and predictable; Agentforce reasons over topics and actions with far greater flexibility.

Agentforce for genuinely open interactions; a tree where the path is known and must never deviate. See Agentforce.

11 Case classification is wanted.Medium

Einstein Case Classification predicts field values from case text, with Case Routing acting on them.

It needs consistent historical categorisation — if humans have been inconsistent, the model learns the inconsistency.

12 Agents want case summaries.Easy

A strong generative use case: real time saved, low risk because a human reads and verifies it immediately.

Summarisation and drafting are where generative AI delivers value with the least exposure.

13 Reply drafting for emails is requested.Medium

Good use case with a human in the loop — the agent edits before sending, so errors are caught before they reach a customer.

Ground it in Knowledge so the content is accurate rather than plausible.

14 Should AI send customer emails unreviewed?Hard

Not initially. Start with draft-and-review, measure the edit rate, and only consider automation where the content is low-risk and the edit rate is very low.

The question is always what happens when it is wrong and who notices.

15 Next Best Action versus a model.Medium

Next Best Action delivers recommendations with business rules and optional model input — strategies are explainable and auditable.

Prefer it where the logic is partly known; it combines rules with scoring rather than replacing one with the other.

16 Einstein Activity Capture is proposed.Medium

It syncs email and events, and the data is stored differently from standard activity records — which affects reporting and retention.

Understand that storage model before committing; it surprises teams at reporting time.

17 Forecasting with Einstein is wanted.Medium

Einstein Forecasting projects from pipeline history, and it needs clean stage and close-date hygiene.

If reps update close dates late and inconsistently, the forecast reflects that behaviour rather than reality.

18 Lead scoring is already in place and ignored.Medium

Adoption problem, not a model problem. Scores nobody acts on have no value — check whether the score appears where the work happens and whether anyone was told what to do with it.

Embed it in the queue or list view that drives the day.

19 What does Data Cloud add to AI?Hard

Unified profiles across systems — ingestion, identity resolution, calculated insights — which gives models and grounding a complete view rather than one org's slice.

It is frequently the prerequisite for AI that reflects the whole customer relationship.

20 Should everything go into Data Cloud?Medium

No — ingest what serves identity resolution, segmentation, grounding or analytics. Consumption has real cost.

Start from the use case and ingest backwards from it, rather than ingesting first and finding a purpose later.

21 Identity resolution is merging the wrong people.Hard

Match rules are too loose — review them, and prefer verified identifiers over fuzzy name and address matching.

A wrong merge exposes one customer's data to another, so this is a privacy incident rather than a data tidiness issue.

22 When is AI the wrong answer?Hard

When the rule is known, when the data is poor or thin, when nobody will change behaviour based on the output, when an error is unacceptable and unreviewable, or when the cost exceeds the value.

Being willing to say so is the most valuable judgement in this area.

Generative & grounding (23–50)

23 What does the Einstein Trust Layer do?Hard

Zero data retention with the model provider, dynamic grounding, PII masking, toxicity detection and an audit trail.

It is the answer to "where does our data go" — and the reason you use platform generative features rather than calling a model directly.

24 Legal asks whether customer data trains the model.Hard

No — zero retention means the provider does not store or train on prompts sent through the Trust Layer.

That commitment is specifically what makes generative features viable for regulated data.

25 What is grounding?Medium

Injecting your own data — records, Knowledge, Data Cloud — into the prompt so the answer comes from your facts rather than the model's general knowledge.

It is the single biggest determinant of output quality, and the main defence against hallucination.

26 The output is plausible but wrong.Hard

Hallucination — almost always insufficient grounding. Ground in authoritative content, instruct the model to answer only from what is supplied, and have it say when it does not know.

Then verify against real cases rather than assuming the instruction was followed.

27 How do you build a prompt template?Medium

Prompt Builder, with record data, related lists, Knowledge or Flow and Apex outputs merged in — version it and test on real examples.

Treat it as code: it has inputs, behaviour and regressions, and it belongs in source control.

28 A prompt needs computed data.Medium

An Apex or Flow grounding source — compute the value and merge it in, rather than hoping the model derives it from raw records.

Models are poor at arithmetic over records and good at language about facts you supply.

29 The prompt exceeds the context window.Hard

Reduce what you send: retrieve the most relevant content rather than everything, summarise before including, and bound related records.

More context is not better context — precision beats volume.

30 Output quality varies between runs.Medium

Expected — generation is non-deterministic. Reduce variance with tighter instructions, better grounding and a constrained output format.

And test across many examples; a single good result tells you very little.

31 You need structured output from a prompt.Medium

Specify the format explicitly, give an example, and validate what comes back before using it.

Never assume well-formed JSON — parse defensively and handle the failure path.

32 How do you test a prompt template?Hard

An evaluation set of representative inputs with expected characteristics, run after every change, with results compared.

Without it, prompt changes are guesswork — and this is the discipline most AI projects skip and later regret.

33 A prompt change broke something else.Hard

Exactly why the evaluation set exists — prompts have regressions like code, and a fix for one case commonly degrades another.

Re-run the full set rather than spot-checking the case you were fixing.

34 Users try to manipulate the prompt.Hard

Prompt injection. Treat all user input as untrusted, keep instructions separate from data, constrain what actions are reachable, and never let model output drive a privileged operation unchecked.

Guardrails must be enforced outside the prompt — the model is not a security boundary.

35 A record contains injected instructions.Hard

Indirect injection — grounded content carrying instructions the model may follow. Anyone who can write to a grounded field can attempt it.

Which is why permissions on grounded data matter, and why actions need their own authorisation checks.

36 Does AI respect sharing rules?Hard

Grounding runs in the user's context, so it should surface only what they can see — and that must be verified, not assumed, particularly where Apex grounding sources are involved.

An Apex source running without sharing leaks through the prompt.

37 How do you audit what AI was asked and answered?Medium

The Trust Layer's audit trail, with feedback captured alongside.

It is what lets you investigate a complaint and measure quality over time — and it needs someone to actually review it.

38 PII is appearing in prompts.Hard

The Trust Layer masks detected PII, and you should also question whether the field needs to be in the prompt at all.

Minimise what you send — masking is a safety net, not a licence to include everything.

39 Toxic or inappropriate output is reported.Hard

Toxicity detection scores and can block it, and the incident should be captured and reviewed rather than dismissed.

For customer-facing output, keep a human in the loop until the evidence says it is safe.

40 Can you bring your own LLM?Medium

Yes — BYOLLM routes through the Trust Layer, so the governance applies to your own model too.

Worth it where you have a fine-tuned or domain-specific model; unnecessary for most cases.

41 Why not call OpenAI directly from Apex?Hard

You lose zero retention, PII masking, toxicity detection and the audit trail — and you own the key management and the compliance argument.

Possible, occasionally justified, and a significant step backwards on governance for most uses.

42 How are generative features consumed commercially?Medium

Credit-based — generations and agent interactions draw down a balance, so an enthusiastic rollout has a real cost.

Monitor consumption from day one and model it before scaling to a whole department.

43 Credit consumption is higher than expected.Medium

Find the heaviest use — usually one feature firing more often than designed, or an automatic trigger nobody intended.

Review where generation is invoked and whether every invocation delivers value.

44 Knowledge quality limits the output.Medium

Grounded answers can only be as good as the grounded content, so Knowledge gardening is AI work.

Articles that are out of date produce confidently wrong answers at scale.

45 Search returns poor grounding results.Hard

Retrieval quality — chunking, indexing and how the search query is formed. The best model cannot fix bad retrieval.

Debug retrieval separately from generation: look at what was retrieved before blaming the answer.

46 How do you measure whether a generative feature works?Hard

Edit rate on drafts, handle-time change, deflection, thumbs-down rate, and sampled human review against a rubric.

Not usage — usage measures curiosity. Edit rate is the most honest early signal.

47 Agents say the summaries are not useful.Medium

Look at real outputs with them and find what is missing or wrong — usually grounding gaps or a prompt optimised for the wrong audience.

Reviewing twenty real outputs with the people who use them beats any amount of speculation.

48 Where should a human stay in the loop?Hard

Anywhere an error is costly, irreversible or customer-visible — and until measurement shows the quality justifies removing them.

The default is review; automation is earned with evidence.

49 How do you roll out a generative feature?Medium

A pilot team, an evaluation set, measured edit rates and feedback capture, then expand — with credit consumption monitored.

Enabling it org-wide on day one produces both a cost surprise and an unmeasured quality problem.

50 What determines generative quality most?Hard

Grounding — the relevance and accuracy of the data you put in the prompt. Model choice and prompt wording matter far less.

Which means most AI quality work is actually data and content work.

Models, scoring & drift (51–76)

51 What does a 70% score mean?Hard

A relative ranking signal, not a 70% certainty of the outcome — and that distinction matters enormously for how people use it.

Teach users to triage by rank rather than to read the number as a probability.

52 Users treat scores as certainties.Medium

An enablement problem with real consequences — ignoring a low-scored deal that was winnable, or trusting a high score blindly.

Frame it as prioritisation, show the top factors, and say plainly that it is probabilistic.

53 Why did the model predict this?Medium

Top predictors and factor contributions provide the explanation, and surfacing them is what makes a score actionable.

A score without reasons is ignored; a score with "because no activity in 30 days" gets acted on.

54 A predictor is surprising.Hard

Investigate rather than accept it — surprising predictors are frequently leakage, a proxy for something else, or an artefact of how data is entered.

Occasionally it is a genuine insight. The investigation distinguishes them.

55 Model performance degraded over months.Hard

Drift — the world changed, the process changed, or the data distribution moved. Monitor accuracy over time and retrain.

A model is not a deliverable you finish; it needs an owner and a monitoring cadence.

56 Who owns a deployed model?Medium

A named person responsible for monitoring accuracy, retraining, reviewing bias and deciding when to retire it.

Unowned models quietly degrade and keep being trusted, which is worse than having no model at all.

57 How often should you retrain?Medium

On a cadence informed by monitoring, and immediately after a material process or market change.

Retraining on a schedule without monitoring misses drift between runs; monitoring tells you when it actually matters.

58 A sales process changed. What happens to the model?Hard

Its training data describes the old process, so its predictions are about a world that no longer exists — retrain, and expect reduced accuracy until enough new history accumulates.

Flag the gap honestly rather than letting people trust stale scores.

59 Is the model biased?Hard

Check for protected-attribute proxies, measure outcomes across segments, and review whether historical bias is being learned and amplified.

Models trained on biased history reproduce it at scale and with apparent objectivity, which is the real danger.

60 Postcode is a strong predictor.Hard

A classic proxy for protected attributes. Examine what it is really standing in for and whether using it is defensible.

Strong and uncomfortable predictors deserve scrutiny rather than acceptance.

61 AI affects a decision about a person.Hard

Higher governance: documented purpose, bias testing, human review, an appeal path, and transparency about AI involvement.

Regulations increasingly require this, and it is the right thing regardless of the regulation.

62 Must customers be told AI was involved?Medium

Increasingly yes, and it is good practice regardless — particularly for generated customer-facing content.

Check your jurisdiction's requirements rather than assuming they do not apply.

63 What does AI governance need?Hard

A register of AI use with purpose and owner, data sources documented, bias and accuracy review, human-in-the-loop decisions recorded, retention and retraining policy, and an incident path.

Most organisations have none of this and considerable AI in production.

64 How do you validate a model before launch?Hard

Hold-out evaluation, a check for leakage, segment-level performance, and a shadow period comparing predictions against actual outcomes before anyone acts on them.

The shadow period is the step most often skipped and the most informative.

65 The model is accurate and nobody uses it.Medium

Delivery problem: wrong place, wrong moment, no explanation, or no clear action attached.

Put it in the list view or queue where the decision happens, with the reasons visible and the next action obvious.

66 Can Einstein features run in a sandbox?Medium

Availability varies by feature, and sandbox data is often unrepresentative for training anyway.

Check availability early — discovering a feature cannot be tested pre-production reshapes the release plan.

67 How do you deploy AI configuration?Hard

Prompt templates and some configuration are metadata; trained models and certain settings are environment-specific.

Document the manual steps, and keep prompt templates in source control with their evaluation set.

68 A prompt template was edited in production.Medium

Treat it as a code change made without review — because that is what it is.

Prompts belong in the pipeline with versioning and an evaluation run, not edited live under pressure.

69 How do you estimate AI project effort?Hard

Weight data readiness heavily — it is usually the majority of the work — then grounding, evaluation, enablement and monitoring.

Estimates that assume the data is ready are the reason AI projects overrun.

70 The executive sponsor wants AI by next quarter.Hard

Offer the fastest genuinely valuable thing — usually a grounded summarisation or drafting feature, which needs no training data and delivers measurable time savings.

And be honest that predictive work waits on data readiness.

71 What is the fastest AI win in most orgs?Medium

Case or conversation summarisation grounded in existing data: no training, immediate time saving, human verification built in.

It also builds the organisational confidence needed for anything more ambitious.

72 Should AI be enabled everywhere it is available?Medium

No — each feature needs a purpose, an owner, measurement and a credit budget.

Enabling everything produces cost, inconsistent quality and no way to tell what is working.

73 An AI feature gives a customer wrong information.Hard

Treat it as an incident: contain by disabling or adding review, correct with the customer, find the cause through the audit trail, and add the case to the evaluation set.

Then ask what else the same grounding gap could have gotten wrong.

74 How do you decide to retire a model?Medium

When accuracy no longer justifies it, when nobody acts on it, or when a rule now does the job better.

Retiring an unused model is a positive outcome, not a failure — stale models erode trust in the rest.

75 Data Cloud costs are rising.Medium

Review ingestion volume, segmentation frequency and calculated insights — consumption is driven by what you process, not just what you store.

Ingest and recalculate what the use cases need and no more.

76 What is the commonest AI project failure?Hard

Starting from the technology rather than a decision someone will make differently — producing an accurate model nobody uses.

Closely followed by proceeding on data that was never good enough.

Judgement & communication (77–100)

77 The sponsor asks "can AI do this?"Medium

Answer with the decision, the data and the measurement: what choice changes, whether the history supports it, and how you will know it works.

That reframing is more useful than a yes or no.

78 How do you explain a probabilistic system to a business user?Medium

It is a prioritisation aid that is right more often than chance, not an oracle — use it to order your day, not to make decisions you would otherwise reason about.

Setting that expectation early prevents both over-trust and dismissal.

79 A stakeholder wants 95% accuracy.Hard

Discuss what accuracy means for their class balance — 95% is trivial if 95% of cases are negative, and that number alone is meaningless.

Agree the metric that reflects the business cost of each error type.

80 False positives and false negatives differ in cost.Hard

Then set the threshold accordingly rather than defaulting to the midpoint — a missed churn risk and a wasted call are not equally expensive.

The threshold is a business decision, not a technical one.

81 How do you present model results honestly?Hard

Performance on held-out data with the relevant metrics, segment breakdowns, known limitations, and what would make it better.

Overselling an AI result is how you lose the credibility you need for the next one.

82 A vendor claims their AI will solve it.Medium

Ask what data it trains on, how it grounds, where data goes, how it is evaluated, and what happens when it is wrong.

Those five questions separate real products from demos.

83 AI is proposed to fix a process problem.Hard

Say so. If cases are mis-routed because the routing rules are wrong, fixing the rules beats predicting the mis-routing.

AI applied over a broken process automates the brokenness.

84 Everyone wants a chatbot.Medium

Ask what questions it answers and whether the content to answer them exists and is accurate.

A chatbot over poor Knowledge is a faster route to wrong answers. See Agentforce scenarios.

85 How do you build organisational trust in AI?Medium

Start with low-risk, human-reviewed, obviously useful features; measure and publish the results; be candid about limitations.

One oversold failure costs more trust than several modest successes build.

86 Users distrust AI after a bad experience.Medium

Acknowledge it, fix the specific cause, show the evidence of improvement, and re-introduce with their involvement.

Dismissing the complaint guarantees the feature is never used again.

87 How do you handle AI feature release velocity?Medium

Track release notes, verify capabilities against current documentation rather than memory, and keep architectural decisions reversible.

This area moves fast enough that year-old knowledge is a genuine liability.

88 You are asked about a feature you are unsure about.Medium

Say you will verify against current documentation rather than guessing — AI feature boundaries change every release.

Confidently wrong answers about AI capability cause real project damage.

89 Where does AI fit in the architecture?Hard

As a component with inputs, outputs, failure modes and an owner — not a layer sprinkled over everything.

Which means designing what happens when it is unavailable, slow or wrong.

90 An AI call needs to happen in a transaction.Hard

Generation is slow and can fail, so keep it asynchronous and out of the synchronous save path where possible.

An AI call inside a trigger is a latency and reliability risk for every save on that object.

91 The AI service is unavailable.Medium

Degrade gracefully — the feature is unavailable, the rest of the application works, and the user sees a clear message.

A failed generation must never block a save or lose the user's work.

92 How do you document an AI feature for handover?Medium

Purpose and decision supported, data sources and grounding, the prompt template with its evaluation set, measurement and current results, the owner, and the known limitations.

The evaluation set is the artefact that makes future change safe.

93 Who should be on an AI project team?Medium

Someone who owns the business decision, someone who knows the data, a platform person, and a reviewer for bias and governance.

Data knowledge is the role most often missing and most often needed.

94 Data quality work is unglamorous and unfunded.Medium

Frame it as the AI project, because it is the majority of the work and the determinant of the outcome.

"We cannot do the AI you want until this data is reliable" is a fundable statement in a way that "data quality" alone is not.

95 How do you prioritise between AI use cases?Medium

Value of the decision, readiness of the data, risk if wrong, and effort — with data readiness usually the binding constraint.

Pick the one with real value and ready data, even if it is less exciting than the alternative.

96 A competitor announced an AI feature.Medium

Separate the competitive pressure from the business case, and be clear that a rushed, unmeasured feature is worse than none.

Offer the fastest genuinely valuable option rather than the fastest visible one.

97 How do you avoid AI technical debt?Hard

Prompts in source control with evaluation sets, models with owners and monitoring, a use register, and retirement of what is no longer used.

Unversioned prompts and unowned models are the two commonest forms of it.

98 What is the single biggest AI risk?Hard

Confident wrongness at scale — a system producing plausible, trusted, incorrect output faster than anyone can check it.

Grounding, human review and measurement are the three controls that address it.

99 What would you ask before any AI project?Hard

What decision changes; who will act on it; is the data good enough; what happens when it is wrong; how will we know it works; what does it cost.

Answering those six honestly prevents most AI project failures.

100 One habit that most improves AI work?Hard

Keeping an evaluation set and running it on every change — a set of real inputs with expected characteristics.

It turns prompt and model work from guesswork into engineering, and it is the discipline that most reliably separates AI features that survive from ones that are quietly switched off.
