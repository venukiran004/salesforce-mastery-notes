CRM Analytics & Advanced Reporting

When standard reports run out of road — CRM Analytics datasets and recipes, Tableau, and how to choose between all the reporting options Salesforce now offers.

Security in CRM Analytics — the part people get wrong

🔓
Sharing is NOT inherited by default A dataset is a flat extract. Unless you configure it, every user who can open the dashboard sees every row. Two options: enable sharing inheritance (mirrors Salesforce sharing, with limits on scale), or write a security predicate — a row-level filter expression evaluated per user. Always test as a restricted user before release.
# Security predicate examples (evaluated per row, per user)
'OwnerId' == "$User.Id"                                    # only my records
'Region' == "$User.Region__c"                              # match a user field
'OwnerId' == "$User.Id" || 'Manager_Id' == "$User.Id"      # mine or my team's
'IsPublic' == "true" || 'OwnerId' == "$User.Id"            # public rows plus mine

Einstein Discovery in practice

  • Point it at a dataset with a clear outcome column and it builds and evaluates models automatically.
  • Outputs: what happened, why, what's likely to happen next, and what to improve — with the top contributing factors.
  • Predictions can be surfaced on a Lightning record page, written to a field, or used in Flow.
  • It reports on model quality and highlights potential bias in sensitive variables — worth mentioning in interviews.
  • Same caveat as all predictive AI: quality depends entirely on the history you feed it. See Predictive Einstein.

Interview questions

1 When do you move a requirement from standard Reports to CRM Analytics?Medium

When you genuinely hit a wall: more than four related objects in one view, blending Salesforce with external data, tens of millions of rows, or a need for interactive faceted exploration rather than a static grouped table.

The judgement worth showing: most "we need Analytics" requests are actually solved by a custom report type, a cross filter, a joined report or a reporting snapshot — all free. Recommend the licensed product only when the free path is genuinely exhausted.

2 A user sees data in a CRM Analytics dashboard they can't see in Salesforce. Why?Hard

Because a dataset is a flat extract and does not inherit sharing by default. Unless you enabled sharing inheritance or wrote a security predicate, every dashboard viewer sees every row.

Fix: apply a predicate such as 'OwnerId' == "$User.Id", or enable sharing inheritance where the volume allows (it has row limits, with the predicate as fallback). Then re-test as a restricted user — this is a real data-leak class of bug, not a cosmetic one.

3 Dataset vs report — what's the fundamental difference?Easy

A report queries live Salesforce data at the moment you run it, and automatically respects sharing and FLS. A dataset is a pre-built, columnar snapshot refreshed on a schedule — much faster over huge volumes and able to blend sources, but the data is as old as the last refresh and security must be re-implemented.

So: reports for operational accuracy, datasets for analytical scale.

4 What is a recipe and how does it differ from a dataflow?Medium

Both are the ETL step producing a dataset. A recipe (Data Prep) is the modern visual builder — you see the data at each node, and it supports joins, aggregates, formulas, transforms, filters and output to multiple targets. A dataflow is the older JSON-defined pipeline.

New work should use recipes; you'll still meet dataflows in established orgs, and migration between them is a common project task.

5 Dashboards are slow. What do you look at?Hard

Reduce what the dataset holds: filter rows at recipe time rather than dashboard time, drop unused columns, and pre-aggregate where detail isn't needed. Then the dashboard itself: fewer widgets per page, avoid many independent queries where one faceted query would do, use bindings deliberately, and check for high-cardinality groupings.

Also verify the refresh schedule isn't overlapping with peak usage, and that a security predicate isn't doing expensive per-row work.

6 Where does Tableau fit versus CRM Analytics?Medium

CRM Analytics lives inside Salesforce, is licensed per Salesforce user, and is strongest when the audience is CRM users who need analytics embedded in their workflow — on a record page, with actionability back into Salesforce.

Tableau is a full BI platform for the whole company across every data source, with the strongest visualisation capability and its own governance. Choose Tableau when the audience extends well beyond Salesforce users; choose CRM Analytics when insight must sit next to the record and drive an action.

7 What is Einstein Discovery and what's its main risk?Hard

Automated model building over a dataset: it finds what drives an outcome, predicts future values, and recommends improvements — surfacable on a record page or in Flow.

The risk is the same as all predictive AI: it learns from historical data, so it will faithfully reproduce historical bias and any leakage (a field only populated after the outcome is known will look like a perfect predictor). Discovery flags model quality and sensitive variables — read those reports rather than shipping the first model that scores well.

Quick check

1. By default, does a CRM Analytics dataset respect Salesforce record sharing?
A dataset is a flat extract. Without a predicate, every dashboard viewer sees every row — a genuine data-leak risk.
2. The business needs a view joining 6 related objects. First thing you try?
Custom report types cap at 4 objects, but joined reports, cross filters and snapshots solve many multi-object requirements for free.
3. What is a "lens" in CRM Analytics?
You explore in a lens, then save the resulting query as a dashboard widget.
