Marketing & Pardot — 100 Scenarios

Connector sync behaviour, Contact Key decisions you cannot undo, journeys and re-entry, deliverability and DMARC alignment, consent architecture, and attribution that survives scrutiny.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Marketing & Pardot and its Advanced section.

Product choice & the connector (1–20)

1 Account Engagement or Marketing Cloud Engagement?Medium

Account Engagement (Pardot) for B2B: prospect nurture, scoring and grading, tight CRM integration, modest volumes.

Marketing Cloud Engagement for B2C scale, cross-channel journeys and complex data-driven personalisation — with a far larger implementation cost.

2 A value keeps reverting after someone edits it.Hard

Connector field sync behaviour. Each field is configured as Salesforce-wins, Account Engagement-wins, or most-recently-updated-wins.

Leaving it at the default and then debugging this is a rite of passage. Set it per field, deliberately, and document it.

3 A prospect synced to the wrong Contact.Medium

Matching is by email, so duplicate emails in CRM make it ambiguous.

Which makes duplicate management a marketing prerequisite rather than hygiene — fix the duplicates before blaming the connector.

4 A change took twenty minutes to appear.Easy

The sync queues rather than applying instantly. That is normal.

So any automation assuming immediate sync will misfire — design for the delay rather than against it.

5 Score and grade are being used interchangeably.Medium

Score is engagement — behavioural, accumulating. Grade is fit — profile-based, a letter.

"Highly engaged but a poor fit" is the distinction sales actually needs. Conflating them wastes sales time.

6 Sales ignore the scores entirely.Medium

Usually because the thresholds were never calibrated against actual conversions, so a "hot" lead is not hot.

Analyse historical conversion by score band and set thresholds from the data, then review them periodically.

7 Connected Campaigns is proposed.Medium

It unifies Account Engagement campaigns with Salesforce Campaigns, and is a prerequisite for sensible attribution reporting.

Enabling it is effectively one-way, so plan it rather than switching it on to see.

8 Deleting a prospect did not delete it.Medium

Prospects go to a recycle bin, and deletion semantics differ between the two systems — which also affects consent records.

Understand the behaviour before relying on deletion for a privacy request.

9 Which records should sync?Medium

Only what marketing needs — syncing every Lead and Contact inflates volume, cost and the matching problem.

Use a marketing-relevant flag or a campaign-based scope, and review it as the database grows.

10 A custom field is not available for segmentation.Easy

It needs creating in Account Engagement and mapping to the Salesforce field — the sync is per mapped field, not automatic.

And the type must be compatible, which is the second most common cause.

11 Engagement Studio is not progressing prospects.Medium

Check the entry criteria, the wait steps, and whether a rule earlier in the program is holding them.

Also whether the prospect is suppressed or opted out, which silently stops everything downstream.

12 Completion actions fire unexpectedly.Medium

They fire on the asset's interaction (a form submit, an email click), not on a program step — so an old form keeps acting.

Audit completion actions on assets, not just the programs.

13 Marketing wants to write to Salesforce directly.Medium

Possible via the connector user, but it needs the same scrutiny as any integration: field ownership, validation rules, and the automation it triggers.

Decide field ownership explicitly or you get the reverting-value problem at scale.

14 A form is creating duplicate prospects.Medium

Different email variants, or a form not matching against existing prospects.

Normalise email at capture, and match-and-update rather than always creating — repeat enquiries should enrich, not fragment.

15 Which system owns the lead lifecycle?Hard

Decide explicitly: marketing nurtures until a threshold, then CRM owns it — with the handoff criteria written down.

Ambiguous ownership produces leads worked by both or neither, which is the commonest marketing-sales friction.

16 Sales complain leads are unqualified.Medium

The handoff threshold is wrong, or grade is being ignored in favour of score.

Agree the definition of a qualified lead with sales, encode it, and measure acceptance rate.

17 How do you test connector changes?Medium

In a sandbox with a sandbox Account Engagement instance where available — and with a small set of test prospects you control end to end.

Testing sync behaviour in production is how real prospects receive test emails.

18 Volume is growing and costs are rising.Medium

Mailable prospect counts and sends drive cost. Suppress and archive unengaged prospects rather than carrying them indefinitely.

It also improves deliverability, so it is two benefits for one decision.

19 Marketing Cloud Growth is suggested instead.Medium

Packaging in this area changes frequently, so verify current capability and licensing against your actual requirements rather than a comparison written last year.

The architectural questions — identity, consent, data ownership — matter more than the product name.

20 What is the commonest connector mistake?Hard

Not setting field sync behaviour deliberately, so values revert unpredictably and nobody can explain it.

Followed by syncing everything, and by duplicate emails in CRM making the match ambiguous.

Marketing Cloud data model (21–42)

21 What is the most consequential early decision?Hard

The Contact Key — the immutable identifier across the whole platform. Every journey, data extension relationship, suppression list and send log keys off it.

Choosing an email address is close to unrecoverable at scale. Use a stable system identifier.

22 The Contact Key was set to email and must change.Hard

Re-keying the estate means rebuilding journeys, relationships and suppression logic — in practice, a rebuild.

Which is why it needs settling with the CRM team on day one, not discovered in year two.

23 A data extension import keeps appending duplicates.Medium

No primary key on the data extension, so imports insert rather than update.

Set the key, and the same import then behaves as an upsert — which is almost always what was intended.

24 A data extension is not usable for sending.Easy

It must be sendable, with a relationship to the subscriber established.

That relationship in Contact Builder is also what makes its attributes available in journeys and content.

25 Data extensions are growing without limit.Medium

No retention policy. Unmanaged extensions grow forever, slow every query and eventually cost real money.

Set retention per extension at creation, which is far easier than retrofitting it.

26 All Contacts and All Subscribers differ.Hard

Two overlapping populations with different deletion semantics — and contact deletion is a long-running, partially irreversible process.

Understand it before you need it, particularly for privacy requests.

27 Personalisation renders "Hi ,".Easy

A null first name with no default. Always provide a fallback in the personalisation string.

It erodes trust faster than sending nothing, and it is entirely preventable.

28 A send is extremely slow.Medium

Per-recipient work: AMPscript lookups inside the email, complex dynamic content, or large exclusion queries.

Pre-compute into the sending data extension so the send reads rather than looks up.

29 AMPscript, SSJS or GTL?Medium

AMPscript for personalisation and lookups; SSJS for logic and API calls; GTL for performance-critical templated content.

Lookups are the expensive part regardless of language — minimise them rather than optimising the syntax.

30 A query activity is timing out.Medium

Volume and joins. Narrow the source, filter earlier, and index-align where the platform allows.

Also check whether the query is doing work that belongs upstream in the data feed.

31 You need data from Salesforce in Marketing Cloud.Medium

Marketing Cloud Connect for synchronised data extensions, or an integration writing to data extensions directly.

Decide which system owns each attribute, or you get the reverting-value problem in a second place.

32 Suppression is not working.Hard

Check the exclusion script, the suppression list, the publication list status, and the contact's opt-out state — suppression has several layers.

Test with a known-suppressed address before every significant send.

33 Someone received an email after unsubscribing.Hard

A serious issue. Likely a send using a data extension that bypasses the publication list, or an opt-out recorded in one system and not the other.

Fix the send logic, then fix the consent architecture so one opt-out reaches every channel.

34 How do you test a send safely?Medium

A test send to a controlled list, with suppression verified, in a non-production business unit where available.

And a send-size sanity check: an unintended 200,000-recipient send is not recallable.

35 Business units are being proposed.Medium

They partition data, permissions and sending — useful for brands, regions or agencies, and they add real complexity.

Shared data and suppression across units needs deliberate design rather than assumption.

36 An automation failed overnight.Medium

Check the activity that failed in Automation Studio — commonly a file import that did not arrive, or a query error.

And alert on failure and absence, because an automation that silently stops leaves yesterday's segment looking current.

37 A file-based feed did not arrive.Medium

Build in a manifest or row-count check so a missing or truncated file is detected rather than processed as complete.

File integrations fail silently more than any other kind, and a partial import corrupts the segment.

38 How do you handle preference management?Medium

Granular preferences (newsletter, product updates, events) retain more subscribers than a single global unsubscribe — and regulators expect them.

Keep the preference centre writing to the system of record, not to a local copy.

39 Einstein engagement scoring is suggested.Medium

Useful for send-time optimisation and engagement prediction if you have enough history and clean data.

On thin data it produces confident recommendations nobody should follow.

40 Marketing data volume is unmanageable.Medium

Retention policies per data extension, archiving send logs, and suppressing long-unengaged contacts.

Also question whether every attribute needs to be in the platform, or whether it can stay in the warehouse and be queried when needed.

41 Two business units send to the same person.Medium

Without shared suppression and a frequency policy, the customer receives both — which reads as organisational incompetence.

Implement a global frequency cap and shared suppression, which is a design decision rather than a feature.

42 What is the commonest Marketing Cloud mistake?Hard

The Contact Key decision, followed by data extensions without primary keys or retention, and suppression logic that a single send can bypass.

All three are expensive to correct once journeys depend on them.

Journeys & automation (43–62)

43 A customer received the welcome series three times.Medium

Re-entry mode: no re-entry, re-entry anytime, or re-entry after exit.

"Re-entry anytime" plus an entry event that fires repeatedly is exactly this outcome.

44 You need to fix a running journey.Hard

Running journeys cannot be meaningfully edited — you create a new version, and contacts already in flight stay in the old one.

Plan for two versions running concurrently, and for how you will report across both.

45 Contacts are stuck in a journey.Medium

A wait activity — a 30-day wait means they sit there for a month, and your edit cannot reach them.

Which is a reason to keep waits short and use re-entry rather than long holds where possible.

46 Someone who purchased is still being nurtured.Medium

No exit criteria. They are not automatic, and without them the journey continues regardless.

Define exit conditions for every journey — it is the most commonly omitted configuration.

47 Entry is too slow for a transactional message.Medium

Event-driven entry evaluates on the event; scheduled entry runs on a cadence.

Anything needing a response within minutes needs the former — and a transactional send may be more appropriate than a journey entirely.

48 A journey behaves differently for some contacts.Medium

Missing attributes taking a default decision path, or data arriving after entry so the decision evaluates nulls.

Ensure the entry data is complete at entry, and test with records that have gaps on purpose.

49 How do you test a journey?Medium

With real data shapes — including nulls, odd characters and edge-case values — and a controlled test population you can follow all the way through.

Journey testing is mostly about the decision paths, which is where unexpected behaviour lives.

50 A journey sends at 3am local time.Medium

Timezone handling on the send, and whether you are using send-time optimisation or a fixed schedule.

For a global audience, this is a design decision rather than an oversight.

51 You need to pause a journey urgently.Medium

Journeys can be paused, and you should know how before you need to — including what happens to contacts mid-flight when you resume.

Rehearse it. An incident is not the time to discover the semantics.

52 Reporting across journey versions is confusing.Medium

Each version reports separately, so metrics fragment every time you publish a new one.

Decide how you will aggregate before you need the number, or accept that a mid-campaign change breaks the trend.

53 An automation and a journey both act on the same contacts.Medium

Likely duplicate sends or conflicting updates. Establish which owns the contact at each stage.

Overlapping marketing automation is as damaging as overlapping CRM automation, and harder to see because the effects are external.

54 How many journeys is too many?Medium

When nobody can say which journeys a given contact is in, or when frequency capping becomes guesswork.

Maintain an inventory with owners and purposes, and retire journeys that have served their campaign.

55 A contact should never receive more than two emails a week.Hard

A frequency cap — implemented centrally (shared suppression plus send logs), not journey by journey.

Per-journey logic cannot see other journeys, which is why the cap has to be a platform-level design.

56 Engagement Studio versus Journey Builder.Easy

Different products: Engagement Studio in Account Engagement for B2B nurture; Journey Builder in Marketing Cloud for cross-channel orchestration.

They are not interchangeable, and the choice follows from the product decision.

57 A journey needs CRM data mid-flight.Medium

A data extension updated by integration, or an API call activity — and remember the contact is already in flight, so late data may miss its decision.

Fetch what you need at entry where the decision depends on it.

58 Journey decisions use stale data.Medium

Entry data is captured at entry; later changes are not reflected unless you explicitly re-read it.

For long journeys, add a decision that re-checks current state rather than trusting the entry snapshot.

59 How do you handle a failed send inside a journey?Medium

Bounces and errors surface in tracking and send logs — but the journey continues unless you branch on it.

Add handling for hard bounces specifically, since continuing to send to them damages deliverability.

60 Marketing wants a journey for everything.Medium

Journeys are for multi-step, time-based orchestration. A single triggered email is a transactional send or an automation.

Using journeys for one-step sends makes the estate harder to reason about and frequency harder to control.

61 How do you document a journey?Easy

Purpose, entry source and criteria, re-entry mode, exit criteria, the decision logic, and the owner.

Re-entry and exit are the two that cause incidents, so they belong at the top of the description.

62 What is the commonest journey mistake?Hard

Re-entry mode left permissive, and no exit criteria — which together produce repeated sends to people who have already converted.

Both are configuration defaults rather than oversights, which is why they recur across implementations.

Deliverability & consent (63–82)

63 Emails are landing in spam despite SPF, DKIM and DMARC.Hard

Alignment. DMARC requires SPF or DKIM to align with the From domain, and all three can be configured while alignment still fails.

Check the DMARC reports — that is what they are for, and most teams never read them.

64 What does each of SPF, DKIM and DMARC do?Medium

SPF authorises sending IPs for the domain; DKIM signs the message so it is verifiably unaltered; DMARC tells receivers what to do when SPF or DKIM fails, and provides reporting.

DMARC without alignment is the configuration that looks complete and is not.

65 SPF is failing intermittently.Medium

Often the DNS lookup limit — too many includes in the SPF record cause soft failures.

Flatten or consolidate the record, and audit which senders genuinely still need authorising.

66 A new dedicated IP is performing badly.Medium

It has no reputation. Warm it gradually, starting with your most engaged recipients and increasing volume.

Blasting a cold IP with a large send gets you throttled or blocked, and recovery takes weeks.

67 Deliverability degraded after a list purchase.Hard

Unengaged and invalid addresses damage reputation quickly, and complaints compound it.

Purchased lists are a reliable way to harm deliverability for your legitimate mail — and in many jurisdictions a consent problem too.

68 Engagement is the dominant reputation signal now.Medium

So sending to years-old unengaged addresses damages deliverability for everyone on the IP.

Suppress long-unengaged contacts. It reduces volume, improves deliverability and cuts cost simultaneously.

69 Hard bounces are still being sent to.Easy

They must suppress immediately — continuing is the fastest route to a blocklist.

Verify the suppression is actually applying, since a send using a custom data extension can bypass it.

70 Complaint rates are rising.Medium

Frequency, relevance, or an unsubscribe that is hard to find. Check all three before blaming the content.

Feedback loops surface complaints — make sure they are being consumed and acted on.

71 Where does consent live?Hard

Nominate one system of record per channel and sync outward — Salesforce's Individual and consent fields, Account Engagement opt-out, Marketing Cloud subscriber status.

A preference centre updating one and not the others is a regulatory finding waiting to be found.

72 What proof of consent do you need?Medium

Timestamp, source, IP, and the exact wording shown.

"They ticked a box in 2021" without the wording is not evidence, and the wording is the part nobody retains.

73 Double opt-in is proposed.Medium

It costs list size and buys deliverability and defensibility — and for B2C in strict jurisdictions it is usually the right trade.

Measure the drop-off so the decision is informed rather than assumed.

74 A customer asks to be forgotten.Hard

Deletion must reach data extensions, send logs, suppression lists, journeys, CRM and any downstream system — and contact deletion in Marketing Cloud is long-running and partially irreversible.

Build it as a documented process with verification, not an ad-hoc deletion.

75 Unsubscribes are not honoured across brands.Hard

Business-unit-scoped opt-outs. Decide whether an unsubscribe is brand-specific or global — and make sure the preference centre says which.

Customers generally expect global unless told otherwise, and regulators tend to agree.

76 A test send went to real customers.Hard

Treat it as an incident: assess who received it, communicate if required, and fix the process.

Prevention is a non-production business unit, a controlled test list, and a send-size sanity check before every send.

77 How do you monitor deliverability?Medium

Delivery rate, bounce rate by type, complaint rate, and engagement by domain — plus the DMARC reports.

Watch per-domain: a problem with one major provider is invisible in an aggregate number.

78 Transactional and marketing mail share a domain.Medium

Risky: marketing complaints can affect transactional delivery, which is the mail you cannot afford to lose.

Separate subdomains (and ideally IPs) for transactional and marketing streams.

79 Legal asks who can be emailed.Medium

Produce the consent model: the system of record per channel, the proof retained, the suppression mechanisms, and how a request is honoured.

If you cannot produce that, the answer to their question is "we are not sure".

80 Marketing wants to email everyone in CRM.Medium

Consent is the constraint, not capability. Existing CRM contacts are not automatically a marketable audience.

Establish lawful basis per contact before the send — retrospective justification is not available.

81 A preference centre is being built.Medium

It must write to the system of record and propagate outward, offer granular choices, and be reachable from every email.

And it is a public endpoint, so it needs the same validation and rate-limiting scrutiny as any form.

82 What is the commonest consent failure?Hard

Consent recorded in one system and not synced, so an opt-out in the preference centre does not stop a send from a different platform.

It is a design problem rather than a bug, which is why it survives until someone complains.

Attribution & judgement (83–100)

83 Marketing and sales disagree about attribution.Hard

Agree the model and the influence window in writing before building any dashboard.

Without those, every ROI report becomes a debate about the numbers rather than a decision from them.

84 First touch or last touch?Medium

First touch judges top-of-funnel demand generation; last touch judges closing content and enablement.

They answer different questions, so reporting both is usually more honest than choosing one.

85 Campaign Influence shows nothing.Medium

It needs campaign members, an influence time window, and a model — all three configured.

And Connected Campaigns if you want Account Engagement activity to participate properly.

86 A custom attribution model is requested.Medium

Fine, with the weights written down and the influence window stated — otherwise it is unauditable and will be re-litigated.

Even distribution is a defensible default when nobody can agree.

87 ROI numbers look implausible.Medium

Check the influence window (a 12-month window attributes almost everything to almost everything) and whether costs are included at all.

Attribution without cost data is revenue reporting dressed as ROI.

88 How do you measure a nurture program?Medium

Progression through the funnel for the nurtured cohort versus a comparable one — not open and click rates, which measure activity.

Engagement metrics are diagnostic; conversion is the outcome.

89 Open rates have become unreliable.Medium

Privacy features inflate opens by pre-fetching images, so opens are a weak signal.

Shift to clicks, conversions and downstream pipeline — and stop using opens as a success measure.

90 Marketing reports more leads than sales received.Medium

Definition and handoff: marketing counts captures, sales counts accepted leads, and the gap is qualification and routing.

Measure the funnel with both numbers and the acceptance rate between them — the gap is the useful metric.

91 How do you prove marketing's contribution?Hard

An agreed model, consistent reporting, and ideally a holdout group for the campaigns where that is feasible.

Holdouts are the only genuinely convincing evidence, and they are rarely offered because they cost reach.

92 Data quality is undermining segmentation.Medium

Segmentation can only be as good as the attributes. Measure completeness of the fields you segment on, and fix capture at source.

Enriching downstream papers over a capture problem that will keep recurring.

93 Who should own the marketing database?Medium

A named owner for data quality, consent, suppression and retention — spanning marketing and CRM, because the data does.

Split ownership between teams is how opt-outs get lost and duplicates accumulate.

94 Data Cloud is proposed for marketing.Medium

Valuable for unified profiles, identity resolution and segment activation across channels — particularly where customer data is fragmented.

And identity resolution is a business decision with real over-merge and under-merge consequences. See Data Cloud.

95 A campaign needs to launch tomorrow.Medium

The non-negotiables are suppression, consent and a send-size check. Everything else can be simplified.

Shortcuts on those three produce incidents that outlast the campaign.

96 How do you hand over a marketing implementation?Medium

The Contact Key definition, the field sync behaviour matrix, the consent model, the suppression mechanisms, the journey inventory with re-entry and exit, and the retention policies.

Those six are what make the estate safe to change.

97 Marketing and CRM teams do not talk.Medium

That shows up as field ownership conflicts, duplicate records, lost opt-outs and attribution disputes.

The technical problems are symptoms. The field-level ownership matrix is a good artefact to force the conversation.

98 Scoring thresholds have never been reviewed.Medium

Then they reflect assumptions from implementation, not current conversion behaviour.

Review quarterly against actual outcomes — scoring that nobody calibrates is scoring sales learn to ignore.

99 What is the commonest marketing-technology mistake?Hard

Consent scattered across systems with no system of record — so an opt-out is honoured in one channel and not another.

Followed by the Contact Key decision, and by field sync behaviour left at defaults.

100 One habit that most improves marketing work?Hard

Deciding one system of record per channel for consent — and testing that an opt-out there stops every send.

It is the control with the highest regulatory and reputational stakes, it is cheap to verify, and it is the one most often assumed rather than checked.
