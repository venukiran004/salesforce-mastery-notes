Identity & SSO — 100 Scenarios

SAML and OpenID Connect end to end, the OAuth flow decision, Connected Apps and scopes, JWT service integrations, MFA and session policy, and how to debug a login that will not work.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Identity & SSO and its Advanced section.

SAML & SSO (1–22)

1 Explain SSO to a non-technical stakeholder.Easy

A trusted system vouches for who you are, so you sign in once instead of holding a password per application.

The value is fewer passwords, central control of who has access, and one place to revoke it.

2 Is Salesforce the IdP or the SP here?Medium

Decide which system owns identity. If the corporate directory does, Salesforce is the service provider and SAML or OIDC flows in. If Salesforce owns customer identity, it is the identity provider for other apps.

Getting this backwards is the commonest design error, and it invalidates the rest of the configuration.

3 Walk through a SAML login.Medium

User hits Salesforce → redirected to the IdP → authenticates → IdP posts a signed assertion back → Salesforce validates the signature against the uploaded certificate, checks issuer, audience, recipient and timestamps, then maps the subject to a user via Federation ID or username.

Every failure mode lives in one of those checks.

4 SP-initiated or IdP-initiated?Medium

SP-initiated starts at Salesforce and is what you want for deep links, because RelayState preserves the destination. IdP-initiated starts at the portal tile.

Support both — users arrive from both directions, and deep links breaking is a common complaint.

5 What does RelayState do?Medium

Carries the intended destination through the SAML round trip, so a user following a link to a record lands on that record rather than the home page.

Which is exactly the behaviour people report as "SSO loses my link".

6 Federation ID or username for mapping?Medium

Federation ID — a stable identifier that survives email changes. Mapping on username ties identity to an address that changes.

Populate it consistently at provisioning; a blank Federation ID is a login failure waiting to happen.

7 A user gets "assertion invalid".Hard

Decode the assertion with the SAML Assertion Validator and check each field: issuer, audience, recipient, subject, conditions, signature and timestamps.

The error is deliberately vague; the validator tells you which check failed.

8 It worked yesterday and fails today for everyone.Hard

Almost always a certificate expiry — the signing certificate rotated or lapsed.

Which is why certificate expiry dates belong in a monitored calendar with an owner. This is the single most common SSO outage.

9 One user fails and everyone else is fine.Medium

User-specific: missing or wrong Federation ID, inactive user, no licence, or a profile-level login restriction. Not the trust configuration.

Scope first — all users versus one user splits the problem immediately.

10 Login fails only from outside the office.Medium

IP restrictions on the profile or org-wide trusted ranges, or network policy on the IdP side.

The split by location is the clue — it tells you to look at restrictions rather than trust.

11 Users are redirected in a loop.Hard

Typically a mismatch between the configured Salesforce login URL and what the IdP posts to, or a session that cannot be established.

Compare the entity ID, ACS URL and login URL character by character — these are copy-paste errors.

12 You are locked out by your own SSO configuration.Hard

Prevention: keep a break-glass admin that bypasses SSO, with its credentials stored securely and its use monitored.

Without one, recovery means Salesforce Support. Configure the break-glass account before enabling SSO.

13 Should "My Domain" be configured?Easy

Yes — it is a prerequisite for most identity features and gives you branded login URLs and login-page customisation.

It is now mandatory in practice, so treat it as a first step.

14 Users should be created automatically on first login.Medium

Just-in-Time provisioning — the assertion carries attributes and Salesforce creates or updates the user.

It removes manual provisioning, and it needs the attribute contract agreed with the IdP team and a decision about which fields JIT owns.

15 JIT is overwriting manual profile changes.Medium

Expected — JIT asserts the attributes on each login, so whatever it sends wins.

Decide which fields JIT owns and manage those only in the IdP; stop editing them in Salesforce.

16 Deprovisioning needs handling.Hard

SSO only controls authentication — a disabled directory account cannot log in, but the Salesforce user is still active and still consuming a licence.

Use SCIM or an identity platform for lifecycle. This gap is a standard audit finding.

17 What is SCIM for?Medium

Standards-based user lifecycle — create, update, deactivate — pushed from the identity platform, which closes the deprovisioning gap.

SSO plus SCIM together give you authentication and lifecycle; SSO alone gives you only authentication.

18 Several IdPs are needed for different populations.Hard

Multiple SAML configurations with discovery logic, or an identity platform fronting them all so Salesforce trusts one IdP.

The second is usually simpler to operate, especially after an acquisition.

19 Single logout is requested.Hard

Supported, and genuinely harder than SSO — every participating application must handle the logout request, and in practice some do not.

Set expectations: a session may survive somewhere, which matters for shared devices.

20 How do you test an SSO change safely?Medium

In a sandbox with its own IdP configuration, then in production with the break-glass account available and a rollback plan.

And verify deep links, mobile and API access — not just the browser login page.

21 Mobile app login breaks after enabling SSO.Medium

The mobile app uses OAuth, which can delegate to SSO — check the Connected App policy and whether the login flow renders in the embedded browser.

Mobile and API paths need testing separately from the desktop browser.

22 What do you check first on any SSO failure?Hard

Scope: all users or one user. All users means certificate or trust configuration; one user means Federation ID, licence or restriction.

Then the Login History status and the decoded assertion. That sequence resolves most cases in minutes.

OAuth flows (23–48)

23 A user-facing web app needs Salesforce access.Medium

Web server flow with PKCE — the user authenticates, the app exchanges a code for tokens, and the refresh token is stored server side.

PKCE protects the code exchange and should be considered default.

24 A server-to-server integration needs access with no user.Medium

JWT bearer flow — a certificate-signed assertion, no stored password, no interactive login.

It is the right default for system integrations and it is what auditors want to see.

25 An integration uses username-password flow.Hard

Migrate it to JWT. It stores a password and security token, breaks on password rotation and MFA, and is now restricted by default.

It is the single most common legacy integration finding, and JWT is a drop-in replacement for the same use case.

26 A single-page app needs to call Salesforce.Hard

Web server flow with PKCE, not implicit — implicit is deprecated and exposes tokens in the URL.

A browser cannot keep a secret, so treat the client as public and never embed a client secret.

27 A CLI tool needs to authenticate on a headless box.Medium

Device flow — the user authorises on another device with a code.

For unattended automation use JWT instead; device flow still needs a human once.

28 What is PKCE protecting against?Hard

Interception of the authorisation code — the client proves it is the same party that started the flow via a verifier and challenge.

Essential for public clients, and good practice for confidential ones too.

29 How long does an access token last?Medium

Until the session timeout configured in the Connected App or org session settings — it is policy-driven, not a fixed constant.

So an integration must handle expiry and refresh rather than assuming a duration.

30 An integration stops working after a few hours.Medium

Access token expired and the code does not refresh. Implement refresh-on-401 with a single retry, or use JWT and request a fresh token per batch.

Caching a token forever is a classic integration bug.

31 A refresh token stopped working.Medium

Revoked, expired per policy, the user was deactivated, their password changed, or the Connected App policy changed.

Handle it as a re-authentication requirement rather than retrying — retrying a dead refresh token never succeeds.

32 Where should tokens be stored?Hard

Server side in a secret manager — never in source, never in client-side storage, never in a custom field without encryption.

In Apex, Named Credentials hold them so your code never does.

33 How should Apex call an external API?Medium

Named Credentials with External Credentials — the platform manages the token, refresh and headers, and no secret appears in code.

Hand-rolled OAuth in Apex is a maintenance and security liability for no benefit.

34 What scopes should a Connected App request?Medium

The minimum. full is almost never justified; api plus refresh_token covers most integrations.

Scopes are the blast radius if the credential leaks, which is the argument for being strict.

35 How do you restrict who can use a Connected App?Medium

Set it to admin-approved users only and grant via profile or permission set, rather than letting any user self-authorise.

Plus IP relaxation and session policies per app, which are independently configurable.

36 An integration user has broad access.Hard

Give it a dedicated profile with least privilege, a restricted IP range, JWT authentication and no UI login — then audit what it actually touches.

Integration users accumulate permissions because nobody reviews them, and they are high-value targets.

37 A credential may have leaked.Hard

Revoke the token and rotate the credential immediately, then assess what was accessible using Event Monitoring and Login History.

Rotation first — assessment can follow, but every minute of exposure counts.

38 How do you rotate a JWT certificate?Hard

Upload the new certificate, update the Connected App, deploy the new key to the client, verify, then remove the old one.

Plan the overlap so there is no outage, and put the expiry in a monitored calendar.

39 Can you see which apps have access?Medium

Connected Apps OAuth Usage shows apps and user counts, and tokens can be revoked from there.

Review it periodically — self-authorised apps nobody sanctioned are a common finding.

40 A partner wants API access to your org.Hard

A dedicated Connected App with minimum scopes, a least-privilege integration user, JWT or web server flow, IP restrictions where feasible, and monitoring of their usage.

Plus an agreed process to revoke when the relationship ends.

41 What is an External Client App?Medium

The newer, metadata-deployable packaging for OAuth clients, with clearer separation between the app and its policies.

Prefer it for new work since it fits source control; Connected Apps remain for existing.

42 API calls return INVALID_SESSION_ID.Medium

The token is expired or invalid, or you are calling the wrong instance URL — always use the instance URL returned with the token.

Hard-coded instance URLs break on org migration, which produces exactly this error.

43 An OAuth call returns invalid_grant.Hard

Deliberately generic: wrong credentials, wrong or expired certificate, clock skew on JWT, a user not pre-authorised for the app, or the user lacking the Connected App grant.

Check clock skew and pre-authorisation — those two account for most JWT cases.

44 How do you test OAuth flows?Medium

Postman or curl against a sandbox, exercising the full lifecycle — initial grant, expiry, refresh, revocation — not just the happy path.

See Postman scenarios for the mechanics.

45 Which flow for a mobile app?Medium

Web server flow with PKCE in the system browser, with the refresh token in the platform's secure storage.

Never embed a client secret in a distributed app — anyone can extract it.

46 A legacy system can only do basic auth.Hard

Front it with a middleware layer that holds the OAuth credential and exposes what the legacy system can consume.

Which keeps the password out of Salesforce, and names the remediation owner explicitly.

47 How many integration users should there be?Medium

One per integration, so permissions are scoped to that integration and audit trails attribute actions correctly.

A shared integration user makes both least privilege and forensics impossible.

48 What is the commonest OAuth mistake?Hard

Using username-password flow for system integrations when JWT is the correct answer.

Followed by over-broad scopes, hard-coded instance URLs, and never handling token expiry.

MFA, sessions & policy (49–74)

49 Is MFA optional?Easy

No — it is contractually required for direct Salesforce logins, and satisfied either by Salesforce MFA or by MFA at the IdP.

If SSO carries it, document that so an auditor can see where it is enforced.

50 SSO is enabled. Is MFA still needed?Medium

It must be enforced somewhere — typically at the IdP, which satisfies the requirement.

But check the paths that bypass SSO: the break-glass admin, API-only users and any direct login URL.

51 Users resist MFA.Medium

Reduce friction rather than exceptions: authenticator apps over SMS, trusted-device remembering within policy, and clear enrolment support.

Exceptions become permanent, so prefer friction reduction over carve-outs.

52 Should SMS be used as a factor?Medium

It is weaker than an authenticator app or security key because of SIM-swap attacks — acceptable as a fallback, not as the default.

Prefer app-based or hardware factors for privileged accounts.

53 A user lost their MFA device.Medium

An identity-verified recovery process — admin-generated temporary code after verifying who they are, out of band.

Social-engineering MFA resets is a known attack, so verification must not rely on the requester's own email.

54 A privileged action needs extra assurance.Hard

High-assurance sessions — require a stronger factor for sensitive operations, configured via session security levels and enforced on specific actions.

So a routine session cannot perform a sensitive action without re-verifying.

55 How long should sessions last?Medium

Short enough to limit exposure on an unattended device, long enough that people are not re-authenticating all day — differentiated by profile.

Privileged profiles get shorter timeouts; a shared-device population shorter still.

56 Sessions should be locked to the originating IP.Medium

"Lock sessions to the IP address from which they originated" does that, and it breaks on mobile networks that change IP.

Which is why it suits fixed-location populations and frustrates field users.

57 Login hours are requested for contractors.Easy

Profile login hours restrict when they can log in, and sessions are terminated outside those hours.

Reasonable for contractors; be careful with timezones and anyone on call.

58 IP ranges should restrict access.Medium

Profile IP ranges restrict access outright; org-wide trusted ranges skip verification but still allow access.

They are different controls and are routinely confused — be clear which behaviour you want.

59 Login Flows — what are they for?Medium

Injecting logic into the login process: terms acceptance, extra verification, risk-based steps or profile completion.

Test carefully — a broken login flow locks out the population it applies to.

60 Risk-based authentication is wanted.Hard

Login Flows with risk signals, or an identity platform that does adaptive authentication and hands Salesforce the result.

The platform route is usually better, because the signals live where the identity does.

61 Who should hold "Modify All Data"?Hard

Almost nobody, permanently. Prefer time-bound elevation with an approval and an audit trail.

Standing god-mode access is the finding; just-in-time elevation is the answer.

62 How do you monitor suspicious logins?Medium

Login History for status, IP, location and flow, plus Event Monitoring and Transaction Security Policies to alert or block in real time.

Login History alone is retrospective; policies act at the moment.

63 Impossible-travel logins are detected.Hard

Treat as potential compromise: verify with the user, revoke sessions and tokens, force a credential reset, and review what was accessed via Event Monitoring.

A VPN explains many cases — verify rather than assume.

64 An employee left and still has access.Hard

Deactivate the user, revoke OAuth tokens and sessions, and check for connected apps authorised under their account.

SSO alone does not deactivate the Salesforce user — that gap is why offboarding needs an explicit step.

65 Should API access be restricted by default?Medium

Yes — "API Enabled" is a permission, and most users do not need it.

Restricting it reduces the surface for credential-based data extraction considerably.

66 A data export was performed by a user account.Hard

Investigate with Event Monitoring — volume, objects, timing — and consider Transaction Security Policies to block or alert on large exports.

Also review who holds export and report-export permissions.

67 How do you secure the break-glass account?Hard

Credentials in a vault with split access, MFA with a hardware factor, login alerting on any use, and a documented procedure for when it is justified.

It bypasses SSO by design, so its monitoring must be louder.

68 Shield Platform Encryption or Classic Encryption?Hard

Shield preserves far more functionality and supports more field types; Classic is limited and breaks more. Both impose functional trade-offs — encrypted fields are restricted in filtering, sorting and some operations.

Encrypt what genuinely needs it, and test the trade-offs on real reports.

69 What does encryption not protect against?Hard

A user with legitimate access — encryption protects data at rest, not over-permissioned access.

Which is why permission hygiene matters more than encryption for the most common breach pattern.

70 How do you approach a permissions audit?Hard

Start with the dangerous permissions — View All Data, Modify All Data, Manage Users, API Enabled, export rights — and ask who holds each and why.

Then sharing: OWD, rules, and anything running without sharing.

71 Permission sets or profiles?Medium

Minimal profiles plus permission sets and permission set groups — additive, composable and far easier to audit.

Profile-heavy models proliferate near-identical profiles nobody can compare.

72 Access must be granted temporarily.Medium

Permission set expiration, or an approval-driven process that grants and removes on a schedule.

Manual temporary grants become permanent, which is the whole problem being solved.

73 How do you prove compliance with an identity standard?Hard

Evidence: where MFA is enforced and the bypass paths, session and password policies, the dangerous-permission holder list with justifications, the deprovisioning process, and certificate rotation records.

Produce it as artefacts rather than assertions.

74 What is the highest-value identity control?Hard

MFA everywhere, with every bypass path known and monitored — it defeats the credential-stuffing and phishing attacks that cause most real incidents.

Followed by least privilege on the dangerous permissions.

Customer identity & judgement (75–100)

75 Customers should log in with Google or Apple.Medium

Social sign-on via an Auth Provider, with a registration handler that maps the external identity to a user and contact.

The handler is a public endpoint — assign profiles and permissions server side from fixed configuration.

76 What does a registration handler do?Hard

Creates or links the user on first external login, and updates on subsequent ones.

Never trust supplied profile, role or permission values — this handler is among the most commonly exploited surfaces in Salesforce.

77 A customer has two accounts from two sign-on methods.Hard

Identity linking in the registration handler — match on verified email or another trusted identifier.

Match only on a verified attribute; matching on an unverified email is an account-takeover vector.

78 Passwordless login for customers.Medium

Email or SMS verification codes, which genuinely improve consumer conversion.

It needs reliable contact data and a recovery path for people who change their number or lose inbox access.

79 Salesforce should be the IdP for other apps.Medium

Enable it as an identity provider and configure each app as a service provider via Connected Apps.

Sensible when Salesforce owns the customer identity; less so when a dedicated identity platform already does.

80 Should Salesforce be the customer identity store?Hard

It can be — with licence implications at consumer scale and a dedicated identity platform often being a better fit for millions of users.

Model the licence cost before committing; this decision is expensive to reverse later.

81 A headless customer identity experience is wanted.Hard

Headless identity APIs support registration, login, passwordless and forgotten-password flows from your own front end.

You then own the security of those surfaces — rate limiting, enumeration resistance and verification are yours.

82 User enumeration is a concern on the login page.Hard

Keep responses identical whether or not the account exists, for login, registration and password reset.

Differential responses let an attacker build a list of valid customers, which is a reportable finding.

83 Credential stuffing is hitting the login page.Hard

Rate limiting at the edge, CAPTCHA, lockout policy, MFA, and monitoring Login History for the failure pattern.

MFA is what actually defeats it; the rest reduces noise.

84 How do you handle customer password policy?Medium

Length over complexity theatre, with lockout thresholds and no forced frequent rotation — current guidance favours length and breach checking.

And MFA or passwordless, which matters more than any password rule.

85 An acquisition brings a second IdP.Hard

Either multiple SAML configurations with discovery, or front both with an identity platform so Salesforce trusts one.

The second is usually less operational burden and easier to reason about during the merge.

86 How do you migrate from one IdP to another?Hard

Run both configurations in parallel, move populations in waves, keep the break-glass path available, and verify deep links, mobile and API at each wave.

A big-bang cutover on authentication has no graceful failure.

87 Where should identity logic live?Medium

In the identity platform where possible — it is built for lifecycle, adaptive authentication and federation across applications.

Salesforce-side logic is for what only Salesforce can do, such as mapping to users and contacts.

88 How do you document an identity architecture?Medium

Who owns identity for each population, the flows and protocols in use, the user-mapping and provisioning model, every bypass path, the certificate inventory with expiry dates, and the break-glass procedure.

The bypass paths and expiry dates are the parts most often missing.

89 A certificate expires next week and nobody noticed.Medium

Rotate it with overlap now, then build the inventory with monitored expiry dates and a named owner.

Certificate expiry is the most predictable outage in identity and therefore the least excusable one.

90 How do you test identity in a sandbox?Medium

A separate IdP configuration per sandbox, since entity IDs and URLs differ — and remember sandbox refreshes reset them.

Script or document the post-refresh identity setup, or every refresh breaks logins.

91 Identity config does not survive a sandbox refresh.Medium

Expected. Document the post-refresh steps: certificates, Auth Providers, Connected App callbacks and My Domain settings.

It is the single most common sandbox-refresh surprise.

92 How do you deploy identity configuration?Hard

Some of it is metadata-deployable — External Client Apps notably more so — and some is environment-specific by nature.

Document the manual steps in the runbook rather than rediscovering them under pressure.

93 An auditor asks about privileged access.Hard

Produce the holder list for each dangerous permission with a business justification, the elevation process, and evidence of review.

"Nobody has reviewed it" is the finding — regular review is the control.

94 A login works in browser but fails in the API.Medium

Different path: the API needs OAuth and the API Enabled permission, and may be blocked by Connected App policy or IP restriction.

Browser success tells you nothing about API access — test them separately.

95 How do you reduce identity operational load?Medium

SCIM for lifecycle, JWT instead of passwords, certificate expiry monitoring, and least-privilege defaults so access requests are rarer and smaller.

Most identity toil is deprovisioning gaps and expired certificates.

96 A vendor insists on a shared admin account.Hard

Decline, and offer a dedicated least-privilege integration user with JWT authentication per vendor.

Shared accounts destroy attribution and cannot be revoked for one party without affecting others.

97 How do you decide between SAML and OIDC?Medium

OIDC for modern applications, mobile and API-centric cases; SAML where the enterprise IdP and existing estate are built on it.

Both are well supported — compatibility with the existing estate usually decides.

98 What is the commonest identity design mistake?Hard

Not deciding clearly which system owns identity — which produces duplicate stores, inconsistent provisioning and no single place to revoke.

Followed by no break-glass account, and unmonitored certificate expiry.

99 You have one hour to review an org's identity posture.Hard

Where MFA is enforced and what bypasses it; who holds the dangerous permissions; which integrations use username-password flow; certificate expiry dates; whether deprovisioning is automated; whether a break-glass account exists.

Those six questions find nearly every real problem.

100 One habit that most improves identity work?Hard

Maintaining the bypass inventory — every path that avoids your controls, with an owner and monitoring: break-glass accounts, API-only users, legacy integrations, direct login URLs.

Controls are only as strong as the paths around them, and those paths are invisible unless you write them down.
