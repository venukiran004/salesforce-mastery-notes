Data Management & Migration

Loading, extracting, de-duplicating, archiving and protecting data — plus large data volume tuning and the privacy obligations that come with holding customer records.

How Salesforce storage works

Data storage

Most records count as a flat 2 KB regardless of how many fields you populate. Person Accounts count as 4 KB. Campaign Members count as 1 KB. Articles and email messages have their own sizes.

Allocation is typically 10 GB base plus a per-user amount, by edition.

File storage

Separate allocation for Files, Attachments, Documents and Content. Large-file problems are almost always file storage, not data storage — check both before buying more.

Big Objects

Billions of rows, outside normal data storage. Queried by a defined index only, via SOQL on the index fields or Async SOQL. No triggers, no reports (until surfaced), no standard sharing.

Use for audit trails, historical events, IoT readings — data you must keep but rarely read.

The Recycle Bin

Holds deleted records for 15 days, up to 25× your storage. DELETE is soft; emptyRecycleBin() or Data Loader "hard delete" is permanent. queryAll and ALL ROWS see soft-deleted rows.

A migration that works

Load order and the dependency chain
🔑
The External Id trick that makes relationships easy Put the legacy key on both objects. Then load children referencing the parent by its external id, not its Salesforce Id — in Data Loader you map to Account:Legacy_Id__c. No lookup pass, no Id spreadsheet, and re-running the load is safe because upsert is idempotent.

Before a big load

  • Rehearse in a Full sandbox and time it. Extrapolate honestly.
  • Disable what you can — deactivate non-essential triggers, flows, validation rules and workflow via a hierarchy custom setting scoped to the migration user, and record how to turn them back on.
  • Defer sharing calculation (Setup → Defer Sharing Calculations) for orgs with heavy sharing rules.
  • Sort by parent Id so parallel batches don't fight over the same parent row.
  • Turn off email deliverability in the sandbox so the load doesn't email real customers.
  • Load off-peak and in tranches, checking error files between them.
  • Reconcile — counts per object, sum of key numeric fields, and a sample of records checked by hand.
📧
The mistake that ends careers A sandbox refresh copies real email addresses. Loading or updating records with automation active can email thousands of real customers. Set sandbox Deliverability to "System email only" immediately after every refresh, and mask the data.

Large data volumes (LDV)

"LDV" generally means millions of rows on one object. Symptoms: slow list views and reports, non-selective query errors, timeouts, and slow sharing recalculation.

TechniqueWhat it does
Custom indexesSalesforce Support adds an index to a field you filter on constantly. Unique and External Id fields are indexed automatically.
Skinny tablesSupport-created flattened table of frequently used fields, avoiding joins. Invisible to you but transformative for reports.
Selective queriesFilter on indexed fields; avoid leading wildcards, !=, NOT IN and nulls. See query selectivity.
DivisionsPartition very large orgs so queries and reports scope to one division.
ArchivingMove cold rows to a Big Object or an external store, then delete from the transactional object.
Ownership spreadAvoid one user owning millions of records ("data skew") — it slows sharing recalculation dramatically.
Avoid lookup skewThousands of children pointing at one parent record causes record-locking contention on load.

Backup, restore & archiving

🛡️
Salesforce protects the platform, you protect the data Salesforce's infrastructure is highly available, but that does not protect you from your own bad data load, a rogue delete or a broken integration. Backup is a customer responsibility — this comes up in every architecture interview.
OptionNotes
Weekly Data ExportFree, scheduled CSV export of all data. Weekly or monthly by edition. Manual, slow restore — a last resort, not a backup strategy.
Salesforce Backup & RestorePaid native product: automated backups with selective restore.
Third-party (OwnBackup, Gearset, Spanning)Automated daily backup, metadata included, granular restore with relationship rebuilding, sandbox seeding, and compare/diff.
Data Loader exportAd-hoc snapshot before a risky change — cheap insurance, always do it.
Field History & Field Audit Trail20 tracked fields per object (18–24 months); Shield's Field Audit Trail extends retention to 10 years.

Restore is the hard part. Re-inserting records generates new Ids, so every relationship, attachment and chatter post must be re-pointed. That's why tools that preserve and rebuild relationships are worth paying for — and why you test a restore before you need one.

Data privacy & compliance

Holding personal data

  • Individual object — stores consent and privacy preferences linked to Lead/Contact/Person Account
  • Consent fields: don't process, don't market, don't track, forget this individual
  • Data Classification metadata on fields (sensitivity, compliance category)
  • Field History and Setup Audit Trail for accountability

Protecting it

  • Shield Platform Encryption — encryption at rest; note it restricts filtering and some features
  • Classic encrypted text — masks in the UI only
  • Data Mask — anonymises sandbox data after refresh (the single best control for non-prod risk)
  • Event Monitoring — who exported what, and when

Right to be forgotten: there's no single button. You must find the person across Leads, Contacts, Cases, Activities, Chatter, Files and Big Objects, then delete or anonymise, hard-delete from the Recycle Bin, and handle backups. Design for this before you're asked for it.

Advanced

The three skews, and the numbers

SkewShapeWhat breaksFix
Ownership skewOne user or queue owns a very large number of records (commonly cited threshold: ~10,000+)Every role or ownership change triggers massive sharing recalculation; record locking becomes routineSpread ownership; keep the skewed owner out of the role hierarchy or at the very top with no peers
Account data skewOne Account with a very large number of child records (~10,000+ contacts, cases, opportunities)Implicit sharing recalculation on that parent; lock contention on any child DMLSplit the mega-account; avoid "House Account" dumping grounds
Lookup skewThousands of children pointing at the same lookup parent (a "Default" picklist-as-lookup record)DML contention — concurrent inserts fight over the parent rowSpread the values, or remove the lookup in favour of a picklist/text field
🔒
Skew is the root cause behind most UNABLE_TO_LOCK_ROW tickets The error appears in an unrelated integration at 2am, so people tune the integration. The actual cause is a parent record that thousands of rows point at, or an integration user who owns a million records. Look at the shape of the data before you tune the code — and sort DML by parent Id so concurrent transactions lock in the same order.

Index selectivity in practice

Rule of thumbDetail
Standard index thresholdsA filter is index-eligible when it returns roughly under 30% of the first million rows and under ~15% beyond that, capped at around a million rows returned. Custom indexes use tighter thresholds (~10%)
The same query changes planAs data grows, a filter that was selective stops being selective and the optimiser switches to a scan. That is why a report "suddenly" times out with no code change
Two-column custom indexesAvailable via Support for common composite filters — often a better answer than two separate indexes
NullsHistorically excluded from custom indexes unless explicitly enabled, which is why WHERE Field__c = null scans. Ask Support if you need it
Formula fieldsDeterministic, single-object formulas can be indexed; anything cross-object or non-deterministic cannot
Skinny tablesSupport-created denormalised tables for heavy read patterns. Up to 100 fields, no joins, and they do not automatically include fields you add later

Bulk API 2.0 and PK chunking

# Always use an External Id for upserts — it is the idempotency guarantee.

  --file accounts.csv --external-id External_Account_Id__c \
  --wait 30 --target-org prod

# so each chunk is selective. Essential above a few million rows.

  --output-file accounts.csv --wait 30
ConcernGuidance
Serial vs parallel modeParallel is faster but causes lock contention on shared parents. Switch to serial when you see UNABLE_TO_LOCK_ROW — slower and finishes, versus faster and fails
Sort the file by parent IdGroups children of the same parent into the same chunk, dramatically reducing lock contention. The single cheapest migration optimisation there is
Disable automation during loadTriggers, flows, validation rules, workflow, roll-ups and sharing recalculation all run per row. Use a bypass switch on the load user, then run a reconciliation pass afterwards

Load orderParents before children; self-referential relationships (Account parent, Contact reports-to) need a second pass to fill the references

Big Objects and archiving

AspectDetail
What they areAppend-oriented storage for billions of rows, outside standard storage limits. Used for history, telemetry, archived transactions
The index is everythingA Big Object has a defined composite index, and you can only query along it, in order, left to right. Get it wrong at design time and the data is effectively unqueryable — and the index cannot be changed afterwards
Async SOQLFor large result sets, results are written to another object rather than returned. Synchronous SOQL works only for narrow, index-aligned queries
No triggers, limited DMLInsert and (limited) delete. No updates in the usual sense, no roll-ups, no sharing model
ReportingNot a standard reporting source. Surface it via CRM Analytics or a custom component

Archiving pattern: a scheduled batch copies closed records older than n years into a Big Object (or an external store), verifies the copy, then hard-deletes the originals. Verify before you delete, in a separate job run, and keep a reconciliation count.

Backup and recovery — the part people discover too late

  • The Weekly Export is not a backup. It is a CSV dump with no relationships preserved, no automation, and a restore measured in weeks of manual work.
  • Recycle bin limits mean a mass delete is not reliably recoverable — capacity is bounded and it purges.
  • Test the restore. A backup you have never restored is a hypothesis. Restore a subset into a sandbox quarterly and time it.
  • Know your RPO and RTO and write them down. "How much data can we lose, and how long can we be down?" drives the tool choice; working backwards from a product does not.
  • Metadata needs backup too — that is what version control is for. A data backup with no metadata to restore into is not a recovery plan.
🎯
The LDV design conversation Lead with four questions: how many rows, growing how fast; which queries must stay fast (and are their filters indexable); what the sharing shape is (skew, OWD, hierarchy depth); and what the retention rule is. Those four answers determine indexes, skinny tables, archiving and whether the data belongs in Salesforce at all — which is sometimes the right answer.

Interview questions

1 Data Import Wizard or Data Loader — how do you choose?Easy

Import Wizard for up to 50,000 records on a supported object when you want built-in duplicate matching and an in-browser experience — typical admin work. Data Loader for up to 5 million, any object, all operations including delete, hard delete and export, and because it can be scripted from the command line for scheduled jobs. Above that, Bulk API 2.0 or an ETL tool.

2 You must load 2 M Contacts under existing Accounts. Walk me through it.Hard
  1. Ensure an External Id exists on Account and Contact; map the child's parent by Account:Legacy_Id__c so no Id lookup pass is needed.
  2. Rehearse in a Full sandbox, with Deliverability set to System email only.
  3. Confirm the Contact trigger is bulk-safe — it will run in chunks of 200 regardless.
  4. Bypass non-essential automation for the migration user via a hierarchy custom setting; defer sharing calculation.
  5. Sort the file by AccountId to avoid UNABLE_TO_LOCK_ROW; use serial mode if locking persists.
  6. Load with Bulk API upsert in tranches, off-peak, checking the failure file between tranches.
  7. Reprocess failures, reconcile counts, re-enable automation, and run a Batch job to apply any skipped logic.
3 Why do blank cells in my CSV not clear the field?Medium

Because Data Loader's "Insert null values" setting is off by default, so empty values are treated as "no change" rather than "set to null". Turn it on to blank fields — but note it then applies to every mapped column, so an incomplete file will wipe data you didn't intend to touch. With the Bulk API you use the #N/A value instead. This trips up nearly every first-time migration.

4 What is data skew and why does it hurt?Hard

Three flavours. Ownership skew — one user owns hundreds of thousands of records, so any role or sharing change forces an enormous sharing recalculation. Lookup skew — tens of thousands of children point at one parent, causing record-lock contention during concurrent loads. Account data skew — an account with a very large number of child records, which slows sharing and updates.

Mitigations: spread ownership across users (or assign to an inactive user with no role, which is excluded from hierarchy recalculation), distribute lookups across more parents, and load in serial mode sorted by parent.

5 How do you make queries fast on a 20 million row object?Hard

Make the filters selective on indexed fields — Id, Name, OwnerId, CreatedDate, SystemModstamp, RecordTypeId, lookups, and anything Unique or External Id. Avoid leading wildcards, !=, NOT IN, null checks and non-deterministic formula fields.

Then escalate: ask Support for a custom index on your hot filter field, or a skinny table to eliminate joins for reports and list views. Structurally: archive cold data to a Big Object, consider Divisions, and use Batch Apex with a QueryLocator rather than trying to process it synchronously.

6 Is Salesforce backing up my data?Medium

Salesforce guarantees platform availability and disaster recovery of the service — not recovery from your mistakes. If a bad data load overwrites 100,000 records, that's yours to fix.

Options: the free Weekly Data Export (CSV, manual restore), Salesforce Backup & Restore, or a third-party tool such as OwnBackup or Gearset which preserves relationships. The critical point most candidates miss: restore is the hard part, because re-inserted records get new Ids and every relationship must be rebuilt — so you test a restore before you need one.

7 When would you use a Big Object?Hard

For very high volume data you must retain but rarely query interactively: audit history, historical events, IoT readings, archived transactions. Billions of rows, outside standard data storage.

The constraints matter: you can only query on the fields in the defined composite index (or use Async SOQL); no triggers, no flows, no standard sharing, and limited reporting. Insert with Database.insertImmediate(). If you need to filter arbitrarily or run automation, a Big Object is the wrong choice.

8 A GDPR "right to be forgotten" request arrives. What do you do?Hard

There's no single button. Locate the individual across Leads, Contacts, Person Accounts, Cases, Activities, Chatter, Files, Email Messages and Big Objects, then delete or anonymise each, empty the Recycle Bin (soft-deleted rows are still retrievable for 15 days), and address backups and any downstream systems you've integrated with.

Design for it in advance: use the Individual object for consent, classify personal-data fields with Data Classification metadata, and keep a documented, repeatable procedure. Also mask non-production copies with Data Mask so sandboxes aren't part of the problem.

Quick check

1. A data load fails intermittently with UNABLE_TO_LOCK_ROW. Best first fix?
Parallel batches touching children of the same parent contend for the parent's lock. Grouping them into the same batch removes the contention.
2. Which storage does a 5 MB PDF attachment consume?
Data and file storage are allocated and consumed separately. Storage warnings are usually about files.
3. You need to keep 10 years of audit rows but rarely read them. Where do they go?
Big Objects are built for retain-rarely-read data at billions of rows, outside standard data storage.
