Mixed Practice — 100 Scenarios

Unsorted and unsignposted, the way real questions arrive. Every topic is in here — no section heading tells you which, so you have to recognise the problem before you can solve it.

⚠️
Harder by design The other banks group questions by topic, which gives away half the answer. These are deliberately shuffled. If you can handle this page, you can handle an interview.

Set one (1–25)

1 A nightly job started failing last week. Nothing changed.Hard

Something changed — data volume crossing a limit threshold, a selectivity boundary, a new trigger firing in the same transaction, or growth in a related list.

"Nothing changed" usually means no code changed. Check the logs for which limit is now being hit and compare record counts to a month ago.

2 A user says "the system is slow".Medium

Narrow it: which page, which record, which action, what time, and is it everyone or one user. A slow record page and a slow list view are different problems.

Then look at the specific cause — component count, Apex in the page, a formula field with cross-object lookups, or row-level sharing calculation.

3 Should this be a trigger or a flow?Medium

Record-triggered flow for most automation; Apex when you need complex logic, callouts with careful handling, performance control or genuine unit testability.

And one automation per object per timing, whichever you choose — the mess comes from having both.

4 An integration is duplicating records.Hard

No idempotency. It is retrying and creating again. Use an external ID with upsert so a retry is harmless.

Any integration that can retry must be idempotent, and most that break were never designed to be.

5 50,000 records need updating from a CSV tonight.Medium

Bulk API 2.0, with automation considered: deactivate what is not needed, defer sharing recalculation if applicable, chunk sensibly and keep a pre-load extract.

And run a subset first — a dry run on 100 records finds the mapping error before it is in 50,000 rows.

6 A customer can see another customer's data in the portal.Hard

Contain immediately, then find the grant: Sharing Set match field, a sharing rule including external users, elevated Apex, a system-context flow, or External OWD left open.

Containment before diagnosis — accepting a functionality outage is the correct trade here.

7 Test coverage is 74% and deployment is blocked.Medium

Find uncovered code and test it meaningfully — the temptation is to write assertion-free tests that lift the number.

That passes the gate and leaves you with no safety net, which is a worse position than being blocked.

8 "Too many SOQL queries: 101".Easy

A query inside a loop. Query once outside with a collection, build a map, then iterate.

It is the single commonest Apex defect and it only appears once data volume grows past testing.

9 A report takes minutes to run.Medium

Non-selective filters over large volumes, formula fields evaluated per row, or a very wide date range.

Filter on indexed fields, narrow the range, and consider a reporting snapshot for the recurring case.

10 Two developers' changes overwrote each other.Medium

They deployed from separate sandboxes without merging through source control — the second deployment reverted the first.

Source control as the single source of truth is the fix; nothing else prevents it.

11 An LWC shows stale data after a save.Medium

The wire is cached. Use refreshApex, or notifyRecordUpdateAvailable for record data.

Imperative calls do not refresh wires, which is the usual reason the save appeared to work and the screen did not.

12 A formula field cannot reference what you need.Medium

Formulas cannot traverse child records or long chains. Consider a rollup, a flow-maintained field, or restructuring the relationship.

And watch the compiled-size limit on deeply nested formulas.

13 The business wants real-time sync with an ERP.Hard

Challenge "real-time" — usually near-real-time is sufficient and far cheaper. Then choose: platform events for decoupling, request-reply for genuine synchronicity, or external objects to avoid copying at all.

And define what happens when the ERP is down.

14 A validation rule fires on an integration load.Medium

The rule applies to all contexts. Either the data is genuinely invalid — in which case fix it upstream — or the rule needs to exempt the integration user.

Prefer fixing the data; exemptions accumulate until the rule protects nothing.

15 Someone needs access to one record only.Medium

Manual share or Apex managed sharing with a custom RowCause — the latter if it must survive ownership change.

Do not widen OWD or add a sharing rule for a single record.

16 A trigger runs twice per save.Medium

Workflow field updates or a process re-entering the save, or a trigger updating its own object. Add a static recursion guard.

And check whether the recursion is a symptom of automation that should be consolidated instead.

17 An Apex callout fails in a trigger.Easy

You cannot call out from a trigger synchronously. Use @future(callout=true) or Queueable — and Queueable is generally the better choice.

Also consider whether the callout belongs in the save path at all.

18 A field needs to be encrypted.Hard

Shield Platform Encryption, understanding the functional trade-offs — filtering, sorting and some operations are restricted.

And remember encryption does not protect against a user who legitimately has access, which is the more common risk.

19 A queue is not receiving cases.Medium

Assignment rules not firing (check whether the creating context runs them), rule entry order, or criteria not matching.

API and integration creates do not run assignment rules unless explicitly requested.

20 You need to delete 10 million records.Hard

Batch it, consider hard delete to skip the Recycle Bin, take a backup first, and expect it to take a long time — indexes and sharing recalculation make deletes expensive.

Also check what cascades, and whether anything downstream depends on those records.

21 A managed package is consuming most of your limits.Hard

You cannot change its code. Document the consumption with evidence, escalate to the vendor, and assess the exit cost.

Which is exactly what the pre-install review was meant to prevent.

22 A user reports data loss.Hard

Check the Recycle Bin, field history, and the audit trail for who changed what. Then determine whether it was a delete, an overwrite, or a sharing change making it invisible.

"Invisible" is more common than "deleted" and is far easier to fix.

23 A flow is hitting CPU limits.Hard

Loops with DML or queries inside, or multiple automations on the same object in one transaction.

Move queries and DML outside loops, consolidate the automations, and consider Apex for genuinely heavy logic.

24 An external system needs a daily extract of changes.Medium

Query by SystemModstamp with a watermark the consumer maintains — not LastModifiedDate, which can be set on load.

Or Change Data Capture if near-real-time is actually wanted.

25 The sponsor wants it live on Friday and it is not ready.Hard

Say what is ready, what is not, and the specific risk of shipping anyway — then let them decide with that information.

Offer a reduced scope that is genuinely ready rather than a full scope that is not.

Set two (26–50)

26 A guest page calls an @AuraEnabled method.Hard

That method is a public endpoint. Audit CRUD and FLS enforcement, parameter validation, row bounds, and whether anything in the path runs without sharing.

Elevated code reachable from a public page is the classic Salesforce exposure path.

27 A query returns inconsistent results between users.Medium

Sharing — the Apex is running with sharing and users see different records, which is usually correct.

Verify it is intended rather than reaching for elevation to make the symptom go away.

28 You inherit an org with 40 workflow rules and 20 processes.Hard

Inventory by object and timing, map the dependencies, then migrate to record-triggered flows incrementally with tests — highest-risk object first.

Not a big-bang migration; the order of operations is where the subtle breakages live.

29 Users want a field on a report that is not available.Medium

The report type does not include that object or the field is not in it — create a custom report type or extend the existing.

And check FLS, which removes fields from reports for users who cannot see them.

30 An approval process is stuck.Medium

The approver is inactive, has no delegate, or the next-step criteria match nothing.

Check the process instance and reassign — and add a delegate policy so it does not recur with the next leaver.

31 A platform event subscriber missed events.Hard

Retention is limited, so a subscriber down longer than the window loses events permanently — and replay IDs are the mechanism for catching up within it.

Design for the gap: a reconciliation job, or an idempotent catch-up by query.

32 A rollup is needed across a lookup.Medium

Roll-up summary fields need master-detail. Across a lookup, use a flow, Apex, or reconsider the relationship.

Changing lookup to master-detail has significant sharing and deletion consequences — do not do it casually.

33 An admin accidentally emailed 10,000 customers.Hard

Contain what can still be stopped, assess who received what, notify whoever needs to know, and then restrict who can send mass email.

The control is permissions and a confirmation step, not training.

34 A record page has 15 components and loads slowly.Medium

Each may make its own server call. Consolidate, lazy-load below the fold, use tabs, and audit what is genuinely needed.

Page layouts accumulate components one reasonable request at a time.

35 A sandbox refresh broke all the integrations.Medium

Endpoints, named credentials, certificates and identity configuration are environment-specific and reset on refresh.

Script the post-refresh setup — doing it manually from memory costs days every time.

36 A SOQL query is non-selective on 5 million records.Hard

Filter on an indexed field — primary keys, external IDs, lookups, or a custom index — and meet the selectivity threshold.

Nulls and negative operators defeat indexes, which is the usual reason an apparently indexed filter is still scanning.

37 One account owner has 400,000 records.Hard

Ownership skew. Keep that user out of the role hierarchy, or distribute ownership — sharing recalculation on that user becomes pathological.

It commonly arises from a default owner on an integration, which is worth checking first.

38 Marketing wants a public form that creates leads.Medium

Web-to-Lead or an Experience Cloud page, with spam controls, server-side validation and a hardened guest profile.

And a default owner that does not develop ownership skew.

39 A deployment succeeded but behaviour is wrong.Hard

Something environment-specific was not deployed: custom settings, a record type mapping, a named credential, or configuration done manually in the source org.

Which is what a deployment checklist and a post-deploy smoke test catch.

40 A junction object is needed.Easy

Many-to-many: a junction object with two master-detail relationships, with the first master determining ownership and sharing.

Which master comes first is a design decision, not an accident.

41 A batch job is failing on one chunk.Medium

Partial failure. Catch per-record errors, log them to an object, and let the rest proceed.

A batch that aborts on the first bad record processes nothing on the second run.

42 Someone asks for Modify All Data.Hard

Ask what they are trying to do — there is almost always a narrower permission that suffices.

If it is genuinely needed, grant it time-bound with an approval and an audit trail.

43 A list view takes 30 seconds.Medium

Non-selective filters, too many columns including formulas, or a very large underlying set.

Filter on indexed fields, reduce columns, and avoid formula columns that traverse objects.

44 Two systems both claim to own customer records.Hard

Decide the system of record per field, not per object — and implement that decision in the sync so writes flow one way.

Bidirectional sync without field-level ownership produces update loops and silent overwrites.

45 A Visualforce page exceeds view state.Medium

Reduce what is in the controller: mark fields transient, trim collections, and move work off the page.

Or rebuild it as an LWC, which has no view state at all.

46 An error appears only in production.Hard

Data volume, data shape, a different user's permissions, an installed package, or configuration that differs from the sandbox.

Reproduce with the affected user's access on production-like data rather than guessing from the sandbox's behaviour.

47 A custom object needs 200 fields.Medium

Challenge it — that is usually several entities, or fields nobody will populate.

And check the limits for your edition, plus the effect on page layouts, reports and query performance.

48 A scheduled job stopped running.Medium

The scheduling user was deactivated, the job was aborted, or an unhandled exception removed it.

Schedule jobs as a dedicated service user, not a person who might leave.

49 An AI feature gave a customer wrong information.Hard

Contain by adding review or disabling, correct with the customer, find the grounding gap through the audit trail, and add the case to the evaluation set.

Then ask what else that same gap could have gotten wrong.

50 You are asked to estimate work you do not understand.Medium

Timebox an investigation and estimate afterwards, or give a range with the assumptions stated explicitly.

A confident number you cannot justify is worse than a range with a named unknown.

Set three (51–75)

51 A trigger needs to run only on field change.Easy

Compare the new value to Trigger.oldMap and exit early when it has not changed.

Without that check the logic runs on every save, which is both wasteful and a source of recursion and spurious updates.

52 An integration user needs to bypass validation rules.Medium

A custom permission checked in the rule, or a bypass custom setting — applied narrowly and reviewed, not blanket.

And ask whether the data is genuinely valid; the rule may be catching a real upstream problem.

53 A customer asks for their data to be deleted.Hard

A documented process covering records, activities, files, backups, sandboxes and downstream systems, with evidence of completion.

Sandboxes and connected systems are the parts most commonly missed.

54 A field update in one flow triggers another.Hard

Cascading automation — map the chain by object and timing, because each hop consumes limits and the order is hard to predict.

Consolidate to one automation per object per timing, which is the only durable fix.

55 A user needs read access to all records of one object.Easy

View All on that object via a permission set — not View All Data, which grants everything on every object.

Object-level View All is the precise tool and it is routinely over-granted to the org-wide version.

56 A Postman request works and Apex callout fails.Medium

Remote site setting or named credential missing, a different TLS or certificate situation, or a header Postman set automatically.

Compare the actual request Apex sent against what Postman sent, header by header.

57 Field history is not showing a change.Medium

History tracking is not enabled on that field, or the change exceeded what history captures — and there is a limit on tracked fields per object.

For a full audit trail of specific fields, plan tracking deliberately rather than retrospectively.

58 A Lightning page is slow only for one profile.Hard

Different component visibility, different record access causing more sharing evaluation, or a component that queries more for that role.

Use the Lightning Usage app and reproduce as that user rather than as an admin.

59 Someone wants to store 50MB of files per record.Medium

Check storage cost and limits — and consider external storage with Files Connect or a link, rather than holding everything in Salesforce.

File storage is a recurring cost that grows without anyone deciding to grow it.

60 A duplicate rule is blocking legitimate records.Medium

Matching rules are too loose. Tune them, and consider alerting rather than blocking so users can judge.

Blocking rules that produce false positives get bypassed, which defeats the whole purpose.

61 An LWC needs to talk to another on the same page.Medium

Events up, properties down for parent-child; Lightning Message Service across the page or across technologies.

Not a shared global — that breaks encapsulation and makes both components untestable.

62 A report needs data from two unrelated objects.Medium

A join needs a relationship. Options: add a lookup, use a joined report if the structure allows, or build it in an analytics tool.

Reporting requirements legitimately influence the data model.

63 An org has 30 permission sets with overlapping grants.Hard

Rationalise into permission set groups by role, with a muting set where needed — and audit what each actually grants.

Overlapping sets make it impossible to answer "what can this user do", which is the question that matters.

64 A process must not run during a data migration.Medium

A bypass custom setting or custom permission the automation checks, enabled for the load user — and switched off afterwards.

Deactivating automation wholesale risks forgetting to reactivate it, which is its own incident.

65 You find without sharing in production code.Hard

Determine why. Sometimes it is legitimate — a system process that must see everything — and it needs its own authorisation check.

Often it was added to make something work, which means a sharing bug is being masked by elevation.

66 A time-based flow fired at the wrong time.Medium

Timezone handling, a changed criteria field, or the scheduled path recalculating when the record changed.

Check the scheduled actions on the record and the user and org timezone settings.

67 An org needs to support three currencies.Hard

Multi-currency, with dated exchange rates if historical accuracy matters — and it is effectively irreversible once enabled.

It affects reports, roll-ups and formulas throughout, so test the reporting impact before committing.

68 A lookup should prevent deletion of the parent.Easy

Set the lookup's delete behaviour to restrict deletion — the options are clear the field, restrict, or cascade with master-detail.

Default behaviour clears the field silently, which is how orphaned records appear.

69 Users are copying data into spreadsheets.Medium

A signal that reporting does not meet their needs — find out what they are doing in the spreadsheet.

It is also a data governance risk, so the fix is making the platform answer their question, not banning exports.

70 An API returns 500 intermittently.Medium

Retry with exponential backoff and jitter, with a cap and a dead-letter path — and log correlation IDs so the vendor can investigate.

Immediate uncapped retries make an overloaded service worse.

71 A custom metadata type or a custom setting?Medium

Custom metadata deploys with the release and is queryable without limits — right for configuration. Hierarchy custom settings are right for per-user or per-profile values.

Configuration that should move between orgs belongs in custom metadata.

72 A trigger framework is proposed.Medium

Worth it for a single entry point per object, ordered handlers, recursion control and bypass support — on an org with meaningful Apex automation.

Overkill for three small triggers, which is also a legitimate answer to give.

73 You need to expose Salesforce data to a website.Hard

Decide authenticated or anonymous first. For anonymous, prefer CMS or published content over exposing records, and harden the guest profile.

For authenticated, Experience Cloud with the appropriate licence and Sharing Sets.

74 A deployment needs a destructive change.Hard

Deploy it separately and deliberately, after verifying nothing references the component — integrations, reports, flows, formulas and code.

Destructive changes bundled with features are how an unrelated release takes down an integration.

75 A stakeholder wants a feature that will not scale.Hard

State the specific volume at which it breaks and what it would cost to fix later, then offer the scalable alternative.

"It will not scale" is unpersuasive; "at 2 million rows this takes 40 seconds" is not.

Set four (76–100)

76 An agent gave a customer an incorrect refund amount.Hard

The limit was in the instruction, not the action. Enforce amount caps and eligibility inside the action so the agent cannot exceed them.

Instructions are guidance; code is a guarantee. That distinction is the whole of agent safety.

77 Two teams need different page layouts on one object.Easy

Record types with different layouts, or dynamic forms with visibility rules — and dynamic forms are usually the lighter answer.

Record types proliferate and affect automation, picklists and reporting, so do not create them for layout alone.

78 A query needs to filter on a formula field.Medium

Formula fields are generally not indexed, so this will be non-selective at volume.

Store the value in a real indexed field maintained by automation if you must filter on it at scale.

79 Users need offline access on mobile.Medium

Check what the mobile app caches and what the Field Service mobile app supports — genuine offline with conflict handling is a real design problem.

And define what happens when two people edited the same record offline.

80 A composite API call partially failed.Hard

Decide whether it should be all-or-none — the composite API supports that — or whether partial success is acceptable and reconcilable.

Unhandled partial success leaves data in a state neither system expects, which is worse than a clean failure.

81 An admin changed a picklist value and reports broke.Medium

Report filters, automation criteria, formulas and integrations may all reference the old value.

Deactivate rather than delete, migrate the data, then remove — and check what references it first.

82 A long-running transaction times out.Hard

Move the work asynchronous — Queueable or Batch — and give the user feedback rather than holding the request.

And find what is actually slow: usually a query, a callout, or cascading automation.

83 A permission set grants more than intended.Medium

Audit what it actually contains — object, field, system permissions and Apex class access — rather than what its name suggests.

Permission sets accumulate grants, and the name stops describing them within a year.

84 Someone proposes storing JSON in a long text field.Medium

Workable for opaque payloads you never query, and a problem the moment anyone wants to report on or filter by what is inside.

If the structure matters, model it; if it is genuinely a blob, document that decision.

85 A test fails only when run with the full suite.Hard

Test interdependence — shared state, order dependence, or reliance on existing org data.

Use @isTest(SeeAllData=false), create your own data, and never rely on what happens to be in the org.

86 An executive asks why a simple change takes two weeks.Hard

Show where the time goes — usually review, testing, release windows and coordination rather than build.

If the answer is embarrassing, it is a governance problem worth fixing rather than explaining.

87 A flow needs to call an external service.Medium

An HTTP callout action with a named credential, or invocable Apex — and not from a record-triggered flow in the save path.

Handle the failure explicitly; a flow that errors on a callout can block the user's save.

88 A picklist needs 500 values.Medium

Almost certainly a lookup to a custom object instead — picklists that large are unusable and hit limits.

And a lookup gives you reporting, maintenance by non-admins and additional fields per value.

89 Two automations need to run in a specific order.Hard

Flow trigger order on record-triggered flows, or consolidate into one flow with ordered branches.

Relying on implicit order between different automation types is how a release reorders your business logic by accident.

90 A user has access they should not and nobody knows why.Hard

Work the sharing model in order and check each grant: OWD, role hierarchy, sharing rules, teams, manual shares, Apex shares, territory, and permission sets with View All.

The Sharing Hierarchy button and "why can this user see this" analysis is the systematic route.

91 An integration needs to call Salesforce 10 times per second.Hard

Model the daily API consumption, and challenge the pattern — that is usually per-record chatter that should be bulk or event-driven.

And consider whether Salesforce should be the system answering that volume of reads.

92 A field needs a different label per business unit.Medium

Translation Workbench handles language; per-business-unit labelling on one field generally does not work.

Which may indicate two fields, or that the business units should agree on terminology.

93 A data model decision is proving irreversible.Hard

Then the design time should have been proportionate to that. Now: assess the cost of living with it against the cost of migrating, and decide explicitly.

Master-detail conversions, field type changes and org strategy deserve scrutiny precisely because of this.

94 Nobody can explain why an automation exists.Medium

Trace what it does, find who it affects, and ask before removing — undocumented automation usually encodes a real requirement badly.

Then write the decision record its author did not write.

95 You disagree with an architectural decision.Hard

State the concern once with the specific consequence and cost, listen to the reasoning you may be missing, and if overruled, document it and build it well.

Re-litigating a settled decision costs more credibility than the point is usually worth.

96 A critical update is enforced next release.Medium

Test in a preview sandbox now and fix before the deadline — enforcement happens whether you are ready or not.

Unmanaged critical updates are the most predictable outage available on the platform.

97 You have one hour to assess an unfamiliar org.Hard

Automation per object, Apex volume and test quality, installed packages, integrations and their auth, who holds dangerous permissions, limit headroom, and whether source control is the truth.

That gives you the risk picture; detail can follow.

98 Everything is on fire and you must pick one thing.Hard

Whatever is losing data, exposing data or blocking revenue — in that order.

Say explicitly what you are not doing and why, so nobody believes it is being handled.

99 What question do you ask about every design?Hard

What happens at ten times the volume, and what happens when it fails halfway?

Those two questions find most of the defects that would otherwise be found in production by a customer.

100 One habit that most improves platform work?Hard

Reproducing the problem before changing anything — as the affected user, on representative data, with the actual error.

Most wasted debugging time is spent fixing a theory rather than a fault, and most bad fixes are changes made before the cause was understood.
