App Builder, UX & Mobile — 100 Scenarios

Page performance and EPT, Dynamic Forms and Actions, accessibility, mobile products, offline, and the UX decisions that determine whether people actually use what you built.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on App Builder, UX & Mobile and its Advanced section.

Page performance (1–18)

1 "The org is slow." Where do you start?Medium

The Lightning Usage App — EPT by page, by browser, by user and over time. It frequently reveals one browser, one geography or one user population rather than a platform problem.

Start with data. "The org is slow" is unactionable; "this page averages 6s EPT for the service team on Chrome" is.

2 EPT tells you a page is slow but not why.Medium

EPT is an aggregate. Use the page's component-level performance view to attribute the time to individual components.

Then the browser network tab for Apex call count and payload size — which is where the cause usually is.

3 A record page has 14 components.Medium

Each is work on load, and the number of components is more often the cause than any single one.

Audit what is genuinely needed above the fold, move the rest to tabs or on-demand, and question each one's business value.

4 Five components each call Apex on load.Medium

Five round trips, five latencies. Consolidate into one call returning a composite payload, or have them share an LDS wire on the same record.

This is usually the single biggest win available on a slow record page.

5 A component in a closed accordion still loads data.Medium

Components are created and connected even when not visible, so their data access runs.

Lazy-load: gate the fetch on an isActive property, or render the child conditionally so it is not created at all.

6 A report chart on a record page is slow.Medium

It runs a report on every page load. If the underlying report is unselective over a large object, the page waits for it.

Make the report's filters selective and indexable, or move the chart off the record page to somewhere users opt in.

7 A related list with 400 rows slows the page.Easy

Use a Dynamic Related List with a filter, a sort, chosen columns and a row limit.

"All 400 contacts" is not information. The three primary contacts are.

8 A managed package component is the slowest on the page.Medium

You cannot optimise it, but you can measure and attribute it — then decide whether it belongs on a high-traffic page at all.

Take the evidence to the vendor. A component-level timing breakdown naming their component gets a support case taken seriously.

9 Users on one site are much slower than others.Medium

Network, not the page. Check EPT by geography in the Lightning Usage App, and whether they are on a VPN routing traffic elsewhere.

Component tuning will not fix a 300ms round-trip penalty on every request.

10 A custom component queries 60 fields and renders 6.Easy

Query only what you render. Payload size matters on every request, and heap matters server-side.

It is the cheapest performance habit available and it is routinely skipped.

11 Salesforce Optimizer flags hundreds of issues.Medium

Treat it as a backlog, not a to-do list: unused fields, too many layouts, excessive components per page, inactive validation rules.

Prioritise what affects users or blocks change. An unprioritised Optimizer report gets ignored.

12 How do you set a performance target?Medium

From the task: a record page a user opens fifty times a day deserves a tighter target than a monthly admin screen.

Measure the baseline, agree a number, and track it — otherwise "faster" is an opinion nobody can verify.

13 A page is fast for you and slow for users.Medium

Your data, your permissions, your browser and your network are not theirs. Admins frequently have smaller related-record counts and broader access that short-circuits queries.

Test as a real user with real data, on their browser.

14 A console app feels slower than a standard page.Medium

Console apps keep multiple tabs alive, so leaked listeners, timers and subscriptions accumulate across a working day.

Audit cleanup in disconnectedCallback, and reduce what each tab holds open.

15 You need to prove a change improved performance.Medium

Baseline EPT before, deploy, compare after over a comparable period and population.

A single before/after page load proves nothing — variance between loads is larger than most improvements.

16 A page loads fast but feels slow.Medium

Perceived performance: content shifting as components load, spinners appearing late, or the thing the user came for rendering last.

Render the primary content first and reserve space so the layout does not jump. Perception is the thing users report.

17 Is caching an option for a slow component?Medium

A cacheable=true wire gives you the platform's client cache keyed by parameters — genuinely effective for reference data.

Not for anything that must be current, and every read must still handle a miss.

18 What is the realistic order of performance wins?Hard

Fewer components above the fold; consolidate Apex calls; fix unselective reports and related lists; query fewer fields; lazy-load inactive tabs; then audit package components.

In that order, most pages come back under control without clever engineering.

App Builder & Dynamic Forms (19–36)

19 You have 10 record types purely for different field arrangements.Hard

Dynamic Forms replaces that: one layout with conditional field visibility, so most layout-driven record types become unnecessary.

Keep record types where they drive genuinely different processes or picklist domains — not different field orders.

20 A field must be visible only to approvers.Medium

Component or field visibility on a custom permission the approvers hold — not a profile name, which breaks when profiles change.

And remember visibility is presentation. If they must not access it, that is field-level security.

21 An action should only appear when the record is open.Easy

Dynamic Actions with visibility criteria on the record's status.

Enforce it server side too: the action's flow or Apex must check the condition, because a hidden button is still callable.

22 Dynamic Forms has 25 conditional sections and the page is slow.Medium

Conditional visibility is still rendering work, and 25 sections is also impossible for anyone to reason about.

Simplify the information architecture: what is needed on load, and what belongs on a second tab or a guided flow?

23 Users say the page has 40 fields and they use six.Medium

Design from the task. The six go in a prominent top section; the rest move to collapsed sections or a details tab.

Also set the compact layout deliberately — its first fields are the highlights panel and the mobile card, and most orgs never choose them on purpose.

24 The business wants a different page per region.Medium

One page with component visibility by region, or App Builder page assignment by app and profile — not twenty pages to maintain in step.

Push back on whether the differences are real: usually a handful of genuine variants hide behind many requests.

25 How do you assign a Lightning page to the right audience?Easy

App Builder activation by app, record type and profile — which is how you give the service team a different Case page from sales.

Document the assignments; an unassigned page is invisible and an over-assigned one surprises people.

26 A component works on a record page but not the home page.Easy

Its meta XML targets, and whether it depends on an injected recordId that only exists on a record page.

Declare the targets you support, and handle the absent-record case rather than breaking.

27 Admins need to configure your component.Easy

Expose @api properties and declare them as design attributes in the meta XML so App Builder shows them, with sensible defaults and descriptions.

Validate the values — an admin will eventually enter something unexpected.

28 A utility bar item is the right choice when?Medium

For something always available regardless of the record in view — a timer, notes, a recent-items panel, a softphone.

It stays loaded, so keep it light; a heavy utility item taxes every page in the app.

29 How do you decide tabs versus one long page?Medium

Tabs when content is genuinely separable and users need one area at a time — and they give you lazy loading as a bonus.

One page when users scan everything. Tabs that hide something people always need just add a click.

30 A screen flow embedded on a record page.Easy

A good pattern for guided tasks, and frequently better than a crowded record page — particularly on mobile.

Pass the record Id in, and give it a clear entry point so users understand what it does.

31 Should you build a custom component or configure?Medium

Configure first: Dynamic Forms, Dynamic Related Lists, embedded reports and flows cover a surprising amount.

Custom components are justified by genuinely different interaction, not by appearance preference — and they are yours to maintain forever.

32 A stakeholder asks for a "simple configurable table".Medium

Check what lightning-datatable plus a Dynamic Related List already does — frequently the requirement is already met.

A generic custom table is one of the most commonly rebuilt and most commonly regretted components in Salesforce projects.

33 How many components is too many on one page?Medium

There is no fixed number — it depends on what each does. The honest test is measured EPT against your target, plus whether a user can find anything.

If you cannot explain why each component is there, there are too many.

34 App Builder changes are not appearing for users.Easy

The page was not activated, or was activated for a different app, profile or record type combination.

Check the activation assignments, and verify by logging in as an affected user.

35 How do you deploy Lightning page assignments?Medium

Pages are metadata and deploy; their assignments are the fiddly part and frequently need verification or manual setting after deployment.

Put assignment verification in the release checklist — "the page deployed but nobody sees it" is a common post-release ticket.

36 Is hiding a field with visibility rules secure?Easy

No. Visibility rules are presentation; the data remains available via API, reports and SOQL.

Field-level security is the control. Demonstrating the gap with one REST call ends the argument quickly.

Accessibility (37–54)

37 A keyboard user cannot operate your component.Easy

Almost certainly a clickable div. It has no keyboard operability, no focus state, no role and no accessible name.

If it behaves like a button, make it a button — that one change fixes four failures at once.

38 An icon-only button announces as "button".Easy

No accessible name. Add alternative-text on base components, or aria-label.

This is the single most common accessibility omission in custom Salesforce UIs.

39 A designer wants focus outlines removed.Easy

Decline, and offer to restyle them. Without a visible focus indicator, keyboard users cannot tell where they are.

A custom focus style meeting contrast requirements satisfies both the design and the requirement.

40 Tab order jumps around the page.Medium

DOM order does not match visual order — often CSS reordering — or someone used a positive tabindex.

Fix the DOM order. Only tabindex="0" and -1 should ever appear.

41 A toast confirms a save but screen-reader users miss it.Medium

Dynamic content needs a live region to be announced. Platform toasts generally handle this; hand-rolled notifications do not.

Use the platform toast, or add an appropriate live region for inline status messages.

42 Status is shown only by a coloured dot.Easy

Colour alone excludes colour-blind users — and it is unclear for everyone without a legend.

Add text or an icon shape alongside the colour.

43 Contrast fails only in dark mode.Medium

Check both themes: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI boundaries.

Using SLDS tokens rather than fixed colours generally gives compliant values in both themes without separate work.

44 A modal traps keyboard users.Hard

A modal needs focus moved in on open, contained while open, Escape to close, and returned to the trigger on close.

That is a lot to get right, which is a strong argument for the platform modal rather than a custom one.

45 Headings are styled divs.Easy

Screen-reader users navigate by heading structure, so styled divs remove their primary navigation mechanism.

Use real h1–h6 in a sensible hierarchy and style them, rather than the reverse.

46 Validation errors are not associated with their fields.Medium

The error must be programmatically linked so it is announced with the input, not just displayed in red nearby.

Base input components handle this via setCustomValidity/reportValidity — another reason to use them.

47 A table reads confusingly in a screen reader.Medium

Missing table semantics: proper th with scope, a caption, and no layout-only tables.

If you are using a table for layout, use CSS or SLDS grid instead.

48 How do you actually test accessibility?Medium

Keyboard only, a screen reader (NVDA or VoiceOver), 200% zoom, and an automated checker like axe.

Automated tools catch roughly a third of real issues. The keyboard pass finds most of the rest and takes two minutes per component.

49 A page is unusable at 200% zoom.Medium

Fixed pixel sizing and fixed-height containers. Use relative units and let content reflow.

200% zoom is a requirement, not an edge case, and it takes seconds to check.

50 Does accessibility apply to internal tools?Easy

Yes — your colleagues include people using keyboards, screen readers and magnification, and in many jurisdictions it is a legal obligation for internal systems too.

It is also the cheapest usability work available: most fixes improve things for everyone.

51 Who owns accessibility on the team?Easy

Whoever builds the component. Treating it as a later QA pass means it never happens, because retrofitting costs far more than building it right.

Put it in the definition of done and the PR checklist.

52 A base component's accessibility is better than your custom one.Easy

Nearly always — base components bring accessible names, keyboard behaviour, focus management and ARIA for free.

That is the strongest practical argument for composing base components rather than re-implementing them.

53 A content author writes link text "click here".Easy

Screen-reader users navigate by link list, where "click here" is meaningless. Link text should describe the destination.

Worth including in content guidance as well as component review.

54 An accessibility audit returns 80 findings.Medium

Group them: most will be a handful of repeated patterns (unnamed icon buttons, clickable divs, colour-only status).

Fix the patterns and add them to the review checklist, rather than working through 80 individual instances.

Mobile (55–76)

55 The business wants a branded mobile app.Medium

Mobile Publisher — your branding, your app store listing, push notifications, still the Salesforce runtime and your existing configuration.

A Mobile SDK build is only justified by genuinely custom UX or device integration, and it is dramatically more expensive.

56 Which mobile option for field technicians?Easy

The Field Service mobile app — a separate, offline-first app built for that job, with its own data and sync model.

The standard Salesforce mobile app is not an offline-first field app. See its architecture.

57 A record page is unusable on a phone.Medium

Too many fields and components, dense tables, and a compact layout nobody chose.

Set the compact layout deliberately, hide desktop-dense components by form factor, and consider a guided flow for the task rather than the record page.

58 Mobile users need different actions.Easy

Configure a separate mobile action list. A phone user wants Call, Log a Call and Navigate — not Clone and Change Owner.

Default action lists are rarely right for mobile.

59 Touch targets are too small.Easy

Aim for roughly 44px minimum. Dense tables and small icon buttons are hostile on a phone.

Switch to a card layout below a breakpoint rather than shrinking a desktop design.

60 How do you branch behaviour by device?Medium

@salesforce/client/formFactor in LWC, and component visibility rules by form factor in App Builder.

Prefer responsive CSS for layout, and reserve form-factor branching for genuinely different behaviour.

61 A screen flow is better than a record page on mobile.Medium

Usually, for a task: one question at a time, large controls, no scrolling a 40-field layout.

Guided flows are the most under-used mobile pattern on the platform.

62 Users need read access with no signal.Medium

Briefcase Builder — define which objects, filters and related records are primed to the device, with a record limit.

Keep briefcases tight: more priming means slower sync, more storage and staler data.

63 Offline edits failed to save.Hard

Validation fires at sync time, not edit time. Any rule the user could not satisfy offline produces a sync error they may not notice.

Audit validation rules and required fields for offline-satisfiability, and move unsatisfiable checks server side.

64 How much offline capability does the standard app have?Medium

Read plus limited edit of primed data — not the full application.

Validate what users actually need to do with no signal before promising offline. For genuine offline-first work, Field Service's app is a different proposition.

65 How do you test offline properly?Medium

On a real device, in genuine aeroplane mode, with a realistic amount of work queued — then reconnect and inspect what failed.

Emulators and "turn off wifi" do not reproduce the sync queue behaviour.

66 Initial sync takes ten minutes.Medium

Over-priming. Narrow the briefcase: tighter filters, fewer related objects, a shorter date window.

Most users need days of data, not months.

67 An LWC must work offline.Hard

Offline means the GraphQL wire adapter against primed data plus supported device capabilities — arbitrary Apex needs a server.

Design the data access around what is on the device, and render an explicit offline state.

68 You need the camera or barcode scanning.Medium

LWC mobile capabilities expose device features such as the barcode scanner and location — with a graceful fallback when unavailable.

Always handle the unavailable case: the same component may run on desktop.

69 Push notifications are needed.Medium

Supported, and a reason to choose Mobile Publisher — but they need a notification strategy, not just a technical switch.

Too many notifications and users disable them, which removes the channel permanently.

70 A deep link should open a specific record in the app.Medium

Supported via the app's URL scheme and Lightning page references — which is how an email or notification lands a user in the right place.

Test it from the real clients users have, because link handling differs between them.

71 Design for a slow, intermittent connection.Medium

Show progress, allow retry, and never lose typed input on failure.

A field user on 2G is the realistic worst case, and losing a form's content on a failed save is the complaint you will hear.

72 A component looks fine on desktop and broken on mobile.Easy

Fixed widths, dense tables or a layout assuming horizontal space.

Test at phone width with a 16px gutter and no horizontal page scroll, and switch to a card layout where a table will not fit.

73 Mobile SDK or Mobile Publisher?Medium

Publisher for branding and distribution of what you already built — far cheaper and it inherits your configuration.

SDK only when the UX or device integration genuinely cannot be done in the runtime, and you accept owning a native codebase.

74 How do you decide what belongs on mobile at all?Medium

From the tasks people genuinely do away from a desk — usually looking something up, logging an interaction, or completing a short form.

Trying to put the whole desktop application on a phone is how mobile rollouts fail to get adopted.

75 Adoption of the mobile app is poor.Medium

Usually the experience, not the technology: too many taps, pages designed for desktop, or it does not work where they have no signal.

Watch three users do their actual job on it. The fix is almost always fewer fields and a guided flow.

76 A mobile user cannot see data they need.Medium

Sharing, or priming. Check record access in the browser as that user first — if a related list is empty there too, it is access, not the app.

That one check saves days of debugging the wrong layer.

UX decisions (77–100)

77 How do you start a UX design?Medium

From the task, not the record: who is doing what, how often, on what device, and what is the fewest interactions that completes it.

Starting from the schema produces a form. Starting from the task produces something people use.

78 A stakeholder specifies the solution, not the problem.Medium

Ask what the user is trying to achieve and how they do it today. "Add a field for X" frequently resolves into a different, simpler change.

Deliver what they asked for if they insist — but ask first, because the question is cheap and the build is not.

79 Users keep entering data in the wrong field.Medium

A labelling or ordering problem, not a training problem. Check the labels, the help text, the field order, and whether two fields sound identical.

Blaming users for a predictable mistake leaves the mistake in place.

80 A process takes 14 clicks.Medium

Map the actual path, then remove steps: defaults instead of entry, a guided flow instead of navigation, an action instead of a page visit.

Count the clicks before and after — it is the most persuasive number you can show a sponsor.

81 Two teams want opposite layouts.Medium

Dynamic Forms with visibility by profile or custom permission, or separate page assignments per app — one page definition where possible.

Two maintained layouts is a cost; two maintained layouts that drift apart is a bigger one.

82 How do you validate a design before building it?Medium

Show three real users a clickable mock or a rough configured version and watch them attempt the task — without explaining it.

Half an hour of that prevents more rework than any amount of specification review.

83 Help text nobody reads.Easy

If the field needs explaining, the label is probably wrong — or the field should not be there.

Help text is a fallback, not a fix for a confusing design. Fix the label and the order first.

84 An error message says "Invalid data".Easy

The cheapest usability win available: say what is wrong, what to change and why, attached to the field where possible.

Users act on specific messages and ignore generic ones.

85 How do you handle a destructive action?Medium

Confirm it, state the consequence plainly, and make the confirm button describe the action rather than saying "OK".

And where possible make it reversible — undo is better than confirmation.

86 A long operation gives no feedback.Easy

Show a spinner immediately, disable the triggering control, and keep the rest of the page usable.

If it may take many seconds, move it async with a notification rather than an indefinite spinner.

87 Users do not trust the data.Medium

Usually a freshness or completeness problem they have no way to assess. Show a "last updated" timestamp and the source.

Visible provenance does more for trust than accuracy improvements users cannot see.

88 A dashboard shows 20 metrics.Medium

Nobody acts on 20 numbers. Ask which three decisions this dashboard supports, and build for those.

Also pair every throughput metric with a quality metric, or the dashboard drives the wrong behaviour.

89 Adoption of a new feature is low.Medium

Measure before theorising: is it discoverable, does it work, is it faster than what they did before?

Most low adoption is discoverability or a workflow regression, not resistance.

90 A power user wants more fields; a new user wants fewer.Medium

Progressive disclosure: the common fields prominent, the rest in collapsed sections — which serves both without two layouts.

Design for the common case and make the rare case reachable.

91 How do you measure UX improvement?Medium

Task completion time, clicks, error and rework rate, adoption, and EPT — baselined before the change.

Without a baseline you cannot distinguish an improvement from a preference.

92 A design works but looks inconsistent with the rest of the app.Easy

Use SLDS tokens, spacing utilities and base components so it inherits the design system.

Consistency is itself usability: users apply learned patterns, and a bespoke-looking screen costs them attention.

93 Should you match a competitor's UI?Medium

Match the pattern where it is genuinely better, not the appearance. Fighting the platform's design system costs maintenance forever.

And verify the pattern actually suits your users' task rather than assuming it does.

94 A page is used by 500 people once a day.Medium

That is 500 interactions daily, so small improvements compound substantially — this is where optimisation effort belongs.

Rank UX work by frequency × population, not by who complained most recently.

95 Training is proposed to fix a confusing screen.Medium

Training is a workaround for a design problem, and it decays with every new joiner.

Fix the screen where you can, and reserve training for genuine process complexity rather than avoidable confusion.

96 How do you say no to a UX request?Medium

With the cost and an alternative: what it would displace, what it adds permanently, and the cheaper way to meet the underlying need.

"No" alone loses; "here is the simpler thing that solves it" usually wins.

97 A record page accumulated 30 components over three years.Medium

Nobody ever removes anything. Audit by usage and value, measure EPT, and remove with the owners' agreement.

Schedule a page review annually — it will not happen otherwise.

98 Mobile and desktop need genuinely different designs.Medium

That is legitimate: different tasks, different contexts. Use form-factor visibility and a mobile-specific action list, or a flow for the mobile task.

Forcing one design to serve both usually serves neither.

99 What signals a well-designed Salesforce screen?Hard

The user's primary task is obvious and takes few interactions; the fields on load are the ones needed; it works by keyboard and at 200% zoom; it is usable on a phone; and EPT meets a target someone agreed.

None of that is about appearance.

100 One habit that most improves UX work?Hard

Watching someone actually do the task before designing, and again after.

Every other technique is a proxy for that. Thirty minutes of observation routinely overturns assumptions that would otherwise be built, shipped and defended.
