CPQ & Revenue Cloud — 100 Scenarios

The pricing waterfall and calculation events, price and product rules, bundles, amendments and renewals, proration disputes, performance ceilings, and the config that is data.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on CPQ & Revenue Cloud and its Advanced section.

The pricing waterfall (1–20)

1 "The price is wrong." Where do you start?Hard

Walk the pricing waterfall stage by stage and find the first point where the number diverges from expectation.

Then: which price rules fired, at which event, in what evaluation order; is a discount schedule or contracted price applying; is the QCP overwriting a value after pricing ran.

2 A discount rule works in the editor and not on save.Hard

It is set to run After Calculate, so pricing has already happened — the value is written but never priced from.

Anything that feeds pricing must run Before Calculate. "Works sometimes" is almost always the wrong event.

3 What are the calculation events, in order?Medium

On Initialization, Before Calculate, the pricing engine, On Calculate, After Calculate, then totals saved.

Knowing the order is how you choose where a rule belongs — and why the same logic behaves differently at two different events.

4 When do you use On Initialization?Medium

Setting defaults when the quote line editor opens, before the user sees anything.

Not for pricing logic — it runs once at open, not on each recalculation.

5 What belongs in After Calculate?Medium

Values derived from the final price — margin, display text, a rounded presentation value.

Anything written there will not re-price, which is exactly why it suits derived output.

6 Two price rules produce an unpredictable result.Hard

They fire at the same event with no deterministic order, or one overwrites the other's field.

Set evaluation order explicitly, and document why — ordering that exists only as numbers is ordering nobody can safely change.

7 List price is not what the price book says.Medium

A contracted price or negotiated rate is overriding it — the waterfall applies special prices above list.

Check contracted prices at account and contract level before suspecting the price book entry.

8 A volume discount is not applying.Medium

Discount schedule configuration: the tier boundaries, whether it is range or slab, and whether it is attached at the right level.

Also check the quantity the schedule is evaluating — bundle quantities can differ from what the user entered.

9 Net price differs from what finance expected.Medium

Work the chain: list → special → prorated → regular → customer → partner → net. Each stage has a reason to differ.

Identify the first divergent stage; debugging from the final number backwards is far slower.

10 A lookup query is needed for rate cards.Medium

Lookup Queries let a price rule read a custom table of rates — the standard pattern for rate cards and matrix pricing.

Each is a query during calculation, so index the lookup object's filter fields.

11 Pricing differs between two reps for the same configuration.Hard

Different price books, different contracted prices on the account, or a rule conditioned on a user or profile field.

Compare the two quotes' inputs before examining the rules — the inputs differ more often than the logic does.

12 Rounding produces a penny difference.Medium

Where the rounding happens matters: per line, per bundle, or at the total.

Agree the convention with finance and encode it explicitly, with a test case stating expected figures to the penny.

13 Multi-currency quotes price incorrectly.Hard

Price book entries must exist per currency, and the conversion behaviour interacts with Advanced Currency Management if enabled.

Test each currency explicitly — a missing entry silently produces a wrong or unavailable price.

14 A product should be free in a bundle.Medium

Set the option's pricing method so it does not contribute, or price it at zero via a rule at Before Calculate.

Decide whether it should appear on the quote document at zero or not appear at all — that is a separate decision.

15 Percent-of-total pricing is required.Medium

CPQ supports percent-of-total pricing for services priced as a share of the bundle — configured on the option.

Watch the ordering: it depends on the other lines being priced first, which constrains where other rules can run.

16 A quote total does not match the opportunity.Medium

The quote is not the primary, or the opportunity has not been synced — CPQ syncs on demand rather than continuously.

Decide which is authoritative and make the sync behaviour match it.

17 Pricing must consider a competitor's quote.Medium

That is a field on the quote plus a rule or approval threshold — CPQ cannot infer it.

Keep such inputs explicit and reportable, so discounting decisions can be analysed later.

18 How do you test pricing changes?Hard

A golden set of quotes with known expected totals, recalculated after every rule change, package upgrade and migration.

It is the only reliable regression test for a pricing engine, and it takes an afternoon to build.

19 A price rule fires for the wrong products.Medium

Its conditions are too broad, or the condition evaluates a field that is not yet populated at that event.

Check both the condition and the event — a correct condition at the wrong event behaves erratically.

20 Finance disputes a total and nobody can explain it.Hard

That is a documentation failure as much as a configuration one. Record the intended waterfall behaviour and the active rules per scenario.

Then the golden set proves it. Pricing nobody can explain is pricing nobody can defend.

Rules & configuration (21–42)

21 What are the four product rule types for?Medium

Validation blocks an invalid configuration; Alert warns without blocking; Selection auto-adds, removes or hides options; Filter narrows what is selectable in a dynamic bundle.

Choosing the wrong type produces either a blocked sale or an unenforced rule.

22 A validation message says "Invalid combination".Easy

Useless to the person configuring. Say what is wrong and what to change: "A 400V chassis requires the high-output power supply."

Message quality in CPQ directly affects how long a quote takes to build.

23 Choosing a chassis should auto-add a power supply.Easy

A Selection product rule, which adds the required option automatically.

Decide whether the user can then remove it — if not, hide or lock it rather than relying on a validation rule to catch the removal.

24 A catalogue of 4,000 products is unusable.Medium

Filter product rules plus search filters in the product selection screen, narrowing by the attributes that matter.

Presenting thousands of options and expecting the user to know is a configuration failure, not a user one.

25 One question should apply across the bundle.Medium

Configuration attributes — asked once, applied to the options beneath.

Combined with twin fields (matching API names) they propagate values between objects without code.

26 What are twin fields?Medium

Fields with the same API name on related CPQ objects, which CPQ copies automatically — product to quote line, quote line to order product, and so on.

A very powerful convention, and a confusing one when someone creates a matching field name by accident.

27 Bundles are nested three deep.Medium

Deep nesting multiplies configuration work and calculation cost. Flatten where the business will accept it.

Each level also makes rules harder to reason about, which is where configuration bugs concentrate.

28 A rule must consider a field on the Account.Medium

Bring it onto the quote (twin field, formula or a lookup query) so the rule can evaluate it — rules work on the quote's data.

Pulling it in once is cleaner than referencing across objects repeatedly.

29 How many rules is too many?Medium

Every rule is evaluated on every calculation, so rule count is a direct performance cost.

Consolidate, add tight conditions, and delete the ones nobody remembers creating — which is usually a meaningful fraction.

30 A QCP is proposed for logic rules could do.Medium

Prefer declarative rules: visible to admins, no deployment, and no JavaScript to maintain.

Reserve the Quote Calculator Plugin for logic genuinely too complex for rules — and expect it to be where performance problems appear.

31 The QCP is slow.Hard

It runs client-side over the whole quote model, so a loop inside a loop over all lines is the standard cause of a sixty-second quote.

Build maps once and iterate linearly — the same discipline as bulk Apex.

32 Which QCP hook should you use?Medium

The same event points as price rules — on initialisation, before calculate, on calculate, after calculate.

Choose for the same reason: anything feeding pricing goes before, anything derived from price goes after.

33 A rule should only apply to one sales channel.Easy

A condition on a quote field identifying the channel — populated from the account, the user or the price book.

Keep the channel on the quote so rules, approvals and reporting all read the same value.

34 Users bypass a validation rule by reordering steps.Medium

The rule evaluates at a point the user can avoid. Move it to a later, unavoidable event — or enforce it at quote submission.

A rule that only fires on one path is not a control.

35 A configuration takes twenty minutes to build.Medium

Too many manual choices. Use selection rules for the forced ones, configuration attributes for the repeated ones, and defaults for the common case.

Measure the time before and after — quote build time is a metric worth tracking.

36 Advanced Approvals is being considered.Medium

A separate package with its own configuration records, suited to complex, conditional, multi-level quote approvals.

It also brings its own migration problem, since that configuration is data too.

37 Approvals are slowing deals down.Medium

Usually thresholds set too low, or approvers who are bottlenecks. Measure time-in-approval by step.

Raising a threshold with a reporting control is frequently better than a faster approval process.

38 A quote document has the wrong terms.Medium

Quote template content and conditional sections — terms driven by the quote's data rather than a single static block.

Review templates when products or jurisdictions change; stale terms on a generated document are a legal problem.

39 The quote document generation is slow.Medium

Large quotes with many lines and conditional sections. Reduce what is rendered, paginate, or split the document.

And set expectations: a 400-line quote document is not going to render instantly.

40 How do you document CPQ configuration?Medium

Per rule: its purpose, event, conditions and the scenario it serves — plus the golden-set quote that proves it.

CPQ configuration is business logic expressed as data, and undocumented it becomes untouchable.

41 A rule was changed and quotes broke.Medium

No regression test. Recalculate the golden set after every configuration change.

Because the configuration is data, there is no deployment gate to catch it — the golden set is your gate.

42 Who should own CPQ configuration?Medium

A named owner who understands both the pricing intent and the event model — it sits between admin and developer.

Shared, undocumented ownership of pricing rules is how a quote becomes unexplainable to finance.

Contracts, amendments & renewals (43–64)

43 What does ordering a quote create?Easy

A Contract with Subscriptions representing what the customer now owns — and subscriptions, not quote lines, are the system of record for entitlement.

Everything about amendments and renewals is driven from subscriptions.

44 A customer adds 10 licences mid-term.Medium

An amendment quote, pre-filled from current subscriptions, priced prorated from the amendment date to the contract end.

The proration convention is where disputes originate, so it must be agreed and tested.

45 Proration is a penny out and finance objects.Hard

Day-based versus month-based proration, whether the amendment date is inclusive, and how partial months round — each changes the invoice.

Agree the convention in writing, encode it in a test case with figures to the penny, and re-run it after every package upgrade.

46 A renewal needs a 5% uplift.Easy

Renewal uplift configuration on the contract, applied when the renewal quote is generated.

Verify how it interacts with contracted prices — a negotiated rate may be intended to survive the uplift, or not, and that is a business decision.

47 The renewal forecast is wrong.Medium

Renewal forecasting is driven from the contract's subscriptions and dates — so missing or incorrect subscription data produces a wrong pipeline.

Check the contract data before investigating the forecast.

48 A customer wants everything to renew on one date.Hard

Co-termination — aligning multiple subscriptions to a single end date, which is essential for account management and fiddly with partial periods.

Test the proration on the shortened subscriptions specifically; that is where co-termination goes wrong.

49 What are contracted prices for?Medium

Negotiated prices stored at account or contract level that override list on future quotes — "this customer always gets that rate".

They sit high in the waterfall, which is why a quote can ignore the price book entirely.

50 An evergreen subscription behaves oddly on amendment.Hard

Evergreen (no end date) subscriptions amend differently from termed ones.

Decide the model before go-live; changing between evergreen and termed later means data remediation.

51 A cancellation mid-term is required.Medium

An amendment reducing quantity to zero or terminating the subscription, with proration determining any credit.

Agree whether credits are issued and how, because CPQ computes the figure but the commercial policy is yours.

52 Amendment quotes show unexpected lines.Medium

They are pre-filled from current subscriptions, so anything the customer owns appears — including things added by previous amendments.

That is correct behaviour, and it surprises users who expect a blank quote.

53 Two amendments are in flight simultaneously.Hard

Risky: both were pre-filled from the same subscription state, so the second does not know about the first.

Control it with process — one open amendment per contract — because the data model does not prevent it.

54 A contract was created with wrong dates.Medium

Everything downstream (proration, renewal forecasting, co-termination) depends on them, so correcting them is a data exercise rather than a field edit.

Validate dates at order time, where the cost of fixing is lowest.

55 How do subscriptions relate to assets?Medium

Subscriptions represent recurring entitlement; assets represent physical or licensed items owned.

Many implementations need both, and keeping their lifecycles consistent is a design decision people leave implicit.

56 Billing needs to know about amendments.Medium

The amendment's prorated amounts and the updated subscription state must reach the billing system — usually via integration.

Define which system calculates the invoice; duplicating proration logic in two systems guarantees divergence.

57 Revenue Cloud is proposed over CPQ.Medium

Establish what it must do that CPQ does not — billing, revenue recognition, broader lifecycle management.

And verify the migration path and feature parity for your specific needs rather than assuming it is a superset.

58 A customer disputes what they are entitled to.Medium

Subscriptions on the contract are the answer — not the original quote, which may have been amended several times.

If subscriptions do not reflect reality, that is the bug to fix, because everything downstream reads them.

59 How do you test amendments and renewals?Hard

Scenario tests with known expected figures: an add mid-term, a reduction, a co-termination, a renewal with uplift.

Each with expected totals to the penny. These are the paths that cost money when wrong.

60 A renewal was generated but not sent.Medium

Renewal generation is automated; the sales process around it usually is not.

Build the follow-up into the process with a task or alert — generated renewals nobody actions are lost revenue that looks like a forecast.

61 Amendments are being done as new quotes.Medium

Then subscriptions do not reflect reality, proration is wrong, and renewals will be wrong.

Fix the process and training — this is a common and damaging shortcut, because the data corruption accumulates silently.

62 A contract needs splitting between entities.Hard

Not a standard operation — it requires terminating and re-creating subscriptions, with careful proration and credit handling.

Treat it as a data exercise with finance involved, not a configuration change.

63 How far ahead should renewals generate?Medium

Far enough for the sales cycle to complete before expiry — which is a business input, not a technical default.

Too early and the quote is stale; too late and renewals slip past the end date.

64 What is the most common amendment mistake?Hard

Not agreeing the proration convention, so finance and CPQ disagree about every mid-term change.

Followed by amendments done as new quotes, which corrupts subscriptions and therefore every renewal that follows.

Performance & deployment (65–84)

65 A 600-line quote times out.Hard

Calculation time grows with lines × rules, and large quotes hit ceilings.

Split into multiple quotes, reduce rule evaluation scope with tighter conditions, and audit the QCP for nested loops — in that order.

66 Calculation is slow with only 50 lines.Medium

Rule count or lookup queries, not line count. Every rule is evaluated on every calculation.

Consolidate rules, tighten conditions, and index the lookup objects' filter fields.

67 How do you profile CPQ performance?Medium

Time a representative quote with rules progressively disabled to attribute the cost, and check the browser for QCP execution time.

Bisecting the rule set is cruder than a profiler and considerably more effective.

68 Quote line editor is unusable on a large catalogue.Medium

Filter rules and search filters to narrow selection, and fewer displayed fields in the editor.

Presenting everything and relying on scrolling is the usual cause.

69 CPQ configuration is missing after deployment.Hard

Because almost all of it is data: price rules, conditions, actions, lookup queries, discount schedules, product options and features, configuration attributes, package settings.

Change sets and source deployments do not carry it. You need a scripted, version-controlled migration.

70 What order do you migrate CPQ config in?Hard

Products → options and features → rules → conditions and actions → lookup queries, re-pointing references at each step.

Record Ids differ per org, so references must be resolved by name or external key during the load.

71 A package upgrade changed calculation behaviour.Hard

It happens. Install the upgrade in a Full sandbox and recalculate the golden set before touching production.

Treat CPQ upgrades as a project with regression time, not a click.

72 How do you keep sandbox CPQ config in sync?Medium

Run the same scripted migration into each environment, and re-run it after every sandbox refresh.

Hand-configuring each environment guarantees they diverge, and then sandbox testing proves nothing about production.

73 A refresh wiped the CPQ configuration.Medium

Expected — it is data. Re-run the seeding migration as part of the post-refresh checklist.

Which is why the migration must be scripted and repeatable rather than a one-off done at go-live.

74 How do you version CPQ configuration?Medium

Export it as data files in version control, with the migration script, so a change is reviewable in a diff.

It is less convenient than metadata, and it is the only way to get a review trail for pricing logic.

75 Someone changed a price rule in production.Hard

There is no deployment gate, so it took effect immediately with no review — and metadata drift detection will not see it.

Restrict who can edit CPQ configuration in production, and reconcile against your version-controlled export.

76 How do you test CPQ in CI?Hard

Apex tests can cover custom code, but the pricing behaviour needs a scenario test: seed configuration, build a quote, calculate, assert totals.

That is slower than a unit test and it is the only thing that verifies the engine behaves as intended.

77 Quote data volume is growing fast.Medium

Quote lines, and especially subscription and schedule records, multiply quickly — a line times twelve periods times thousands of quotes.

Plan retention for abandoned quotes, and watch storage as a metric.

78 Reporting on CPQ data is difficult.Medium

The object model is deep (quote → line → option → subscription), so report types get complex and joins multiply.

Flatten key values onto the quote or opportunity with twin fields for reporting, rather than reporting across the whole chain.

79 Integration needs quote data.Medium

Expose a business contract (an Apex REST endpoint shaped for the consumer) rather than raw CPQ objects, whose structure will change with upgrades.

CPQ's internal model is not an API you want partners depending on.

80 CPQ needs to work on mobile.Medium

The quote line editor is not designed for phones. Realistically, configuration happens on a laptop.

If field sales genuinely need mobile quoting, scope it to a simplified flow rather than the full editor.

81 A sandbox has no realistic quote data.Medium

Seed the golden set as part of the environment setup, so every environment can be validated the same way.

Testing CPQ with three hand-built quotes tells you very little about the behaviour that matters.

82 How do you hand over a CPQ implementation?Medium

The configuration export and migration script, the documented waterfall behaviour, the rule inventory with purposes and events, the golden set, and the proration conventions.

Without those, the next person cannot change pricing safely.

83 CPQ is slow and the vendor says it is your config.Medium

Produce evidence: rule count, quote line count, timings with rules progressively disabled, and QCP execution time.

Frequently it is the configuration — and the measurement tells you which part rather than leaving it a dispute.

84 What is the biggest CPQ deployment risk?Hard

That the configuration is data, so there is no deployment gate, no review trail and no drift detection unless you build them.

A pricing change can reach production unreviewed — which is why the golden set and the version-controlled export matter.

Judgement (85–100)

85 When is CPQ genuinely justified?Medium

Configurable bundles with options and rules, discount schedules, subscription pricing, amendments mid-term and renewals.

Standard Products, Price Books and Schedules cover simple catalogue selling — and if that is the requirement, CPQ is a large amount of machinery to operate.

86 The business wants CPQ for "better quotes".Medium

Establish what "better" means: fewer errors, faster build, better documents, or discount control. Each has a cheaper path.

CPQ brings a real operating cost — configuration-as-data, upgrades, a regression suite — which must be justified by the requirement.

87 A CPQ implementation is six months late.Hard

Usually pricing rules that were never agreed in writing, discovered one scenario at a time.

Stop building and document the intended waterfall behaviour and the scenarios with expected figures. The configuration follows quickly once the pricing intent is settled.

88 Two stakeholders describe pricing differently.Medium

That is the project risk. Resolve it with a documented waterfall and a golden set they both sign off.

Configuring both interpretations and seeing which complaints arrive is the expensive approach.

89 Should pricing logic live in CPQ or an external system?Hard

In CPQ if sales needs it during configuration. In an external pricing engine if it is genuinely complex, shared with other channels, or owned by another team.

Duplicating it in both guarantees divergence, which is the worst outcome.

90 How do you reduce CPQ complexity?Medium

Fewer rules with tighter conditions, flatter bundles, fewer price books, and configuration attributes instead of repeated questions.

Complexity accumulates one reasonable request at a time, so periodic consolidation is necessary.

91 Reps are quoting outside CPQ in spreadsheets.Medium

Because it is faster for them. Find out which part is slow — configuration time, approvals, or document generation.

Shadow quoting is a usability signal, and enforcement without fixing the cause just moves it further out of sight.

92 How do you measure whether CPQ is working?Medium

Quote build time, quote error and rework rate, discount distribution, time in approval, and quote-to-order conversion.

Baseline them before go-live, or you cannot demonstrate the benefit.

93 Discounting is out of control.Medium

Report the distribution by rep, product and deal size before changing thresholds — the pattern usually points at a specific cause.

Then use discount schedules and approval thresholds rather than blanket restrictions.

94 A new product family needs adding.Medium

Products, price book entries per book and currency, options and features if bundled, rules, and a golden-set quote to prove it.

The last step is the one skipped, and it is the one that catches the pricing mistake.

95 How do you onboard someone to CPQ?Medium

The waterfall, the event model, the rule inventory, and the golden set — in that order.

Without the event model they will put a rule at After Calculate and spend a day confused, which is the standard CPQ onboarding experience.

96 The sales process changed. What breaks?Medium

Rules conditioned on stage or channel fields, approval thresholds, and quote templates with stage-dependent content.

Review the rule inventory against the new process rather than waiting for the first wrong quote.

97 Is CPQ or standard quoting right for this org?Medium

Ask whether products are configurable, whether pricing is tiered or negotiated, and whether contracts are amended and renewed.

Two or more yeses point to CPQ. One yes usually does not justify the operating cost.

98 A CPQ consultant has left and nothing is documented.Hard

Reconstruct from the configuration: export it, inventory the rules with their events and conditions, and build a golden set from recent real quotes.

That gives you a regression baseline before you dare change anything — which is the first priority, not the documentation.

99 What is the most common CPQ mistake?Hard

Rules at the wrong calculation event — right logic, wrong moment, so it works in the editor and not on save.

Followed by not agreeing proration in writing, and treating the configuration as deployable metadata.

100 One habit that most improves CPQ work?Hard

Maintaining a golden set of quotes with expected totals, recalculated after every rule change, migration and package upgrade.

Because the configuration is data with no deployment gate, that set is the only regression test you have — and it is the difference between changing pricing confidently and changing it hopefully.
