Events, CDC & Connect — 100 Scenarios

Delivery semantics, replay and retention, publish behaviour, retries, CDC gaps, Pub/Sub, schema evolution, external objects, and the reconciliation nobody builds until it is too late.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Events, CDC & Connect and its Advanced section.

Delivery semantics (1–18)

1 A subscriber processed the same event twice.Easy

Delivery is at-least-once. Duplicates are part of the contract, not a bug.

Consumers must be idempotent — a unique key on whatever you create, and a repeated event becomes a no-op.

2 Events arrived out of order and your state is wrong.Hard

Ordering is by ReplayId within a channel, but retries and redelivery break the logical sequence — and CDC is not ordered across objects at all.

Never derive state from event order. Carry the authoritative values in the payload, or query current state.

3 Your subscriber was down for four days. What did you lose?Hard

Everything beyond the retention window (commonly 72 hours for high-volume events, 24 for some channels) — permanently, with no error anywhere.

Which is why every event-driven integration needs a reconciliation job that repairs state independently of the stream.

4 Publishing succeeded. Does that mean it was consumed?Easy

No. Publishing means the bus accepted the event. There is no delivery receipt.

If you need to know it was processed, the consumer has to tell you — a status field, a callback, or a reconciliation check.

5 Can you ask the bus "what did I miss"?Medium

Only positionally: resume from a stored ReplayId. There is no semantic "give me the changes I have not seen".

So durable storage of the last processed replay id is mandatory, and a gap beyond retention is unrecoverable from the stream.

6 Where should the replay id be stored?Medium

Durably, on the consumer side — a database row, not memory. A restart otherwise replays from the beginning or skips ahead.

Commit it only after the event is successfully processed, or you will skip events on a crash.

7 You need guaranteed exactly-once processing.Hard

Not available. What you can build is at-least-once delivery plus idempotent processing, which is operationally equivalent.

Anyone insisting on exactly-once is usually asking for idempotency without the term.

8 How long are events retained?Easy

A limited window — commonly 72 hours for high-volume platform events and shorter for some channels. Verify for your channel type and release.

Design the consumer's maximum acceptable downtime around it, and alert when the consumer falls behind.

9 Standard-volume or high-volume events?Medium

High-volume is the modern default: better scale, replay support and retention. Standard-volume is legacy with tighter limits.

Check what your org and channel support before designing around replay, since the behaviours differ.

10 How do you detect that a consumer is falling behind?Medium

Track the lag between the latest published replay id and your last processed one, and alert on it.

Rising lag is your only warning before the retention window silently eats the backlog.

11 A business owner asks whether events are reliable.Medium

Honestly: reliable for freshness, not for completeness. They give decoupling and low latency; they do not guarantee ordering, exactly-once, or delivery after a long outage.

Which is why the design pairs events with reconciliation. That framing prevents a false expectation you cannot meet.

12 What belongs in an event payload?Medium

Identifiers, a correlation id, a published timestamp, and the few fields the consumer needs to decide what to do.

Fat events become a de facto API you cannot change, and they consume your allocation faster.

13 Should events carry full record state?Medium

Generally no — it couples consumers to your schema and goes stale immediately. Send a fact plus ids, and let consumers query if they need detail.

The exception is when the consumer genuinely cannot query you (an external system with no access), where a self-contained payload is pragmatic.

14 How do you make a consumer idempotent?Medium

Derive a deterministic key from the event (correlation id, or source id plus operation), store it, and skip anything already processed.

Enforce it with a unique constraint rather than a pre-check query, which does not hold under concurrency.

15 Your reconciliation job found 40 missing records.Medium

The system working as designed — the stream had a gap and reconciliation repaired it.

Log the count as a metric: a rising number tells you the stream is degrading before anyone complains.

16 How often should reconciliation run?Medium

From the business's tolerance for divergence: nightly for most, hourly where the data drives money or operations.

Compare counts and checksums rather than re-sending everything, so the job stays cheap enough to run often.

17 Is an event stream an audit trail?Easy

No. Retention is short, delivery is at-least-once, and events can be missing.

For audit use Field Audit Trail or an external store populated deliberately. Treating the bus as a system of record is a common and expensive mistake.

18 How do you test a consumer's resilience?Medium

Deliver the same event twice, deliver events out of order, deliver one with a missing optional field, and stop the consumer for longer than the retention window.

Those four tests find almost every real event-driven defect.

Publishing & transactions (19–34)

19 Publish After Commit or Publish Immediately?Medium

After Commit for anything announcing a state change that must be real — the event only publishes if the transaction commits.

Immediately only for telemetry where you want a record of the attempt even if the transaction rolls back.

20 An event announced a change that was rolled back.Medium

The event was configured to publish immediately. Consumers acted on something that never happened.

Switch to after-commit, and make consumers tolerant — querying for current state is a good defence against this class of error.

21 Do you need to check the publish result?Easy

Yes — EventBus.publish returns Database.SaveResult, and publishing can fail (allocation, validation, size).

Ignoring it means silently losing the event, which is the worst outcome in an event-driven design.

22 What context does a platform event trigger run in?Medium

After-insert only, as the Automated Process user, in its own transaction with fresh limits and no access to the publisher's static state.

Set trace flags on that user to debug it, and pass everything needed in the payload.

23 Using events to escape mixed DML.Medium

Legitimate and common: the subscriber runs in a separate transaction, so setup and non-setup objects can be handled separately.

Just remember the subscriber is asynchronous, so the caller cannot depend on the result being immediate.

24 A subscriber threw an exception. What happens?Medium

Behaviour varies by channel: it may not be automatically redelivered, so an uncaught exception can simply lose the event.

Catch, log durably, and decide explicitly between EventBus.RetryableException and dead-lettering.

25 How do you retry inside a subscriber?Hard

Throw EventBus.RetryableException to ask the bus to redeliver, and read EventBus.TriggerContext.currentContext().retries to count attempts.

The retry limit is small, so what matters is stopping and recording rather than looping until the event is discarded.

26 Events are published from a trigger in a loop.Easy

Build a list and publish once — EventBus.publish accepts a collection.

Per-record publishing in a loop burns allocation and DML-like statement budget unnecessarily.

27 A subscriber loops with the publisher.Hard

The subscriber's write re-triggers the publishing logic. Because each hop is a separate transaction, no static guard sees it and the loop is invisible in a single debug log.

Break it with a durable marker — a source field saying "this came from the subscriber" — and a counter you can alert on.

28 Can you publish from a subscriber?Medium

Yes, and it is a legitimate choreography pattern — but it is also how cascades begin.

Carry a hop count in the payload and refuse beyond a maximum, so a mistake is bounded rather than unbounded.

29 An event-triggered flow processes events one at a time.Medium

It may receive them individually or in batches depending on configuration and volume — design for both.

Make the flow idempotent and avoid per-event DML where a collection would do, since a batch of events shares one transaction.

30 Event publishing hit a limit.Medium

Daily publish allocation, or the per-transaction publish limit. Both are real and the daily one is org-wide.

Reduce event volume (publish a summary rather than per record), consolidate subscribers, and monitor the allocation.

31 Ten subscribers consume one event. What does that cost?Hard

Delivery to each subscriber consumes allocation, so roughly ten times the consumption of one.

Consolidate: one subscriber that fans out internally, or question whether all ten genuinely need every event.

32 You need the publishing user's identity in the subscriber.Medium

Put it in the payload. The subscriber runs as Automated Process and cannot infer who published.

Same for any context you need — the subscriber has none of the publisher's transaction state.

33 Should a platform event replace a trigger?Medium

For decoupling heavy post-commit work, yes — it gives fresh limits, isolation and natural retry.

For same-record field defaults, no: a before-save flow or before trigger is immediate and far cheaper.

34 How do you debug a subscriber that never runs?Medium

Confirm the event actually published (check the SaveResult and your log), then set a trace flag on the Automated Process user — your own trace flag catches nothing.

Then check whether the trigger is active and whether an exception is being swallowed somewhere.

Change Data Capture (35–52)

35 A CDC payload is missing most fields.Easy

By design — it contains only the changed fields plus a header listing them.

If the consumer needs full state it must query, accepting that the record may have changed again since.

36 You received a gap event. What now?Hard

CDC emits a gap event when it cannot produce a full change event — very large transactions, certain operations. Ignoring it silently loses changes.

Handle it by querying the affected records (or triggering a targeted reconciliation) rather than treating it as noise.

37 A child's change arrived before its parent's.Hard

CDC is not ordered across objects. Use the transaction key in the header to group changes that happened together, or query for current state.

Designing a consumer that assumes parent-first ordering guarantees intermittent failures.

38 What is the transaction key for?Medium

It groups all change events from one Salesforce transaction, so a consumer can reassemble "these ten records changed together".

Essential for keeping a downstream replica internally consistent rather than applying changes piecemeal.

39 CDC or a platform event for a downstream replica?Medium

CDC if the consumer genuinely wants raw field-level changes and you accept exposing your schema.

A platform event if you want a stable business contract you control. For replication specifically, CDC plus reconciliation is the common answer.

40 You need CDC on 20 objects.Medium

CDC is enabled per object and the number is bounded by licence — so choose deliberately rather than enabling everything.

Each enabled object also adds event volume against your allocation.

41 A CDC consumer sees deletes it cannot interpret.Medium

The change type is in the header (create, update, delete, undelete). Handle all four explicitly.

Undelete in particular is usually unhandled, which leaves downstream systems believing a restored record is still deleted.

42 Does CDC fire for changes made by your own integration?Medium

Yes — which is how echo loops form when the consumer writes back.

Carry a source marker on the record and have the consumer ignore changes it caused, or filter on the publishing user.

43 Can you filter which CDC events are published?Medium

Channel configuration allows some filtering, which is worth using to reduce volume — but do not rely on it for security.

The consumer should still validate what it receives, and your allocation benefits from publishing less in the first place.

44 A CDC-driven replica has drifted.Hard

Expected eventually: gap events, retention, out-of-order delivery and consumer bugs all contribute.

Run a periodic full or delta reconciliation comparing counts and checksums, and repair differences. A replica with no reconciliation is a replica you cannot trust.

45 How do you test CDC logic?Medium

Test.enableChangeDataCapture() before the DML, then deliver the event bus and assert the subscriber's effect.

Also test the gap-event branch explicitly — it is the one most likely to be unimplemented.

46 CDC versus a nightly extract for a data warehouse.Medium

CDC for freshness; a nightly Bulk extract for completeness and simplicity.

Mature pipelines use both: CDC to keep current, a periodic extract to guarantee the warehouse matches. Choosing only one means accepting either staleness or drift.

47 You need to know who made the change.Easy

The CDC header includes the committing user, among other metadata.

Useful for both loop prevention and audit-style downstream processing — but remember it is the user, not the business reason.

48 CDC volume is consuming your allocation.Medium

A chatty object — bulk loads and roll-up-driven updates generate a lot of change events.

Narrow the enabled objects and fields, filter the channel, and consider whether a nightly extract would serve that object better.

49 Does CDC capture changes made by a data load?Medium

Yes, which can produce an enormous burst — and large transactions are exactly where gap events appear.

For a migration, consider disabling the channel or suspending the consumer, then reconciling afterwards rather than streaming millions of changes.

50 How do you resume a CDC consumer after downtime?Medium

From the stored replay id if it is still within retention; otherwise run a reconciliation to catch up, then resume from the current position.

Attempting to replay beyond retention silently gives you partial data.

51 A consumer must process CDC in order per record.Hard

Within a channel, replay order gives you per-record sequence in normal operation — but redelivery breaks it.

Make updates idempotent and last-write-wins by comparing a timestamp or version in the payload, rather than assuming arrival order.

52 Is CDC suitable for triggering business processes?Medium

It can be, but a platform event expressing the business fact is usually better — it is a contract you control, it carries intent, and it does not fire on every incidental field change.

CDC triggering business logic tends to produce surprising behaviour when someone edits an unrelated field.

Pub/Sub, Streaming & external consumers (53–70)

53 Pub/Sub API or CometD for an external consumer?Medium

Pub/Sub API (gRPC) — it is the current recommendation, supports flow control so a slow consumer is not drowned, uses Avro with explicit schema ids, and can publish as well as subscribe.

CometD remains what empApi uses in the browser.

54 Your consumer cannot keep up with the stream.Hard

With Pub/Sub API, use flow control — request only as many events as you can process, which is genuine backpressure.

With CometD you must keep up or fall behind, which is one of the main reasons to migrate.

55 You have run out of streaming connections.Medium

There is a bounded number of concurrent subscribers per org, and crashed consumers leave zombie connections consuming slots.

Ensure clean disconnection, monitor active subscriptions, and consolidate consumers where possible.

56 A browser component needs to react to server events.Easy

lightning/empApi — subscribe in connectedCallback and unsubscribe in disconnectedCallback, or you leak subscriptions.

Handle the error channel too, and remember ordering is not guaranteed.

57 How does Avro schema evolution work here?Medium

Each event carries a schema id the consumer resolves, so additive changes are handled gracefully if the consumer is written to tolerate them.

Removing or retyping a field is still breaking — which is the same rule as any contract.

58 You need to add a field to an existing event.Easy

Additive changes are safe. Add it, and ensure consumers ignore unknown fields.

What you cannot do is remove or retype a field — that requires a new event (Order_Placed_v2__e).

59 How do you version a platform event?Medium

In the event name, running both channels until consumers migrate. There is no in-place versioning.

Log usage of the old channel so you know who is still subscribed before you retire it.

60 Never reuse a field name for a different meaning.Easy

Correct — a consumer you forgot about is still reading it and will silently misinterpret the new semantics.

Add a new field instead. Renaming semantics without renaming the field is one of the nastiest integration bugs because nothing errors.

61 Relaying events to Kafka or EventBridge.Medium

Supported patterns exist (event relay, or a Pub/Sub API consumer bridging into your bus), which is usually the right architecture for an enterprise estate.

Then your idempotency and ordering concerns move to the bridge — they do not disappear.

62 An external consumer needs to publish into Salesforce.Medium

Pub/Sub API supports publishing, with batching — which is cleaner than having them call a REST endpoint that then publishes.

Still validate in the subscriber: an inbound event is untrusted input like any other payload.

63 How do you secure an event channel?Medium

Through the subscribing user's permissions — access to the event object and the channel. Use a dedicated least-privilege integration user per consumer.

And keep sensitive data out of payloads, because an event is visible to anyone who can subscribe to its channel.

64 Should PII go in an event payload?Hard

Prefer not to. Events are retained on the bus, delivered to every subscriber, and frequently logged by consumers — creating copies with weaker controls.

Send identifiers and let authorised consumers fetch the detail through an API that enforces access.

65 How do you monitor end-to-end event latency?Medium

Include a published timestamp in the payload and record the processing time at the consumer, then report the difference.

The platform will not tell you this, and "events feel slow" is unactionable without the number.

66 A consumer's dead letters are piling up.Medium

Something systematic — a schema change, a permission change, or a data shape you did not anticipate.

Alert on dead-letter volume, not just existence, and make replay a one-click operation once the cause is fixed.

67 You need to fan one event out to several internal processes.Medium

One subscriber that dispatches internally is usually cheaper than several subscribers, because delivery allocation is per subscriber.

It also gives you one place to handle errors, log and reconcile.

68 Events or a callout for notifying one system?Medium

A callout is simpler and gives you an immediate result and explicit retry control. Events are better when there may be more consumers later, or when you want to decouple deployment.

For exactly one consumer with a stable contract, a callout from an async context is often the honest choice.

69 How do you replay events for a new consumer?Medium

Subscribe from the earliest available replay id — but that only reaches back to the retention window.

For historical data, a one-off extract is the answer; the stream is for current changes, not backfill.

70 A consumer needs events only for certain records.Medium

Filter in the channel configuration where supported, or filter in the consumer — but prefer publishing a narrower event in the first place.

Publishing everything and filtering at the edge wastes your allocation on events nobody wants.

Salesforce Connect & external objects (71–84)

71 When is Salesforce Connect the right answer?Medium

When the data must stay in the source system — volume, residency, or it changes constantly — and users need to see it in context.

You avoid storage and sync entirely. The cost is that the remote system's availability and latency become yours.

72 A page using external objects is slow.Medium

Every view is a live remote query. If the remote system is slow, the page is slow, and you have little control.

Reduce the fields and rows displayed, and consider caching a summary locally for anything on a high-traffic page.

73 You cannot report on external objects as expected.Medium

Reporting support is limited and differs from standard objects — joins, roll-ups and some filters are unavailable.

If reporting is a core requirement, replication into Salesforce or a warehouse is usually the better architecture.

74 External object relationships behave oddly.Medium

External lookups and indirect lookups have their own semantics — indirect lookups match on an External Id, which must be unique and populated.

Get the key strategy right first; relationship problems here are almost always key problems.

75 Can you write to an external object?Medium

Writeable external objects are supported with the right adapter and remote capabilities, but behaviour and error handling differ from local DML.

Validate the write path thoroughly — partial failures and remote validation errors surface differently than expected.

76 The remote system is down. What do users see?Medium

Errors where the external data would be. Design the page so a failure degrades gracefully rather than breaking the whole record.

That availability coupling is the main reason to choose replication over virtualisation for critical data.

77 Sharing for external objects.Hard

The sharing model is limited compared with standard objects — access is largely governed by the connection and object permissions rather than record-level sharing.

If per-record access control is a requirement, virtualisation is probably the wrong approach.

78 OData v2 or v4?Easy

v4 where the remote system supports it — better query capability and type handling.

The decision is usually made by the remote system; confirm what it exposes before designing around a capability it does not have.

79 A custom adapter is needed.Hard

The Apex Connector Framework lets you build one for a non-OData source — powerful and a real maintenance commitment.

Before committing, weigh it against a nightly extract or a middleware-provided OData facade, which are frequently cheaper.

80 Cross-org Salesforce Connect.Medium

Lets one org surface another's data as external objects — useful in a multi-org estate for read-mostly reference data.

Same caveats apply: latency, reporting limits and availability coupling between the two orgs.

81 Virtualise or replicate — how do you choose?Hard

Virtualise when the data is large, changes constantly, must not be copied, and is viewed occasionally in context. Replicate when you need reporting, automation, offline access or reliable performance.

Write the decision and its reason down — this one gets revisited and relitigated repeatedly.

82 Users complain external data is missing.Medium

Check the connection credentials, the remote system's filters, and whether the indirect lookup key is populated on both sides.

Missing data in a virtualised model is usually a key-matching problem rather than an access problem.

83 How do you monitor an external object connection?Medium

Synthetic checks: a scheduled job that queries a known record and alerts on failure or slow response.

Otherwise you learn about an outage from users, which for a virtualised integration is the normal outcome.

84 Can you use external objects in flows and Apex?Medium

To a degree, with restrictions — and every access is a remote call, so loops over external objects are expensive and fragile.

Treat them as read-mostly UI surfaces rather than as a foundation for automation.

Judgement (85–100)

85 Request-reply or event-driven for a new integration?Medium

Request-reply when the caller needs the answer to proceed. Event-driven when the producer should not care who consumes, or when you want decoupling and resilience.

Event-driven costs you ordering, delivery guarantees and easy debugging — which must be bought back with idempotency and reconciliation.

86 The business wants "event-driven architecture".Medium

Ask what problem it solves for them: decoupling teams, reducing point-to-point integrations, or freshness. Then design for that specific benefit.

Adopting events as a style rather than for a reason produces a distributed system with all the costs and none of the payoff.

87 How do you explain at-least-once to a stakeholder?Easy

"A message may arrive twice, so we make processing it twice harmless. That is cheaper and more reliable than trying to guarantee it arrives exactly once."

Framing it as a design choice rather than a limitation usually ends the conversation.

88 A new consumer wants to subscribe to your event.Medium

Treat it as a contract change: review the payload for anything they should not see, check your allocation can absorb the extra delivery, and document them as a consumer.

Undocumented subscribers are why you cannot safely change an event later.

89 You need to change an event's payload.Medium

Additive only, or a new versioned event with both running. And you need to know who the consumers are — which requires having tracked them.

This is why thin payloads matter: a small contract is one you can evolve.

90 How do you decide the reconciliation strategy?Hard

From the cost of divergence. Financial data gets frequent checksums; a marketing preference gets a nightly count comparison.

Also decide who is notified and what they do — a reconciliation report nobody reads is not reconciliation.

91 An event-driven design has become hard to debug.Hard

Expected without discipline. Add correlation ids end to end, log publish and process with timestamps, document every publisher and subscriber, and keep payloads thin.

Distributed systems are debuggable only to the extent you instrumented them in advance.

92 A consumer team wants a field you consider internal.Medium

Push back and understand the requirement — frequently they want a derived fact, not the raw field.

Publishing an internal field makes it a contract forever. Publishing a named business fact leaves you free to change the implementation.

93 How do you stop event volume growing uncontrollably?Medium

Publish business facts rather than every change, filter channels, consolidate subscribers, and review the top publishers monthly.

Also check whether bulk loads are generating bursts nobody intended — that is the usual cause of a sudden step-change.

94 You inherit an event-driven integration with no reconciliation.Hard

Build reconciliation first, before changing anything — it will tell you how much divergence already exists, which is usually more than anyone expects.

Then add monitoring for lag and dead letters, and document the publishers and subscribers.

95 Should you use events inside a single org?Medium

Yes, for decoupling post-commit work, escaping mixed DML, and getting fresh limits — all legitimate and common.

But not as a substitute for a direct call where a direct call is simpler; indirection has a debugging cost.

96 How do you document an event?Easy

Name, meaning (the business fact it asserts), payload fields with types and semantics, publish behaviour, known consumers, and the version history.

Undocumented events become folklore, and folklore cannot be changed.

97 A subscriber is modifying records the publisher owns.Medium

A likely loop and an ownership-of-data problem. Establish which system owns which fields, and have the subscriber write only what it owns.

Carry a source marker so each side can ignore changes it caused.

98 What is the most under-built part of event-driven designs?Hard

Reconciliation, followed by dead-letter handling and lag monitoring. Teams build the happy path and treat the gap as hypothetical.

It is not hypothetical — the stream will have a hole in it, and "the event was lost" is not an explanation the business accepts.

99 How do you explain the design to an architect reviewing it?Hard

State the semantics you rely on and the defences you built: at-least-once so consumers are idempotent; no ordering so state is carried in the payload; bounded retention so there is reconciliation; retries capped with a dead letter; correlation ids throughout.

That sequence demonstrates you designed for the failure modes rather than the demo.

100 One habit that most improves event-driven work?Hard

Building the reconciliation job at the same time as the publisher — not later, not when there is an incident.

It forces you to define what "consistent" means, it catches bugs during development, and it is the only thing that makes an eventually-consistent design defensible.
