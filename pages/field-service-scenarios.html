Field Service — 100 Scenarios

The object model in practice, scheduling and dispatch, the mobile app, parts and assets, and the day a technician cannot do their job — framed as situations rather than configuration.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Field Service, with the deep material on Field Service — Advanced.

The object model (1–20)

1 Someone is trying to schedule a Work Order.Easy

They have misunderstood the model. A Work Order is what needs doing; a Service Appointment is a visit to do some of it.

The scheduling engine, the Gantt and the mobile app all operate on Service Appointments.

2 A job needs three visits.Easy

One Work Order with three Service Appointments — diagnose, return with the part, sign off — each scheduled independently, possibly to different engineers.

That separation is the whole reason both objects exist.

3 Where do required skills come from?Easy

Usually the Work Type, which acts as a template: default duration, required skills, and the appointment window.

Getting Work Types right is the highest-leverage configuration in Field Service, because everything downstream inherits them.

4 Durations are consistently wrong.Medium

The Work Type's estimated duration does not match reality, and every plan inherits that error.

Compare estimated against actual times from the mobile app and recalibrate — bad durations poison scheduling, travel and capacity.

5 What does Assigned Resource do?Easy

It is the junction linking a Service Resource to a Service Appointment — which is how one appointment can have more than one resource.

Reporting on "who did what" goes through Assigned Resource, not through a field on the appointment.

6 A resource does not appear anywhere.Medium

Check in order: is the Service Resource active, is it linked to a User, and is it a Service Territory Member with current effective dates.

Expired territory membership is the single most common cause of a resource vanishing from the Gantt.

7 What is a Service Territory actually for?Medium

It is the unit of scheduling, optimisation runs, the Gantt and usually sharing — a geography with operating hours, whose members are the resources.

Its shape therefore sets your performance ceiling and your dispatcher workload.

8 Operating Hours exist in several places.Medium

Deliberately: territory hours define when work can be scheduled; account hours define when the customer can be visited; and appointment windows derive from the ones designated for booking.

They can conflict, which is why the visiting-hours rule matters.

9 An engineer's holiday did not block scheduling.Easy

No Resource Absence, or it was created after the appointment was already scheduled.

Absences are frequently fed from an HR integration, which means a day's plan can evaporate without anyone in Salesforce doing anything.

10 Preventive maintenance visits are needed.Medium

A Maintenance Plan on the asset (or account/location) with a frequency and a Work Type, generating Work Orders ahead of time.

Those then flow into scheduling like any other job — the value is shifting from reactive to planned.

11 Maintenance Work Orders did not generate.Medium

Work through it: the plan's date range and next-suggested date, the generation horizon (it may simply not be time), whether the batch is scheduled and completing, and whether the covered assets are active.

It is a background job and it fails quietly.

12 Where do parts fit in the model?Medium

Product Items are stock at a Location (a van is a location that moves); Products Consumed records what was used on the job and feeds billing.

Product Requests and Transfers move stock; Return Orders bring it back.

13 A Service Report is required for sign-off.Easy

Template-driven, generated on the mobile app with signature capture, then synced.

Design the template with the content the customer and the business actually need — it is the artefact of the visit.

14 Two engineers are needed at once.Medium

A Crew if the same people always work together — one schedulable resource, far easier to optimise.

Complex work dependencies (same start) when the pairing varies or you need two different specialists.

15 How does a Case become a Work Order?Easy

Usually automation or an action on the Case, using a Work Type that sets duration and skills — with the entitlement and SLA checked first.

That SLA should then become the appointment's Due Date, or scheduling has no way to honour it.

16 An appointment has no arrival window.Medium

It derives from operating hours designated for booking, via the Work Type or the booking process.

Without one, the customer has no promise and the engine has no customer-facing constraint to respect.

17 What are the appointment time fields?Medium

Earliest Start Permitted and Due Date are the hard edges; Arrival Window is the customer promise; Scheduled Start/End is the engine's plan; Actual Start/End is what happened.

Squeeze the first two together and you get "no candidates" regardless of availability.

18 Custom appointment statuses are being added.Hard

Fine — but every one must map to the correct status category, because the engine, Gantt and mobile app all key off the category rather than your label.

A wrong category produces appointments optimisation will not move, or work that never reaches a device.

19 Which licences do you need?Medium

Dispatcher, technician (plus mobile), and a scheduling/optimisation entitlement with a nominated running user.

Contractors are separate again. Getting this wrong is a common reason a go-live slips.

20 What is the one distinction to get right?Easy

Work Order versus Service Appointment. Everything else follows from understanding that one is the work and the other is a visit to do part of it.

If someone is scheduling Work Orders, stop and fix the model understanding first.

Scheduling & dispatch (21–46)

21 "No candidates found." Walk your diagnosis.Hard

In order: skills (level and expiry), territory membership dates, operating hours and shifts including time zone, absences and existing bookings, capacity, Match Fields rules, then the Earliest Start to Due Date window.

Compare against a stripped-down diagnostic policy to bisect which rule is excluding everyone.

22 Candidates appear but the wrong engineer is chosen.Medium

That is objectives, not rules. Rules decide who is allowed; objectives decide which allowed slot wins.

Check preferred-resource and skill-level objective weights particularly.

23 Work rules versus service objectives.Easy

Work rules are hard constraints — fail one and the resource disappears. Service objectives are weighted preferences ranking the survivors.

Too many hard rules and nothing schedules; too few and you send an unqualified engineer 200 km.

24 A customer was booked at 03:00.Medium

The visiting-hours rule is missing from the policy — that rule enforces the customer's allowed hours, not the engineer's.

Then check the account's operating hours and the time zones.

25 A booking is an hour out from what the customer was told.Hard

Time zones. Operating hours, the territory, the resource's user record, the customer's account and the dispatcher's browser can all differ.

Write down all five before touching a rule — that resolves most of these cases.

26 What does a candidate's grade mean?Hard

A weighted sum of that policy's objectives normalised to 0–100. It is not a probability and not comparable across policies.

And a cluster of near-identical grades means your objectives are not discriminating at all.

27 How many scheduling policies should you have?Medium

One per intent — routine, emergency, contractor fill, optimisation — plus a diagnostic policy with rules stripped for debugging.

One policy with compromise weights serves none of those intents well.

28 Which policy actually applied?Medium

It depends on the entry point: the console's selection, the booking component's parameter, the recipe's named policy, or the optimisation request's — which is frequently a different one.

Check the entry point before investigating the policy.

29 A policy Id is hard-coded in Apex.Easy

It will break in every other org and after every sandbox refresh, because policies are records.

Resolve by name at runtime, with the name in Custom Metadata.

30 Travel time is far higher than expected.Hard

In order: objective weights (is travel actually beating ASAP and preferred resource), pinned and dispatched appointments, territory overlap, arrival windows too narrow, wrong home base, horizon too small, and travel settings.

Calibrate travel before tuning weights, or you are tuning against a broken model.

31 Engineers have stopped following the schedule.Hard

Usually travel estimates that do not match reality — they fall behind by mid-morning and quietly go their own way.

That failure is silent, because nobody raises a ticket saying "the plan is optimistic". Compare estimated travel against actual start times.

32 Optimisation runs and changes almost nothing.Hard

Check the optimisation request record for status and result, then how many appointments are pinned or already dispatched.

Dispatchers pinning everything is the most common cause — and then they report that optimisation "stopped working".

33 What does pinning do?Medium

Tells optimisation never to move that appointment. Legitimate for genuine customer commitments; corrosive when used habitually.

Report on pinned counts weekly and train dispatchers on what it costs.

34 Which optimisation run type do you need?Medium

Global for the nightly re-plan; in-day for recovering a bad morning; resource schedule for one messy route; reshuffle for an emergency; fill-in for a gap; group nearby for squeezing value from a trip.

They are different tools with different scopes.

35 A dispatcher needs to insert an emergency job.Medium

Reshuffle from the console with an emergency policy, which moves lower-priority work aside.

Define in advance what may be bumped and who is allowed to do it, or the emergency path becomes the normal path.

36 The optimisation running user matters. Why?Hard

Optimisation executes as a nominated user, so its record access limits what it can see and move.

A narrow running user silently optimises a smaller world than you think — the output looks merely disappointing rather than broken.

37 Fifteen short jobs are at one site.Medium

Appointment bundling — one visit, one arrival promise, scheduled and dispatched as a single appointment.

Decide the unbundling behaviour before go-live: what happens to member statuses and actual times when a bundle is broken mid-visit.

38 A survey must precede the install.Medium

Complex work dependencies (start after finish), and optionally same-resource if continuity matters.

The dependency rule must be in both the scheduling and optimisation policies, or overnight optimisation will undo your sequence.

39 A three-day installation needs scheduling.Medium

A multiday appointment with the multiday rule enabled, spread across consecutive working days.

Optimisation treats these as heavy blocks, and they frequently explain why a territory's plan looks poor.

40 Contractors tell you capacity, not hours.Medium

Capacity-based resources with Service Resource Capacity records and the capacity work rule.

Capacity is consumed by scheduled work, so cancellations must release it or contractors silently go "full".

41 How should appointments be released to technicians?Medium

Auto-dispatch per territory within a horizon, or drip feed releasing only the next few.

Dispatch too early and the plan freezes before the day settles; too late and technicians start blind.

42 Appointments keep rescheduling themselves.Hard

An automation loop: a trigger updates the appointment, a recipe reschedules it, the reschedule fires the trigger again.

Because the engine is asynchronous, this is invisible in a single debug log. Guard with explicit conditions and never react to engine-written fields.

43 Should scheduling be automated?Medium

Eventually — but prove it manually first. If dispatchers do not trust the candidate grades, no automated scheduling will be accepted.

Then enable one recipe at a time, measuring against the baseline.

44 Never call the scheduling API from a trigger. Why?Medium

It is slow, effectively a round trip to the engine, and cannot be bulkified — so a 200-record load blows CPU and a retry corrupts the plan.

Use a recipe, a platform event or a Queueable instead.

45 The Gantt is unusably slow.Medium

Territory size. Gantt rendering and candidate calculation grow superlinearly with resources × appointments.

Split into territories a dispatcher can actually hold in their head, and use a hierarchy for reporting.

46 What single change most improves scheduling quality?Hard

Calibrating travel so the engine's estimates match reality — because every decision it makes is built on them.

Until that is true, objective tuning is tuning against fiction.

The mobile app (47–70)

47 A technician completed eleven jobs in a basement and lost them.Hard

Validation fires at sync time, not edit time. A required field or rule they could not satisfy offline rejected the upload into a sync-error queue.

Audit every validation rule and required field for offline-satisfiability, and move unsatisfiable checks server side.

48 What determines what is available offline?Medium

Offline priming configuration: which objects, related lists and date range are downloaded.

More priming means longer sync, more storage and staler data — tighten the date window aggressively.

49 A technician sees blank screens on a record.Medium

Check record access in the browser as that user first. If a related list is empty there too, it is sharing — not the app.

That one check saves days of debugging the wrong layer.

50 What can mobile flows do offline?Medium

Screen flows on the device with restricted element support: no callouts, no server-side Apex, and Get Records only against primed data.

Which means guided procedures work offline and anything needing a server does not.

51 A technician needs to launch a manufacturer's diagnostic app.Medium

App extensions — launch another app, a URL, a flow or a component from the appointment.

That is how you integrate a meter-reading or diagnostic tool without rebuilding it in Salesforce.

52 How does conflict resolution work?Hard

The app generally uploads changed fields, which limits genuine collisions — but it does not eliminate them on the same field.

Decide what happens when the office edited a record the technician also changed, rather than discovering the default behaviour.

53 Post-sync automation ran twice.Hard

Records can arrive twice, late, and out of order. Post-sync logic must be idempotent and order-tolerant.

Guard with your own processed flag, key off status category rather than label, and make re-running a no-op.

54 Actual end time is before actual start time.Medium

Clock skew or manual entry — and it will happen. Post-sync logic must tolerate it rather than throwing.

Validate and flag for review instead of blocking the sync, which would strand the technician's work.

55 Initial sync takes fifteen minutes.Medium

Over-priming. Narrow the objects, related lists and date window — most technicians need days of data, not months.

It also reduces device storage and battery consumption.

56 Technicians are not using the app.Medium

Usually too many taps, fields they do not need, or it failing where they have no signal.

Watch three technicians do a real job. The fix is almost always fewer fields and a guided flow for the task.

57 How do you test offline properly?Medium

On a real device, in genuine aeroplane mode, with a full day's work queued — then reconnect and inspect the sync-error queue.

Emulators and "turn off wifi" do not reproduce the sync queue behaviour.

58 Parts consumed are not reaching van stock.Medium

Products Consumed should decrement the Product Item at the van's Location — check the location is set on the resource and the stock exists there.

Without it, van stock becomes fiction and the next job's parts check is meaningless.

59 A signature is required but the customer is absent.Medium

Design the exception path: a "customer unavailable" option that still allows completion with a reason, rather than a hard requirement.

A mandatory signature with no alternative is a rule technicians will work around.

60 Push notifications are not arriving.Medium

Notification configuration, device permissions, or the status change not being one that triggers them.

They matter most if you drip-feed, since the technician has no other signal that new work arrived.

61 Location tracking is proposed.Medium

It feeds travel estimation, customer tracking and "who is nearest" decisions — and it is a works-council and privacy conversation.

Have that conversation early rather than enabling it and explaining afterwards.

62 An LWC is needed in the mobile app.Hard

Supported, with offline data access through the GraphQL wire adapter and device capabilities such as barcode scanning.

Verify current targets and offline-supported adapters in the mobile developer guide for your release rather than assuming.

63 Service reports must be generated offline.Medium

They are generated on the device and synced, which is why the template must not depend on server-side data.

Test the report on a device with no signal, with realistic content lengths.

64 A technician needs a knowledge article on site.Easy

Attach articles to the Work Type so they are primed and available offline.

Which only works if the knowledge base is maintained — unfindable or outdated articles are worse than none.

65 Sync errors are accumulating unnoticed.Hard

Nobody is monitoring the queue. Build a report and an alert on sync errors by technician.

A technician's whole day can sit in that queue, and they will not notice until tomorrow.

66 A field must be editable offline but validated centrally.Medium

Accept it on the device, then validate server side after sync — raising a task or flagging the record rather than rejecting the upload.

That is the general pattern for anything that cannot be checked offline.

67 What should never be on the mobile critical path?Medium

Anything requiring a callout, server-side Apex or real-time validation — it will fail in a basement or a rural site.

Keep on-device logic to mobile flows and simple field updates, and do the heavy processing after sync.

68 Battery drain is a complaint.Medium

Location polling frequency and large priming volumes are the usual contributors.

Tune both. A technician whose device dies at 15:00 cannot complete the day's work, which costs more than the data freshness.

69 How do you roll out a mobile change safely?Medium

A pilot group of technicians on real jobs, with the sync-error queue monitored, before a wider release.

Mobile problems surface in the field rather than in testing, so the pilot is the test.

70 What is the commonest mobile mistake?Hard

Validation rules and required fields that cannot be satisfied offline — which strand a day's work in a queue nobody watches.

Followed by over-priming, and post-sync automation that is not idempotent.

Parts, assets & the business (71–88)

71 An engineer arrived without the part.Hard

There is no out-of-the-box rule for "only schedule to someone carrying the part". The practical patterns: do not release the appointment until parts are confirmed; stamp a field from your own check and use a Match Fields rule; or accept the second visit.

Most organisations do the third while claiming the first. Decide explicitly.

72 First-time fix rate is the business's main metric.Medium

Then the parts chain and the skills matching are where the work is — not scheduling efficiency.

Measure the reasons for repeat visits; usually parts and diagnosis dominate, and both are addressable upstream.

73 Van stock does not match reality.Medium

Products Consumed not being recorded, transfers not received, or returns not processed.

Without accurate Product Items, every parts decision is guesswork — and technicians stop trusting the system.

74 How does a part get to a van?Medium

Product Required on the job signals demand; a Product Request sources it; a Product Transfer (with a Shipment if in transit) moves it; the van's Product Item updates.

Each step is a record, and a gap anywhere breaks the chain.

75 Faulty parts need returning.Easy

Return Orders and line items — unused, faulty, or for warranty and repair.

Design the process, because unreturned stock sits on vans indefinitely and distorts both inventory and cost.

76 Serialised equipment needs tracking.Medium

Individually, as Assets — not as quantities of Product Items.

That is also what enables warranty, maintenance plans and service history per unit.

77 Asset history is empty.Medium

Assets are not being created from orders or fulfilment, or work orders are not being related to them.

An empty asset base blocks warranty, maintenance and condition-based service — it is foundational rather than optional.

78 Warranty should determine what the customer pays.Medium

Warranty terms define coverage and the customer's share of parts, labour and travel, flowing into work order pricing.

Without them, every warranty question becomes a manual judgement.

79 A chiller is inside a plant room inside a building.Easy

Asset hierarchy — parent/child assets, so faults and history roll up meaningfully.

It also lets a maintenance plan cover a structure rather than a list of unrelated units.

80 Maintenance should be driven by usage, not the calendar.Medium

Criteria-based maintenance using readings held on the Asset — which requires a reliable feed of those readings.

No feed, no plan. Condition-based via telemetry is the next step up, and needs real integration.

81 Where does the SLA come from?Medium

The entitlement or service contract, with milestones tracked on the work order and Business Hours defining what "four hours" means.

And it must be translated into the appointment's Due Date, or scheduling cannot honour it.

82 SLAs are breaching while every rule passes.Hard

Two clocks: the SLA runs on the entitlement's Business Hours; scheduling runs on territory operating hours in the territory's zone.

Reconcile them, and make the SLA an explicit constraint on the appointment rather than a parallel measure.

83 Billing needs completed work.Medium

Work order line items price from a price book and roll up to the work order; consumed products and labour feed the invoice.

Usually an integration to ERP or Revenue Cloud — define which system owns the invoice and the tax.

84 Payroll needs technician hours.Medium

Time Sheets and Time Sheet Entries capturing work, travel and breaks per resource per period.

Entry types matter — and so do actual times from the app, which is why accurate completion data has a payroll consequence as well as an operational one.

85 Customers want to know when the engineer will arrive.Medium

Real-time tracking and reminders — the single biggest CSAT lever in field service, and it cuts no-access failures.

It needs location from the app, honest travel estimates and a privacy position.

86 No-access failures are wasting visits.Medium

Confirmation and reminder messaging before the visit, and self-service rescheduling so customers move the appointment rather than missing it.

Pure wasted travel is the cheapest cost to eliminate.

87 Where is the money in field service?Hard

Ranked: avoid the visit (remote resolution, better triage), fix it first time (skills and parts), cut travel (territories, bundling, optimisation), then shave minutes on site.

Teams start at the last one because it feels technical, then wonder why the business case never lands.

88 Remote assistance is proposed.Medium

A video call with annotation frequently resolves the job with no visit at all — the cheapest visit is the one you avoid.

It needs agent training more than technology, which is where such initiatives usually fail.

Rollout & judgement (89–100)

89 Where do you start a Field Service implementation?Hard

The operating model on paper: territories, shifts, skills, who may do what, and the three numbers the business will judge you on (first-time fix, SLA, travel).

Then Work Types with honest durations — because every downstream plan inherits them.

90 Why calibrate travel before tuning objectives?Hard

Because every scheduling decision is built on travel estimates. If they are wrong, the plan is fiction and your tuning is tuning against it.

Compare a week of estimates against actual start times, then tune.

91 Dispatchers do not trust the system.Medium

Usually candidate grades that do not reflect their judgement, or travel estimates they know are wrong.

Prove it manually first — if dispatchers do not trust the grades, no automation will be accepted.

92 The deployment is missing all the configuration.Hard

Because scheduling policies, work rules, objectives, territories, operating hours and shift patterns are records, not metadata.

You need a repeatable, version-controlled data migration — and never hard-code the resulting Ids.

93 How do you test a scheduling change?Hard

A sandbox with realistic geography, resources, skills and a full week of appointments — run the actual policies and inspect the plan.

Keep that as a regression set and re-run it after every policy change and package upgrade.

94 You cannot unit-test the optimiser.Medium

Correct — so do not pretend to. Unit-test your own handlers, guards and idempotency; scenario-test the engine's behaviour.

Conflating the two produces tests that pass and a plan that is wrong.

95 What should you instrument from day one?Medium

Unscheduled count, travel minutes, SLA attainment, first-time fix, overtime, pinned appointments and sync errors.

Review weekly and tune from evidence. Without the baseline you cannot show the programme worked.

96 Is Field Service the right product at all?Medium

If nobody drives anywhere and nothing gets fitted, it is a large amount of machinery you will pay for and not use.

Salesforce Scheduler covers appointments with people. Field Service is for sending people to places.

97 The business wants everything automated at go-live.Medium

Sequence it: manual scheduling first to build trust, then one recipe at a time measured against a baseline, then optimisation piloted on one territory.

Automating before dispatchers trust the grades produces a system they fight rather than use.

98 A contractor population needs onboarding.Medium

Capacity-based resources, Experience Cloud access with Sharing Sets or contractor licences, and sharing tested with a real contractor login.

Under-sharing shows up as blank screens on their device, which looks like a mobile bug for days.

99 What is the commonest Field Service mistake?Hard

Uncalibrated travel, followed by custom statuses mapped to the wrong category, and configuration treated as deployable metadata.

All three produce behaviour that looks like a platform problem and is a configuration one.

100 One habit that most improves Field Service work?Hard

Comparing estimated against actual — travel, durations, arrival times — every week, and correcting the model.

Scheduling quality is downstream of estimate accuracy. Everything else is tuning parameters against numbers that may not be true.
