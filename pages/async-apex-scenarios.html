Asynchronous Apex — 100 Scenarios

Choosing the right async tool, batch internals, chaining, finalizers, idempotency, limits that are org-wide, and monitoring work that nobody is watching.

📚
How to use this Answer out loud in ninety seconds, then open the answer. Concepts on Asynchronous Apex and its Advanced section.

Choosing the right tool (1–14)

1 You need one callout after a record saves.Easy

A Queueable enqueued from the trigger, or a platform event with a subscriber. You cannot call out from a trigger, and you cannot call out after DML in the same transaction.

Queueable is preferable to @future for new code: it returns a monitorable job Id, takes complex types, chains, and supports finalizers.

2 You need to process 8 million records overnight.Easy

Batch Apex with a QueryLocator — it streams rows outside the 50,000-row transaction limit and chunks them for you.

Tune scope to the work per record: 200 is the default, but drop to single digits if each record does a callout or heavy computation.

3 You need stage A, then B, then C, each potentially long.Medium

A Queueable chain: each link does one stage and enqueues the next, carrying small state in instance fields rather than statics.

Persist progress durably if the sequence matters across failures — a chain that dies between links leaves no evidence unless you wrote some.

4 You need the same work to run every night at 02:00.Easy

Schedulable Apex, scheduled with a cron expression — usually a thin class whose execute immediately starts a batch.

Keep the schedulable trivial: its own limits are modest, and separating scheduling from processing makes the processing testable on its own.

5 Fan out independent work across many consumers.Medium

Platform events: publish once, let subscribers process in parallel in their own transactions with fresh limits.

Beware the allocation cost — delivery to each subscriber consumes allocation, so ten subscribers multiply consumption — and never assume ordering.

6 @future or Queueable?Easy

Queueable for anything new. @future cannot take sObjects or complex types, returns no job Id, cannot chain, and has no finalizer support.

Keep @future only in existing code, or when you specifically want a simple fire-and-forget with no monitoring.

7 Work must finish before the user's next action.Medium

Then it cannot be async — async offers no timing guarantee. Either do it synchronously (within limits) or change the UX to reflect reality: show a pending state and refresh when complete.

Promising "it will be done in a second" with async work is how you get support tickets about data that is merely late.

8 You need to process a 200MB file.Hard

Not in Apex. Heap is 12MB async, so the file cannot be loaded. Options: Bulk API from the client, middleware doing the transformation, or chunked upload where each chunk is processed separately.

If the file must land in Salesforce first, store it and process in slices with an offset you persist.

9 An integration needs a callback when processing finishes.Medium

Do the work in Batch Apex and make the callout from finish(), or publish a completion platform event the integration subscribes to.

finish() runs once after all chunks, which makes it the natural completion hook — including for reporting partial failures.

10 Which async option gives you the most limit headroom?Medium

All async contexts get 60s CPU, 12MB heap and 200 queries — but row and DML-row limits do not increase, which is the detail people get wrong.

Batch Apex is the only option that genuinely escapes the row limit, because the locator streams rather than loading.

11 You need parallelism, not just asynchrony.Hard

Several batch jobs partitioned by a hash of the Id, or multiple Queueables enqueued at once — co-ordinated by a controller that knows when all have finished.

Watch record contention: parallel jobs touching children of the same parents will fight over locks, and you may finish slower than serial. See skew.

12 Work must be retried automatically on failure.Medium

Finalizer-based retry for Queueables (with an attempt cap), or EventBus.RetryableException for platform event subscribers.

Both need a limit and a dead-letter destination. Unbounded retry consumes your daily async allocation and turns one failure into an outage.

13 A nightly job must not overlap with itself.Medium

Check for an existing in-flight job before starting: query AsyncApexJob for Processing/Queued instances of the class and exit if one exists.

Without that, a job that runs long gets a second instance the next night, and two copies processing the same records produce duplicate side effects.

14 The business asks for "real-time" processing of 50,000 daily records.Medium

Decompose the requirement: 50,000 a day is less than one a second, so event-driven processing within seconds is achievable and batch would not be.

Platform events per record with an idempotent subscriber, plus a nightly reconciliation to catch anything the stream missed.

Batch Apex (15–32)

15 A batch job fails at 95% after four hours.Hard

Design so that cannot waste the run: persist a high-water mark so a re-run resumes, and check remaining limits mid-chunk so the job re-enqueues the remainder instead of dying.

Also use partial-success DML with an error log so one bad record costs that record, not the chunk.

16 One chunk fails. Does the job stop?Easy

No — remaining chunks continue, and the job completes with an error count. That is usually desirable but means silent partial failure.

finish() must report it: read AsyncApexJob.NumberOfErrors and your own error log, and notify someone. A job that "completed" having failed 400 records is the dangerous case.

17 Your batch needs state across chunks.Medium

Database.Stateful preserves instance variables between execute calls — but it serialises the whole instance between chunks, so keep the state tiny (counters, not collections).

For anything substantial, persist to a record instead; stateful batches with large state are slow and can exceed serialisation limits.

18 You need to batch over data a query cannot produce.Medium

Iterable<SObject> instead of a QueryLocator — but it loads into heap, so cap the size deliberately.

Common for parsing a file or wrapping an API result. If the source is large, chunk at the source rather than building one huge iterable.

19 Each record needs a callout. What scope?Medium

Implement Database.AllowsCallouts and use a very small scope (1–10), because callout limits are per execution, not per job.

If the remote API supports batching, send many records per call instead — that is usually an order of magnitude faster and kinder to their rate limits.

20 Batch job throws on the first chunk with a query timeout.Hard

The start() query itself is unselective. The locator still has to resolve the query plan, so an unindexed filter over tens of millions of rows can time out before any chunk runs.

Lead with an indexed selective filter, or chunk by Id range (PK-chunking style) across multiple job runs.

21 Two batch jobs on related data deadlock.Hard

They lock the same parents in different orders. Deadlock is an ordering problem.

Sort the query by parent Id so chunks group children of the same parent, reduce scope, and consider serialising the jobs rather than running them concurrently.

22 How do you test a batch job?Easy

Inside Test.startTest()/stopTest(), call Database.executeBatch with a scope — one execute runs per chunk within the test.

Assert the data outcome, and assert finish() behaviour separately by calling it directly or by checking its side effects.

23 A batch job must process newest records first.Easy

Add ORDER BY CreatedDate DESC to the start() query. Ordering is respected, which matters when you may not finish the whole set.

If order matters because of dependencies between records, batch is usually the wrong tool — consider a sequenced chain.

24 You need to abort a running batch.Easy

System.abortJob(jobId), or the Apex Jobs page. Chunks already committed stay committed — there is no rollback.

Which is why long batches should write progress and be safely re-runnable: aborting mid-way must leave a recoverable state.

25 Jobs are stuck in Holding and the window is passing.Medium

The Flex Queue — more batch jobs than the concurrent limit. You can reorder the queue; you cannot increase the concurrency.

Consolidate jobs, stagger schedules, and treat a growing Holding count as the early warning that the nightly window no longer fits.

26 How do you know a batch job is slower than it used to be?Medium

Record duration yourself — write start and end times plus record counts to a job-log record each run, and report on the trend.

AsyncApexJob holds the completion data for a while but not a usable history. Without your own log, "it is getting slower" is an impression.

27 Your batch recalculates everything nightly and takes six hours.Medium

Process only what changed: filter on SystemModstamp since the last successful run (persisted), with a periodic full reconciliation.

Incremental plus occasional full is almost always the right shape, and it is what keeps the window viable as data grows.

28 A batch must skip records another process is editing.Hard

Use a status or lock field the other process sets, and filter on it — a database row lock will not help across separate transactions.

Handle the stale-lock case: if a process dies holding the flag, you need a timeout so records are not excluded forever.

29 The batch's finish() needs to start another batch.Easy

Legitimate and common: chain batches from finish() so stage two begins when stage one completes.

Guard against loops (a batch that re-enqueues itself unconditionally) and record which stage ran, so a failure midway through a chain is diagnosable.

30 You need to batch over Big Object data.Hard

Standard batch locators do not apply the same way. Use async SOQL to write results into another object, or query along the composite index in slices.

Which is why the Big Object's index design matters so much up front — it determines whether the data is processable at all. See Big Objects.

31 Scope 200 fails on CPU but scope 50 succeeds. Is that fine?Medium

It works, but it is a signal: per-record cost is high, so the job will become fragile as logic grows.

Reduce scope as a short-term fix, then look at what is expensive per record — usually a query or describe call that should have been done once per chunk.

32 A batch job silently stopped running weeks ago.Medium

Check CronTrigger — a deployment or an abort left the schedule broken, and nothing alerts on absence.

Add a heartbeat: the job writes a completion record, and a separate check alerts if none appeared today. Alerting on absence rather than on errors is what catches this class of failure.

Queueable & finalizers (33–50)

33 A Queueable disappeared with no log and no error.Hard

It hit an uncatchable limit. Attach a Finalizer: it runs after the job even on an unhandled exception, in a fresh context with its own small allocation, so it can log the failure.

Without one, limit breaches vanish. This is the single most valuable reliability addition to async Apex.

34 Your finalizer re-enqueues and loops forever.Hard

Carry an attempt counter in the job's constructor and cap it. Also halve the scope on each retry so a deterministic failure isolates itself rather than repeating identically.

Unbounded retry burns the daily async allocation and converts one failure into total async unavailability.

35 How much work should a finalizer do?Easy

Very little: log, alert, perhaps re-enqueue. Its limits are small and it runs in a failure context.

Business logic in a finalizer is logic that only executes when something went wrong — the worst possible place for it.

36 Chaining works in production, fails in tests.Medium

Tests permit one level of chaining. Assert the first link's work and that the next job was enqueued; test later links as separate units.

That constraint is a useful design pressure — each link should be independently testable anyway.

37 How deep can a Queueable chain go?Medium

Effectively unlimited in production (bounded by the daily async allocation), tightly limited in tests.

Depth is rarely the constraint; the real risks are an accidental infinite chain and consuming your async allocation. Carry a hop counter and cap it.

38 A Queueable needs a callout.Easy

Implement Database.AllowsCallouts. Set an explicit timeout on the request, and remember the cumulative 120-second callout budget per transaction.

Classify the response: 2xx success, 409 as already-processed, 429/5xx as retryable, 4xx as your bug and dead-letter it.

39 You enqueue 100 Queueables from one transaction and it fails.Medium

There is a cap on jobs added per transaction (and only one from within a Queueable). Enqueue one job that processes the collection, or chain.

Fan-out of that shape is usually better expressed as a batch job or as platform events.

40 Queueable state is lost between links.Medium

Each link is a new transaction and a new instance, so statics reset. State must be passed through the constructor (and it must be serialisable) or persisted to a record.

Keep constructor state small — large collections serialised between links are slow and can exceed limits.

41 You need a job Id to show progress to the user.Easy

System.enqueueJob() returns the AsyncApexJob Id — return it to the client, which polls AsyncApexJob for status.

That is also why Queueable beats @future: you cannot monitor what you cannot identify.

42 Async work runs twice and duplicates records.Hard

Make it idempotent: a unique External Id on created records, insert with allOrNone = false, and treat DUPLICATE_VALUE as "already done".

The unique index is the guarantee; a pre-check query is only an optimisation and does not hold under concurrency.

43 A static recursion guard does not work across async hops.Medium

Each hop is a new transaction, so statics are fresh. Guards cannot span transactions.

Use a durable marker on the record — a processed flag or timestamp — when the guarantee must persist.

44 You need to run async work as a specific user.Medium

Async runs as the enqueuing user (platform event subscribers run as Automated Process). You cannot choose arbitrarily.

If the context matters, pass the relevant user Id and do explicit permission checks, or trigger the work from a scheduled job owned by a dedicated service user.

45 Mixed DML error inside a Queueable.Medium

The same rule applies in async: setup objects (User, Group, PermissionSetAssignment) cannot be mixed with non-setup objects in one transaction.

Split across two Queueables, or chain them. The separate transaction is in fact the standard way to resolve mixed DML.

46 You need to process records in a guaranteed order.Hard

Async gives no ordering guarantee across jobs, and events are not reliably ordered. Enforce order explicitly with a sequenced chain, each link handling one ordered slice.

Or remove the ordering requirement by making the operations commutative and idempotent, which is usually the more robust design.

47 Queueable fails with "Too many queueable jobs added".Easy

You enqueued more than permitted from a single transaction, or more than one from inside a Queueable.

Enqueue a single job that iterates, or restructure as a chain where each link adds exactly one.

48 How do you dead-letter work that cannot succeed?Medium

A custom object holding the payload, the error, the attempt count and a correlation id — inspectable, reportable and replayable.

A log line is not a dead-letter queue. If nobody can see what failed and re-run it, the failure is permanent.

49 A Queueable needs to update records another transaction holds.Medium

It will fail on a lock. Retry with backoff via the finalizer, and reduce the window by sorting DML consistently and keeping transactions short.

Persistent contention usually means data skew rather than a concurrency bug — check whether one parent or one owner is involved.

50 Should every Queueable have a finalizer?Medium

Any Queueable whose failure matters, which is nearly all of them. The cost is a few lines; the benefit is knowing when it died.

Make it part of your async template so it is the default rather than something to remember.

Limits & scale (51–66)

51 "Make it async" did not fix the row limit.Medium

Async doubles CPU, heap and query count, but total query rows (50,000) and DML rows (10,000) are unchanged.

Only Batch Apex with a locator escapes the row limit, because it streams. Moving a 200,000-row query into a Queueable changes nothing.

52 All async work stopped mid-afternoon.Hard

The daily async execution allocation — org-wide and licence-scaled — is exhausted, most often by an uncapped retry loop.

Cap retries, back off, dead-letter, and monitor the allocation with an alert rather than discovering it when everything halts.

53 Users report errors when your integration runs.Hard

The concurrent long-running request limit: ten synchronous requests over five seconds per org, shared with interactive users.

Move integration work async or use Continuations. This limit's symptom appears nowhere near its cause, which is what makes it so hard to diagnose.

54 You cannot schedule a new job.Easy

Org-wide scheduled job limit reached — commonly from scheduling one job per region or entity.

Use one scheduled job that iterates, and audit CronTrigger for abandoned schedules from old projects.

55 Batch jobs are limited to a few concurrent. How do you go faster?Hard

Reduce total work (incremental processing), make each record cheaper, or partition into fewer, larger, better-tuned jobs rather than many small ones competing for the same concurrency.

More parallelism also means more lock contention, so measure — serial sometimes finishes sooner.

56 Heap exceeded in an async job.Medium

12MB is not much once you select 60 fields across several objects. Query fewer fields, avoid parallel copies of collections, null out large structures, and prefer a locator over a list.

Deserialising a large JSON payload is another frequent cause — parse once and discard.

57 How do you check limits mid-job and stop gracefully?Medium

Compare Limits.getCpuTime() and heap against Limits.getLimitX() inside the loop, and when you cross a threshold, record the high-water mark and re-enqueue the remainder.

The job then completes in two passes instead of failing at 95%.

58 Platform event allocation disappeared overnight.Medium

Both publishing and delivery per subscriber consume allocation, so several subscribers multiply consumption.

Consolidate subscribers, keep payloads thin, and review whether every subscriber needs every event. See allocations.

59 Email notifications from async jobs stop sending.Medium

Daily email limits are org-wide and fail quietly. A per-record notification from a batch will exhaust them.

Batch the notifications into a digest, or use platform events and custom notifications which do not share that cap.

60 A job needs to finish within a two-hour window and does not.Hard

Reduce the work (incremental, filtered), reduce per-record cost, then parallelise with partitioning — in that order, because parallelism adds contention.

Also check whether the window is competing with other scheduled jobs for batch concurrency; staggering can recover an hour with no code change.

61 An async job triggers automation that triggers more async work.Hard

A cascade that can consume your allocation and run for hours, invisible in any single debug log because each hop is its own transaction.

Guard with durable markers, a hop counter you persist, and a circuit breaker you can alert on. Then question whether the cascade is necessary.

62 How do you size scope for a batch that does DML?Medium

Work backwards from DML rows per chunk: 10,000 is the cap, so a job inserting five children per record cannot use scope 2,000.

Then check CPU and heap headroom at that scope and leave margin — a chunk running at 90% of CPU will fail when the data gets slightly heavier.

63 You need to know your org's async headroom.Medium

The Limits API (or /limits via REST) reports daily async executions, API calls, storage and more. Query it on a schedule and alert at a percentage.

These are the limits that fail silently rather than throwing — nobody notices until work stops happening.

64 A managed package's async jobs compete with yours.Medium

They share your org-wide batch concurrency, flex queue and daily allocation. Their nightly job can push yours into Holding.

Stagger your schedules around theirs, and raise it with the vendor if their job is uncontrollable — with evidence from the Apex Jobs history.

65 Is it better to run one big job or many small ones?Medium

Few, well-tuned jobs. Many small jobs compete for limited batch concurrency, multiply monitoring surface, and increase lock contention.

Partition only when a single job genuinely cannot fit the window, and partition by a key that avoids shared parents.

66 How do you design async work that scales with data growth?Hard

Make it incremental (process only what changed, with a persisted watermark), bounded (explicit limits you chose), resumable (high-water marks), and observable (duration and volume logged per run).

Those four properties mean the job degrades gracefully and visibly as data grows, rather than failing suddenly one Tuesday.

Monitoring & testing (67–82)

67 Nobody knew a job had been failing for a month.Medium

Query AsyncApexJob on a schedule for Failed/Aborted and alert. The Apex Jobs UI is for humans and nobody looks at it.

Also alert on absence — a job that normally completes daily and did not should page someone, because a broken schedule throws no error.

68 NumberOfErrors is 400 but you do not know why.Medium

It is only a count. Your own error log, written per chunk with the record Id, status code and message, is what makes it actionable.

Publish it as a platform event so the log survives a rollback within the chunk.

69 A job's debug log is empty.Medium

Trace flags are per user, and async work may run as a different user — platform event subscribers run as Automated Process.

Set the trace flag on the correct user, and build structured logging so you are not dependent on catching a trace at the right moment.

70 How do you test a finalizer?Hard

Call the execute(FinalizerContext) method directly with a test double for the context where possible, asserting the logging and re-enqueue decisions.

You cannot easily force an uncatchable limit breach in a test, so test the finalizer's logic as a unit rather than trying to reproduce the failure.

71 How do you test a scheduled job?Easy

System.schedule() inside the test and assert on CronTrigger, and separately call the execute method directly to test the logic.

Keeping the schedulable trivial makes this easy — a schedulable that only starts a batch needs almost no testing.

72 Your async test passes but production behaves differently.Medium

Tests run everything synchronously inside startTest/stopTest, with one chaining level and clean data. Production has concurrency, real volumes, other automation and partial failures.

Supplement unit tests with a scenario test in a Full sandbox at realistic volume — particularly for anything with lock or ordering sensitivity.

73 How do you test platform event subscribers?Medium

Publish the event, then Test.getEventBus().deliver() to force synchronous delivery, then assert the subscriber's effect.

Also test the retry path by making the handler throw, and assert your dead-letter behaviour — the failure path is where event-driven designs break.

74 You need to prove idempotency in a test.Medium

Run the operation twice in the test and assert the state after the second run is identical to after the first — same record count, same field values.

That single test prevents most duplicate-side-effect incidents from retries and redelivery.

75 A job is slow but you cannot tell which part.Medium

Instrument stages: capture Limits.getCpuTime() deltas per phase and log them with record counts.

Combined with the query timings in a debug log, that tells you whether the cost is query, computation or DML — which determines completely different fixes.

76 How do you alert when a job takes twice as long as usual?Medium

Write duration to a job-log record each run, then a scheduled check compares the latest against a rolling average and raises a notification.

That catches degradation before it becomes a missed window, which is far better than discovering it when the window fails.

77 Apex exception emails go to a developer who left.Easy

Set the Apex exception recipients to a monitored distribution list, and build real alerting off your error log rather than relying on email at all.

Single-person notification is a guaranteed future gap and one of the most common findings when an org changes hands.

78 You need a dashboard for async health.Medium

Build it from your own job-log object plus AsyncApexJob queries: jobs completed today, failures, average duration, records processed, oldest unprocessed item, allocation consumed.

"Oldest unprocessed item" is the single most useful number — a rising value is the earliest signal that something is wrong.

79 How do you replay failed work?Medium

Store enough in the dead-letter record to reconstruct the operation — payload, target Ids, parameters — and provide a button or a batch that re-submits selected rows.

Replay must be idempotent, because some of what you replay may have partially succeeded.

80 A deployment broke your schedules again.Medium

Make schedule setup an idempotent Apex method run as a documented post-deployment step, which aborts and recreates everything.

Then verify CronTrigger state after each release. Treating schedules as deployable configuration rather than manual setup is the fix.

81 How do you test behaviour under concurrency?Hard

Not in unit tests — they are single-threaded. Use a sandbox: run two Bulk API loads simultaneously against related data and observe lock errors and duplicates.

Then verify your idempotency and lock-ordering defences actually hold. Concurrency bugs are only reproducible under real concurrency.

82 Your async code has 90% coverage and still fails in production.Medium

Coverage measures executed lines, not scenarios. Async failures come from volume, concurrency, partial failure, retries and limit exhaustion — none of which a typical unit test exercises.

Add tests for: a chunk that fails, a repeated run (idempotency), and an empty input. Then scenario-test at volume in a sandbox.

Judgement (83–100)

83 A stakeholder wants instant processing; you know it must be async.Medium

Decompose "instant": must the user see it before finishing their task (then it is synchronous, within limits), or must it merely happen quickly (events, seconds)?

Then make the asynchrony visible in the UX — a pending state that resolves — rather than pretending it is instant.

84 Should business logic live in the async job or a service?Easy

A service the job calls. The job owns scheduling, chunking and error handling; the service owns the logic and is testable without async.

Logic embedded in execute() can only be tested through the async harness, which is slower and weaker.

85 You are asked to process everything in real time "to be safe".Medium

Real-time processing is more expensive, more fragile and harder to recover than batch. "To be safe" usually argues for the opposite.

Ask what decision depends on the freshness. If none does today, nightly batch plus the option to add events later is the cheaper and more reliable start.

86 How do you choose between events and a scheduled job?Medium

Events for freshness and decoupling, accepting at-least-once delivery, no ordering and a retention window. Scheduled jobs for completeness, ordering and easy reasoning.

Mature designs use both: events for immediacy, a scheduled reconciliation for correctness. See delivery semantics.

87 A job must not run during business hours.Easy

Schedule it outside them, and add a guard in the job that exits if it finds itself running inside them — schedules get changed by accident.

Also consider the user impact of the work itself: heavy DML during the day causes lock contention for interactive users.

88 How do you migrate a synchronous process to async safely?Medium

Extract the logic into a service first (testable synchronously), then add the async wrapper, then switch the caller behind a feature flag.

Run both paths in parallel in a sandbox and compare outputs before cutting over. The UX change — results arriving later — usually needs more attention than the code.

89 Users complain data "takes a while to appear".Medium

That is async working as designed, poorly communicated. Show a pending state, a last-updated timestamp, or a progress indicator driven by the job Id.

A visible "processing, updated at 14:32" eliminates most of these complaints without any change to the processing.

90 An async job writes to records users are editing.Medium

You will get lock errors and you may overwrite user edits. Decide the conflict rule: skip records modified recently, write only to fields users do not touch, or notify rather than overwrite.

Silently overwriting user input is the worst outcome and the easiest to ship accidentally.

91 How do you make async failures visible to the business?Medium

Surface them as data, not logs: a dead-letter object with a list view and a report, plus a count on an operational dashboard.

Business users will chase failures they can see. Nobody chases a log nobody can read.

92 A job processes 100 records a night and you built it as a batch.Easy

Over-engineered — a scheduled Queueable or even synchronous processing would do, with less monitoring surface.

Batch earns its complexity above the row and transaction limits. Below them it is extra machinery to operate for no benefit.

93 You need to coordinate async work across two orgs.Hard

No distributed transaction exists. Design for eventual consistency with idempotent operations, correlation ids, and compensating actions for partial failure.

Then add reconciliation comparing counts and checksums between the orgs — the only way to detect divergence. See consistency models.

94 Async work must respect the user's permissions.Medium

It runs as the enqueuing user, so sharing applies if your class declares it — but verify, because async entry points frequently end up in system mode.

Declare with sharing and use USER_MODE explicitly rather than relying on inherited context.

95 You inherit async code with no error handling.Medium

Add observability before changing behaviour: finalizers, structured logging, a job-log record per run. You cannot improve what you cannot see.

Then address the loudest failure mode. Rewriting first and instrumenting later means you will not know whether the rewrite helped.

96 How much retry is too much?Medium

Beyond about three attempts with backoff, a failure is usually deterministic and retrying just consumes allocation.

Cap it, dead-letter, and alert. Infinite retry is how a single malformed record takes down an org's async capacity.

97 A job is business-critical. What does that change?Hard

It needs: a finalizer, structured logging, a dead-letter path, alerting on failure and on absence, a documented runbook for re-running it, and a tested resume mechanism.

"Business-critical" should mean those six things exist — not simply that someone would be upset if it failed.

98 Async or middleware for heavy integration processing?Medium

Middleware, usually, for transformation and orchestration of large volumes — it has better memory, retry and observability than Apex, and it does not consume your org's limits.

Keep Apex for logic that genuinely needs Salesforce context. "We did it in Apex because that is where the data is" is how orgs hit limit walls at scale.

99 What is the most common async design mistake?Hard

Assuming it will succeed. Async work is retried, redelivered, interrupted and run concurrently — and most code is written as though it runs once, in order, successfully.

Idempotency plus observability converts that assumption from a hope into something you can verify.

100 One habit worth adopting for all async work?Hard

Write the failure path first: the finalizer, the log, the dead letter, the alert — before the happy path.

It takes an hour, it forces you to decide what "failed" means, and it is the difference between a job you operate and a job you hope about.
