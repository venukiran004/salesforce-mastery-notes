Field Service

Getting the right engineer, with the right skills and the right parts, to the right place at the right time — and letting them work offline when they get there.

The data model

From a Case to a completed visit
ObjectRepresents
Work OrderThe job to be done, usually created from a Case, Asset or Entitlement
Work Order Line ItemIndividual tasks or billable items within the job; can be scheduled separately
Service AppointmentThe unit that actually gets scheduled. Holds the arrival window, duration and status
Service ResourceA technician, contractor, crew or asset that can be assigned

Maintenance Plan / Maintenance Work RuleGenerates preventive-maintenance work orders on a schedule
🎯
The one distinction to get right A Work Order is what needs doing. A Service Appointment is a visit to do some of it. One work order can have several appointments — an initial diagnosis, a return visit with the part, a final sign-off. The scheduling engine works on Service Appointments, never Work Orders.

Scheduling & optimisation

ComponentWhat it controls
Scheduling PolicyThe named strategy — a bundle of work rules and objectives. Different policies for emergency vs routine work
Work RulesHard constraints — must be satisfied. Match Skills, Match Territory, Working Hours, Resource Availability, Match Fields
Service ObjectivesSoft preferences — weighted and optimised. Minimise Travel, Preferred Resource, ASAP, Minimise Overtime, Highest Priority First
Candidate → Book"Get Candidates" lists valid slots; "Book Appointment" or "Schedule" assigns one
OptimisationBatch re-shuffling of a whole territory/day to minimise total travel; runs on a schedule or on demand
Emergency dispatchInserts an urgent job, bumping lower-priority work
Complex WorkDependencies between appointments — "B must start after A finishes", or same resource, or same time

Dispatcher Console is the control room: a Gantt chart of resources against time, a map with travel routes, an appointment list with filters, and drag-and-drop rescheduling. Colour-coded by status so a dispatcher can see jams at a glance.

🧮
Rules vs objectives — the mental model Work Rules answer "is this assignment legal?" (right skills, right territory, resource actually working). Service Objectives answer "of the legal options, which is best?" (least travel, preferred engineer, soonest slot). Too many hard rules and nothing can be scheduled; too few and you'll send an unqualified engineer 200 km.

The mobile app

What technicians get

  • Full offline capability — view, edit and create records with no signal, syncing when back online
  • Today's schedule with navigation and travel
  • Knowledge articles attached to the work type
  • Barcode scanning for parts and assets
  • Photos, signature capture and Service Reports
  • Products Consumed recorded against van stock

What you configure

  • Mobile flows — guided procedures run on the device
  • Quick actions on the appointment
  • Offline priming: which related records get downloaded
  • Branding and layout per profile
  • Push notifications for new or changed jobs
  • Conflict handling when two people edited the same record offline
📴
Design for offline from day one Anything requiring a callout, a server-side Apex round trip or real-time validation will fail in a basement or a rural site. Keep on-device logic in mobile flows and simple field updates; do the heavy processing server-side after sync, and make it tolerant of records arriving hours later and out of order.

A day in the life

  • Case raised — the customer reports a fault; the agent confirms entitlement and SLA.
  • Work Order created from the Case, using a Work Type that sets duration and required skills.
  • Service Appointment generated with an arrival window derived from operating hours.
  • Scheduling — the policy filters candidates by skills, territory and availability, then optimises for least travel.
  • Dispatch — the dispatcher reviews the Gantt, adjusts, and releases the day's work.
  • Technician receives it on mobile, navigates, and works offline on site.
  • Completion — parts consumed recorded, photos and customer signature captured, Service Report generated.
  • Follow-up — the Work Order closes, the Case updates, van stock decrements and a replenishment Product Request is raised; billing picks up the billable lines.

Interview questions

1 Work Order vs Service Appointment — why are there two objects?Easy

A Work Order is the work that needs doing; a Service Appointment is a scheduled visit to do some of it. Separating them means one job can span multiple visits — diagnose today, return with the part on Thursday, sign off on Friday — each independently scheduled, possibly to different engineers.

Practical consequence: the scheduling engine, the dispatcher console and the mobile app all operate on Service Appointments. If you're trying to schedule a Work Order, you've misunderstood the model.

2 Work Rules vs Service Objectives.Medium

Work Rules are hard constraints — a candidate that fails one is excluded entirely (Match Skills, Match Territory, Working Hours, Resource Availability). Service Objectives are weighted preferences used to rank the remaining valid candidates (Minimise Travel, Preferred Resource, ASAP, Minimise Overtime).

Both live in a Scheduling Policy, and you'll typically have several policies — an emergency policy weighting speed, a routine policy weighting travel cost.

3 "No candidates found" when scheduling. How do you debug it?Hard

Work through the hard rules in order, because one of them is excluding everybody: ① Skills — does any resource hold the required skill at the required level, and hasn't it expired? ② Territory — is the resource a member of the appointment's territory for those dates? ③ Operating hours — does the window fall inside them, allowing for time zones? ④ Availability — absences, existing appointments, capacity. ⑤ Match Fields or custom rules quietly filtering everyone out. ⑥ The appointment's earliest start / due date window being too narrow.

Temporarily removing rules one at a time from a test policy isolates the culprit quickly.

4 How does offline mode change how you build?Hard

You cannot rely on the server. No callouts, no server-side Apex round trips, no real-time validation against data the device doesn't hold. Anything requiring them fails in a basement.

So: keep on-device logic in mobile flows and simple field updates; prime the right related records for offline access; do heavy processing server-side after sync; and — critically — make that server-side logic idempotent and order-tolerant, because records may arrive hours late, out of sequence, and occasionally in conflict with someone else's edit.

5 How do you handle a job that needs two engineers at once?Hard

Two options. A Crew — a Service Crew record with members, treated as a single schedulable resource, which is right when the pair always work together. Or Complex Work dependencies — multiple Service Appointments on the same Work Order with a "same start time / same resource / start after" rule between them, which is right when you need two different specialists simultaneously.

Crews are simpler to schedule; complex work is more flexible but harder to optimise.

6 How does preventive maintenance work?Medium

A Maintenance Plan is attached to an Asset (or Account/Location) with a frequency and a Work Type. Maintenance Work Rules define the generation pattern — calendar-based (every 6 months) or criteria-based (every 500 operating hours, driven by a field on the Asset).

The plan generates Work Orders ahead of time in a batch, which then flow into scheduling like any other job. The business value is shifting from reactive break-fix to planned visits, which is far cheaper and lets you batch nearby jobs.

7 Optimisation runs but travel time is still high. What do you check?Hard

Check the objective weightings — if "Preferred Resource" or "ASAP" outweighs "Minimise Travel", the engine is doing exactly what you told it. Then: are territories drawn sensibly, or do they overlap so engineers cross each other? Are appointment windows so narrow that no efficient route exists? Are travel-time calculations using real street routing and correct home base / start location for each resource? And is optimisation actually running over a wide enough horizon and resource pool to find better routes?

Quick check

1. Which object does the scheduling engine actually schedule?
A Work Order is the work; a Service Appointment is a visit. One Work Order can have several appointments, each scheduled independently.
2. "Match Skills" is what kind of scheduling component?
Work Rules exclude invalid candidates outright; Service Objectives rank the ones that remain.
3. Why must post-sync server logic be idempotent in Field Service?
A technician may work all day with no signal. Your logic must tolerate a batch of records arriving hours later, in an unexpected sequence.
